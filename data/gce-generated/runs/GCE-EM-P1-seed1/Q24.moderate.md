# PAPER 1, QUESTION 24 of 27, 3 MARKS

The time, $T$ seconds, taken by a pendulum to complete one swing is directly proportional to the square root of its length, $L$ cm.
A pendulum of length $64$ cm takes $1.6$ seconds to complete one swing.
(a) Find an expression for $T$ in terms of $L$. [2]
(b) Find the length of a pendulum that takes $2.5$ seconds to complete one swing. [1]

# SETTER'S ANSWER KEY
(a) $T = 0.2\sqrt{L}$ (or $T = \frac{\sqrt{L}}{5}$)
(b) $156.25$ cm (accept $156$ cm)

# SETTER'S WORKED SOLUTION
(a) Since $T$ is directly proportional to $\sqrt{L}$, $T = k\sqrt{L}$ for some constant $k$.
When $L = 64$, $T = 1.6$: $1.6 = k\sqrt{64} = 8k$, so $k = 0.2$.
Hence $T = 0.2\sqrt{L}$, that is $T = \frac{\sqrt{L}}{5}$.

(b) When $T = 2.5$: $2.5 = 0.2\sqrt{L}$, so $\sqrt{L} = 12.5$ and $L = 12.5^2 = 156.25$.
The pendulum is $156.25$ cm long (156 cm to 3 s.f.).
