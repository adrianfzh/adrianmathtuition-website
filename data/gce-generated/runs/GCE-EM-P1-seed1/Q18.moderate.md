# PAPER 1, QUESTION 18 of 27, 4 MARKS

The mass of one helium atom is $6.64 \times 10^{-27}$ kg.
A balloon contains $2.5 \times 10^{23}$ helium atoms.
(a) Calculate the mass of the helium in the balloon.
Give your answer in kilograms in standard form. [2]
(b) A second balloon contains $4.15 \times 10^{-3}$ kg of helium.
Calculate the total number of helium atoms in the two balloons.
Give your answer in standard form. [2]

# SETTER'S ANSWER KEY
(a) $1.66 \times 10^{-3}$ kg
(b) $8.75 \times 10^{23}$

# SETTER'S WORKED SOLUTION
(a) Mass $= (6.64 \times 10^{-27}) \times (2.5 \times 10^{23})$
$= (6.64 \times 2.5) \times 10^{-27 + 23}$
$= 16.6 \times 10^{-4}$
$= 1.66 \times 10^{-3}$ kg.

(b) Number of atoms in the second balloon $= \frac{4.15 \times 10^{-3}}{6.64 \times 10^{-27}}$
$= \frac{4.15}{6.64} \times 10^{-3 - (-27)}$
$= 0.625 \times 10^{24}$
$= 6.25 \times 10^{23}$.
Total number of atoms $= 2.5 \times 10^{23} + 6.25 \times 10^{23}$
$= 8.75 \times 10^{23}$.
