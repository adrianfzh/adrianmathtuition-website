# PAPER 1, QUESTION 6 of 27, 3 MARKS

The curve $y = x^2 + bx + c$ has line of symmetry $x = 1.5$.
One of the points where the curve crosses the $x$-axis is $(-2, 0)$.
(a) Write down the coordinates of the other point where the curve crosses the $x$-axis. [1]
(b) Find the value of $b$ and the value of $c$. [2]

# SETTER'S ANSWER KEY
(a) $(5, 0)$
(b) $b = -3$, $c = -10$

# SETTER'S WORKED SOLUTION
(a) The two $x$-intercepts are symmetrical about the line $x = 1.5$.
$-2$ is $3.5$ units to the left of $1.5$, so the other intercept is $3.5$ units to the right: $1.5 + 3.5 = 5$.
The other point is $(5, 0)$.

(b) The roots of $x^2 + bx + c = 0$ are $x = -2$ and $x = 5$, so
$y = (x + 2)(x - 5) = x^2 - 5x + 2x - 10 = x^2 - 3x - 10$.
Hence $b = -3$ and $c = -10$.
(Check: line of symmetry $x = -\frac{b}{2} = \frac{3}{2} = 1.5$.)
