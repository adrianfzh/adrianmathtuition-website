A bakery sells muffins in three flavours: chocolate, banana and lemon.
Each customer buys exactly one muffin.
The probability that a customer buys a chocolate muffin is $0.35$.
The probability that a customer buys a lemon muffin is $0.2$.
(a) Find the probability that a customer buys a banana muffin. [1]
(b) Two customers each buy a muffin. The flavour each customer buys is independent of the other.
Find the probability that the two customers buy muffins of different flavours. [2]

# SETTER'S ANSWER KEY
(a) $0.45$
(b) $0.635$ (or $\frac{127}{200}$)

# SETTER'S WORKED SOLUTION
(a) The three probabilities sum to 1.
P(banana) $= 1 - 0.35 - 0.2 = 0.45$

(b) The two customers are independent, so multiply along each pair of choices.
P(both chocolate) $= 0.35 \times 0.35 = 0.1225$
P(both banana) $= 0.45 \times 0.45 = 0.2025$
P(both lemon) $= 0.2 \times 0.2 = 0.04$
P(same flavour) $= 0.1225 + 0.2025 + 0.04 = 0.365$
P(different flavours) $= 1 - 0.365 = 0.635$

Alternative for (b): add the six ordered pairs of different flavours, $2(0.35 \times 0.45) + 2(0.35 \times 0.2) + 2(0.45 \times 0.2) = 0.315 + 0.14 + 0.18 = 0.635$.
