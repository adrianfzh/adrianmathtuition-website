# SETTER'S WORKED SOLUTION
(a)(i) Angle $ABC = 90^\circ$ (angle in a semicircle, since $AC$ is a diameter).
Angle $ACB = 180^\circ - 90^\circ - 32^\circ = 58^\circ$ (angle sum of triangle $ABC$).

(a)(ii) Angle $OCT = 90^\circ$ (tangent perpendicular to radius $OC$ at the point of contact $C$), so angle $ACT = 90^\circ$ since $AOC$ is a straight line.
Angle $BCT = 90^\circ - 58^\circ = 32^\circ$.

(a)(iii) Angle $CAD$ = angle $CBD = 37^\circ$ (angles in the same segment, on chord $CD$).

(b) If $AD$ were parallel to $BC$, then angle $CAD$ would equal angle $ACB$ (alternate angles). But angle $CAD = 37^\circ$ and angle $ACB = 58^\circ$, and $37^\circ \ne 58^\circ$, so $AD$ is not parallel to $BC$.

(c)(i) In triangles $ABC$ and $CBT$:
angle $ABC = 90^\circ$ (angle in a semicircle) and angle $CBT = 180^\circ - 90^\circ = 90^\circ$ (angles on the straight line $ABT$), so angle $ABC$ = angle $CBT$;
angle $BAC = 32^\circ$ (given) and angle $BCT = 32^\circ$ (from (a)(ii)), so angle $BAC$ = angle $BCT$.
Two pairs of corresponding angles are equal, so triangle $ABC$ is similar to triangle $CBT$ (AA).

(c)(ii) $AC = 2 \times 6 = 12$ cm.
In right-angled triangle $ABC$: $BC = 12 \sin 32^\circ = 6.359\ldots$ cm and $AB = 12 \cos 32^\circ = 10.176\ldots$ cm.
From the similar triangles, $\frac{BT}{BC} = \frac{CB}{AB}$, so $BT = \frac{BC^2}{AB} = \frac{(12 \sin 32^\circ)^2}{12 \cos 32^\circ} = 3.973\ldots$
(Equivalently, in right-angled triangle $CBT$, $BT = BC \tan 32^\circ = 6.359\ldots \times \tan 32^\circ = 3.973\ldots$.)
$BT = 3.97$ cm (3 s.f.).

Consistency check: with $O$ at the origin and radius 6, $A(-6, 0)$, $C(6, 0)$, $B$ at $64^\circ$ from $OC$ (angle at centre $= 2 \times 32^\circ$), $D$ at $-74^\circ$ (angle at centre $= 2 \times 37^\circ$); line $AB$ has gradient $\tan 32^\circ$ and meets the tangent $x = 6$ at $T(6, 12\tan 32^\circ) = (6, 7.50)$, above $B$, so $ABT$ is a straight line with $B$ between $A$ and $T$. Every given angle is realised.
