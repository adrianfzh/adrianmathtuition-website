Mr Tan is deciding whether to install a rainwater tank to water the plants in his garden.
He finds the following information.

Rainwater tank
The tank is a cylinder with internal diameter $80$ cm and internal height $120$ cm.
Price, including installation: $\$420$
Expected lifespan: $10$ years

Collecting rainwater
Rain falling on the roof of the garden shed drains into the tank.
The roof is a rectangle $3.6$ m by $2.5$ m.
$80\%$ of the rain that falls on the roof reaches the tank.
Average rainfall: $175$ mm per month

Watering the garden
Mr Tan waters his garden with a hose for $12$ minutes every day.
The hose delivers $9$ litres of water per minute.

Monthly water tariff
$\begin{array}{|l|c|} \hline \text{First } 40 \text{ m}^3 & \$2.74 \text{ per m}^3 \\ \hline \text{Each m}^3 \text{ above } 40 \text{ m}^3 & \$3.69 \text{ per m}^3 \\ \hline \end{array}$

$1 \text{ m}^3 = 1000$ litres
(a) Calculate the capacity of the tank.
Give your answer in litres. [2]
(b) In a month with $30$ days, calculate [3]
  (i) the volume of water, in litres, Mr Tan uses to water his garden, [1]
  (ii) the volume of rainwater, in litres, that reaches the tank. [2]
(c) Before installing the tank, Mr Tan's household uses $43 \text{ m}^3$ of tap water each month.
Assume that all the rainwater that reaches the tank is used to water the garden instead of tap water.
Is it worth Mr Tan installing the rainwater tank?
Justify your answer with calculations. [5]

# SETTER'S WORKED SOLUTION
(a) Radius $= 40$ cm. Capacity $= \pi \times 40^2 \times 120 = 192\,000\pi = 603\,185.8 \text{ cm}^3$. Since $1000 \text{ cm}^3 = 1$ litre, capacity $= 603.19 = 603$ litres (3 s.f.). [M1 for $\pi r^2 h$ with $r = 40$, A1]

(b)(i) Water used per day $= 9 \times 12 = 108$ litres. In $30$ days: $108 \times 30 = 3240$ litres. [B1]

(b)(ii) Roof area $= 3.6 \times 2.5 = 9 \text{ m}^2$. Rainfall $175$ mm $= 0.175$ m, so volume of rain on the roof in a month $= 9 \times 0.175 = 1.575 \text{ m}^3 = 1575$ litres. Volume reaching the tank $= 80\% \times 1575 = 1260$ litres. [M1 for $9 \times 0.175$ (or $1575$), A1]

(c) Rainwater available $= 1260$ litres per month, which is less than the $3240$ litres used, so all $1.26 \text{ m}^3$ replaces tap water each month. [M1 for $1.26 \text{ m}^3$ replacing tap water]
Tap water used falls from $43 \text{ m}^3$ to $43 - 1.26 = 41.74 \text{ m}^3$, still above $40 \text{ m}^3$, so the water saved is all charged at the higher rate of $\$3.69$ per m$^3$. [M1 for recognising the saving is at the $\$3.69$ tier]
Monthly saving $= 1.26 \times 3.69 = \$4.6494 = \$4.65$. [A1]
Saving over the $10$-year lifespan $= 4.6494 \times 12 \times 10 = \$557.93$ (or $4.65 \times 120 = \$558.00$). [M1]
$\$557.93 > \$420$, so the tank saves more than it costs: net saving $\approx \$138$ over $10$ years, and it pays for itself after $420 \div 4.6494 = 90.3$, i.e. $91$ months (about $7.5$ years), within its lifespan. Conclusion: YES, it is worth installing. [A1 for a conclusion consistent with correct figures]
Alternative acceptable conclusion: NO — the net saving of about $\$138$ over $10$ years (about $\$14$ a year) is small, and rainfall varies so the tank may overflow in wet months and run dry in dry months; award the final A1 for this if the figures are correct and the reason is valid.
Note: using $\$2.74$ per m$^3$ gives $1.26 \times 2.74 = \$3.45$ per month and $\$414.29$ over $10$ years, which is less than $\$420$; this uses the wrong tier, so award M1 M0 A0 M1 A0 (max 2).
