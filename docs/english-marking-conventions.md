# How schools mark English Paper 2 — the conventions

Adrian, 14 Oct 2026: *"yes"* — learn the schools' English marking habits from their answer
schemes and make the English Practise checker follow them.

**What was read.** Every English row of the language bank that carries a scheme: 821 rows from 59
papers (2021–2026 prelims, a few mid-years and practice papers; Paper 2 comprehension, visual text,
vocabulary and summary; Paper 1 editing). Four plan-billed readers (Sonnet) took about 15 papers
each and wrote down how the scheme marks; the main session (Opus) merged them and checked the
headline counts with a plain word search over all 59 papers.

**Grounding only.** This file holds the RULES, in our words. No question, answer, passage or scheme
wording is quoted, and none reaches a student or a prompt (`docs/CONTENT-POLICY.md`).

**How to read the counts.** "papers" = of 59. A word-search count (exact) is marked *ws*; the rest
are the readers' counts, added across the four batches (≈). Many schemes show a habit without
saying it, so a count is a floor, not the full spread.

## Short answers

| # | The rule | Seen in | In our checker |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | **Two marks = two separate ideas, one mark each** (cause + result, point + example, quote + explanation). Half the answer = 1 of 2. | ≈140 of the 258 two-mark rows, ≈50 papers | had it ("half marks only when … two separable ideas") — now says one mark per idea |
| S2 | **"In your own words": the key words must be changed.** A whole lifted sentence = 0. Keeping the key word (or its root) while changing the rest does not earn that idea. | own-words / recast wording: 59 rows, 30 papers *ws*; "lift" notes: 31 rows, 16 papers *ws* | had the whole-lift rule — **added** the kept-key-word and same-root parts |
| S3 | **Explaining a word with the same word (or its root) earns nothing.** | 6 rows, 6 papers *ws*; readers ≈10 | **added** |
| S4 | **Lifting is right when the question asks to find, quote or give evidence.** | ≈12 rows, ≈6 papers | **added** (so S2 is never used against an evidence question) |
| S5 | **"Excess denied": a right idea plus a wrong or contradicting idea loses that mark.** True but unneeded detail costs nothing. Several items where ONE is asked, with a wrong one among them, also lose it. | 34 rows, 18 papers *ws* | **added** |
| S6 | **Quote a word or phrase: the boundaries matter both ways.** A whole sentence when a phrase is asked is wrong; a quote that drops the word carrying the idea is wrong. | ≈80 rows, ≈40 papers (readers) | had "extra words that blur it" — **added** the "drops the key word" half |
| S7 | **Language / effect questions: a quote with no explanation earns nothing, and naming the device alone (simile, metaphor, short sentence) earns nothing.** The mark is for what the words suggest, tied to the question. A generic effect ("to make it interesting") earns nothing. | quote-without-explanation notes: ≈15 rows, ≈10 papers; device-alone notes in every batch | **added** |
| S8 | **Feeling, tone and attitude words must be the right strength and the right kind.** Too strong, too mild, a vague label (positive, negative, sad), or a behaviour instead of a feeling is rejected. A same-strength synonym is accepted. | "too strong / too mild / intensity": 26 rows, 16 papers *ws*; "vague / too general": 37 rows, 19 papers *ws*; reject lists: 252 rows, 37 papers *ws* | **added** (we had only "vague = 0") |
| S9 | **When the scheme's idea has two halves, both are needed** — a feeling AND its reason, both sides of a comparison or contrast. | ≈15 rows, ≈10 papers | **added** |
| S10 | **The same idea counts once** — two quotes carrying one idea, or the same point repeated in a later part. | ≈6 rows, ≈5 papers | **added** |
| S11 | **"Any two of three" (a points list longer than the marks): one mark per different point, up to the marks.** | "any one / two / three": 53 rows, 25 papers *ws* | **added** |
| S12 | **An inference must go past the text.** Restating the passage where the question asks what it suggests or why is not the inference. | ≈20 rows (readers; two papers tag every question literal / inference) | had "vague = 0"; **added** |
| S13 | **Visual text: a picture question needs what the picture shows AND how it backs the message.** Only one half = 0. | ≈15 rows, ≈14 papers | **added** |
| S14 | **Spelling: a small slip is fine when the word is still clear;** a slip that makes a different word is wrong. Quotation marks and capitals are not needed. | spelling / minor-slip notes: 12 rows, 9 papers *ws* | had "do not correct spelling unless it changes the meaning" — **kept**, quotation-mark half added |
| S15 | Answer the question asked: an answer about the wrong person, moment or paragraph earns nothing. | ≈15 rows, ≈10 papers | had it ("an answer to a different question") |

## The summary

| # | The rule | Seen in | In our checker |
|---|---|---|---|
| U1 | 15 marks: **content 8 + language 7**. One mark a content point, up to 8. | content-8 / style-7 printed: 12–15 papers *ws*; 35 summaries in all | had it (content out of 8; no language mark is invented) |
| U2 | Only the named paragraphs count; everything after the 80th word is struck out. | the question wording on every summary; struck-out rule printed in 1 paper | had it |
| U3 | **A point's key word must be there.** A different word that changes the idea does not make the point. | every points table ("passage → own words", with rejected rewordings) | **added** |
| U4 | **Two points merged into one vague general line make neither.** | 1 paper says it in words; the tables show it | had it ("merged so loosely that the idea is lost") |
| U5 | **The same point said twice counts once.** | ≈3 papers (colour-grouped tables) | **added** |
| U6 | **Lifting costs language marks, not content.** A lifted line that carries a point still makes the point; the lifting shows in the language comment. | the 8 + 7 split itself; band notes in 1 paper | **added** (we only said "own words or close to them") |

The language band, where printed (1 paper): 7 = own words and structures throughout, ideas well
linked · 5–6 = mostly · 3–4 = some · 1–2 = occasional · 0 = none. Our checker gives no language
mark (`SPEC-ENGLISH-PRACTICE.md` §What it is); its one wording line follows this band.

## Editing (Paper 1)

10 marks, one a line; two lines are clean (a tick); the corrected word is what is marked, with a
second accepted word where two fit; a plausible wrong correction is wrong. Our editing is marked by
rule (`checkEditing`) and already follows this.

## Seen, not adopted

- **"Mark only the first answer"** — 2 rows in 2 papers. Too rare to be a convention; S5 covers the
  usual case.
- **Paragraph-matching and flowchart items** (pick a phrase from a box; copy it exactly, a
  misspelling = 0) — our sets have no flowchart questions.
- **A full sentence needed** — no scheme requires it; most accept a phrase.

## Before and after (14 Oct 2026, all on the plan)

Same readers (Sonnet, as the page's plan reader), same sheets, only the rules changed. Sample: 9 sets
(na01, na06, na11, nn01, nn06, nn11, vt01, vt06, vt11) — 666 rows. Hard set: the 57 student-style
answers, each read twice. Rule check: 21 new answers aimed at these habits
(`scripts/english-bench/conventions-answers.json`).

| | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Sample — seeded | 388/389 | 388/389 |
| Sample — repeats · padding · swapped · summary | 99/99 · 76/76 · 72/72 · 30/30 | 99/99 · 76/76 · 72/72 · 30/30 |
| Hard set — seeded | 55/57 | 54/57 and 56/57 (two reads) |
| Hard set — same mark twice | 56/57 | 55/57 and 56/57 |
| Rule check — on the mark | 20/21 | 20/21 |

- **Nothing got worse beyond the noise of one read.** The hard set's misses are the same few
  half-right answers before and after (na01 Q7, nn01 Q6, and na01 Q11 read 1 where its seed says 0),
  and they move half a mark either way between reads.
- **A first wording did slip** (hard set 55 and 54 the same twice): "two halves are needed" was read
  as taking away a mark that stands alone. One sentence fixed it ("This never takes away a mark the
  scheme gives for an idea on its own"); the after column is that wording.
- **The checker already knew most of these habits.** The rule check scored 20/21 with the OLD rules
  too. What the new rules buy is that the standard is written down and the same on every read; the
  one rule-check miss (an own-words answer that keeps the key words of a quote printed in the
  question) is arguable.
- Results: `scripts/english-bench/results/conv-*-2026-10-14.json` (before, after = the first wording,
  after2 = the shipped wording).
