Likely Q1 · Algebra (Inequalities), Numbers (Prime Factorization) · 2 marks
Why it might be Advanced: Prime k(k+196): factor, one factor must equal 1 (hardest part (b))
(a) The number line in the diagram shows all the possible values of $y$.
Write down an inequality in $y$ that describes the number line. [1]
Answer: $y \le -3.75$
(b) $k$ is a positive integer and $k^2 + 196k$ is a prime number.
State the value of $k$. [1]
Answer: $k = 1$
Worked solution
(a)
The circle at $-3.75$ is filled in, so $-3.75$ itself is included.
The shading goes to the left, so $y$ is $-3.75$ or smaller.
**Answer:** $y \le -3.75$

(b)
Factorise: $k^2 + 196k = k(k + 196)$
A prime number has only two factors, $1$ and itself.
$k$ and $k + 196$ are both positive integers, and $k + 196 > k$.
So the smaller factor must be $1$: $k = 1$
Check: $1 \times 197 = 197$, and $197$ is prime (not divisible by $2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13$).
**Answer:** $k = 1$

Maybe Q2 · Algebra (Inequalities) · 5 marks
Why it might be Advanced: Extreme of x/y over ranges with negative y; check corners (hardest part (b)(i))
(a) Find the range of values of $t$ for which $2t+3 \le 7t-2 < 4t+10$, and illustrate the solution on a number line. [3]
Answer: $1 \le t < 4$
(b) The numbers $x$, $y$ and $z$ satisfy $-8 \le x \le 6$, $-3 \le y \le -1$ and $-2 \le z \le 5$. Find the [2]
(i) smallest value of $\dfrac{x}{y}$, [1]
Answer: $-6$
(ii) largest value of $yz$. [1]
Answer: $6$
Worked solution
(a) Split the chain into two inequalities and solve each one.
Left part: $2t+3 \le 7t-2$
$3+2 \le 7t-2t$
$5 \le 5t$
$t \ge 1$
Right part: $7t-2 < 4t+10$
$7t-4t < 10+2$
$3t < 12$
$t < 4$
Both must hold together.
**Answer:** $1 \le t < 4$
Number line: a shaded (closed) dot at $1$, an open (hollow) dot at $4$, and the line between them shaded. Closed dot because $t = 1$ is allowed; open dot because $t = 4$ is not.

(b)(i) $y$ is always negative, so $\dfrac{x}{y}$ has the opposite sign to $x$.
To make $\dfrac{x}{y}$ as negative as possible, take $x$ as large and positive as possible and $y$ as close to $0$ as possible.
Check the corners: $\dfrac{6}{-1} = -6$, $\dfrac{6}{-3} = -2$, $\dfrac{-8}{-1} = 8$, $\dfrac{-8}{-3} = \dfrac{8}{3}$.
**Answer:** smallest value of $\dfrac{x}{y}$ is $-6$

(b)(ii) $y$ is negative, so $yz$ is largest when $z$ is negative too (negative × negative = positive).
Check the corners: $(-3)(-2) = 6$, $(-1)(-2) = 2$, $(-3)(5) = -15$, $(-1)(5) = -5$.
**Answer:** largest value of $yz$ is $6$

Maybe Q3 · Algebra (Inequalities) · 3 marks
Why it might be Advanced: Greatest of m/n with integer ranges including negatives and zero (hardest part (b))
Two integers $m$ and $n$ satisfy $-7.4 < m \leq 3$ and $-\frac{9}{2} \leq n < -\frac{2}{3}$.
Write down
(a) the greatest possible value of $mn$, [1]
Answer: $28$
(b) the greatest possible value of $\frac{m}{n}$, [1]
Answer: $7$
(c) the least possible value of $m^2 - n^2$. [1]
Answer: $-16$
Worked solution
List the integers first.
$-7.4 < m \leq 3$ gives $m = -7, -6, -5, \ldots, 2, 3$.
$-\frac{9}{2} \leq n < -\frac{2}{3}$ means $-4.5 \leq n < -0.666\ldots$, so $n = -4, -3, -2, -1$.
Note that $n$ is always negative.

(a) For $mn$ to be greatest, it must be positive, so $m$ must be negative too.
Negative $\times$ negative is largest when both are furthest from zero.
Take $m = -7$ and $n = -4$.
$mn = (-7)(-4) = 28$
Check the other end: $m = 3$, $n = -1$ gives $-3$, which is smaller.
**Answer:** $28$

(b) For $\frac{m}{n}$ to be greatest, it must be positive, so $m$ is negative.
Make the top as far from zero as possible and the bottom as close to zero as possible.
Take $m = -7$ and $n = -1$.
$\frac{m}{n} = \frac{-7}{-1} = 7$
**Answer:** $7$

(c) For $m^2 - n^2$ to be least, make $m^2$ as small as possible and $n^2$ as large as possible.
A square is never negative, and $m = 0$ is allowed, so the least $m^2$ is $0^2 = 0$.
The largest $n^2$ comes from the value of $n$ furthest from zero: $(-4)^2 = 16$.
$m^2 - n^2 = 0 - 16 = -16$
**Answer:** $-16$

Maybe Q4 · Algebra (Linear Equations) · 4 marks
Why it might be Advanced: Fraction equation with two unknowns combined with linear equation (hardest part whole)
Find the values of $x$ and $y$ that satisfy both of the equations

$$\dfrac{4}{2x - y} = \dfrac{3}{x + 2y},$$
$$2x + 3y = 7.$$
Worked solution
$\dfrac{4}{2x - y} = \dfrac{3}{x + 2y} \quad \text{---[1]}$
$2x + 3y = 7 \quad \text{---[2]}$
From [1], cross-multiply: $4(x + 2y) = 3(2x - y)$
$4x + 8y = 6x - 3y$
$11y = 2x$
$x = \dfrac{11y}{2} \quad \text{---[3]}$
Sub [3] into [2]: $2\left(\dfrac{11y}{2}\right) + 3y = 7$
$11y + 3y = 7$
$14y = 7$
$y = \dfrac{1}{2}$
Sub $y = \dfrac{1}{2}$ into [3]: $x = \dfrac{11}{2} \times \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{11}{4}$
Check in [1]: $2x - y = 5$ and $x + 2y = \dfrac{15}{4}$, so $\dfrac{4}{5} = \dfrac{3}{15/4} = \dfrac{12}{15} = \dfrac{4}{5}$ ✓
**Answer:** $x = \dfrac{11}{4}, \ y = \dfrac{1}{2}$

Maybe Q5 · Number Patterns, Algebra (Factorization) · 0 marks
Why it might be Advanced: Extend number pattern to a third case, then apply to 98x104+9 (hardest part (iii))
Kai Wen notices that multiplying two whole numbers which differ by $2$ and then adding $1$ always seems to give a perfect square. He writes the first four lines of his pattern in the table below.
$$\begin{array}{|c|c|} \hline \text{Line} & \text{Pattern} \\ \hline 1 & 1 \times 3 + 1 = 2^2 \\ \hline 2 & 2 \times 4 + 1 = 3^2 \\ \hline 3 & 3 \times 5 + 1 = 4^2 \\ \hline 4 & 4 \times 6 + 1 = 5^2 \\ \hline \end{array}$$
(i) Complete the table below.
$$\begin{array}{|c|c|} \hline \text{Line} & \text{Pattern} \\ \hline 1 & 1 \times 3 + 1 = 2^2 \\ \hline 2 & 2 \times 4 + 1 = 3^2 \\ \hline 3 & 3 \times 5 + 1 = 4^2 \\ \hline 4 & 4 \times 6 + 1 = 5^2 \\ \hline 5 & \\ \hline 6 & \\ \hline \vdots & \vdots \\ \hline n & \\ \hline \end{array}$$ [0]
Answer: Line 5: $5 \times 7 + 1 = 6^2$; Line 6: $6 \times 8 + 1 = 7^2$; Line $n$: $n(n+2) + 1 = (n+1)^2$
(ii) He then tries numbers which differ by $4$, and adds $4$ instead. The first four lines of this second pattern are shown below.
$$\begin{array}{|c|c|} \hline \text{Line} & \text{Pattern} \\ \hline 1 & 1 \times 5 + 4 = 3^2 \\ \hline 2 & 2 \times 6 + 4 = 4^2 \\ \hline 3 & 3 \times 7 + 4 = 5^2 \\ \hline 4 & 4 \times 8 + 4 = 6^2 \\ \hline \end{array}$$
Complete the table below.
$$\begin{array}{|c|c|} \hline \text{Line} & \text{Pattern} \\ \hline 1 & 1 \times 5 + 4 = 3^2 \\ \hline 2 & 2 \times 6 + 4 = 4^2 \\ \hline 3 & 3 \times 7 + 4 = 5^2 \\ \hline 4 & 4 \times 8 + 4 = 6^2 \\ \hline \vdots & \vdots \\ \hline n & \\ \hline \end{array}$$ [0]
Answer: Line $n$: $n(n+4) + 4 = (n+2)^2$
(iii) By considering your answers in parts (i) and (ii), find the value of $98 \times 104 + 9$ without the use of a calculator. [0]
Answer: $10201$
Worked solution
(i)
In Line $k$, the first number is $k$, the second is $k+2$, and the square is $(k+1)^2$.
Line 5: $5 \times 7 + 1 = 36 = 6^2$
Line 6: $6 \times 8 + 1 = 49 = 7^2$
Line $n$: $n(n+2) + 1 = (n+1)^2$
Check: $n(n+2) + 1 = n^2 + 2n + 1 = (n+1)^2$ ✓
**Answer:** $5 \times 7 + 1 = 6^2$, $6 \times 8 + 1 = 7^2$, $n(n+2) + 1 = (n+1)^2$

(ii)
In Line $k$, the first number is $k$, the second is $k+4$, and the square is $(k+2)^2$.
Line $n$: $n(n+4) + 4 = (n+2)^2$
Check: $n(n+4) + 4 = n^2 + 4n + 4 = (n+2)^2$ ✓
**Answer:** $n(n+4) + 4 = (n+2)^2$

(iii)
Compare the two results:
$n(n+2) + 1^2 = (n+1)^2$
$n(n+4) + 2^2 = (n+2)^2$
So the next pattern is $n(n+6) + 3^2 = (n+3)^2$.
Check: $n(n+6) + 9 = n^2 + 6n + 9 = (n+3)^2$ ✓
$98 \times 104$ is $n(n+6)$ with $n = 98$.
$98 \times 104 + 9 = (98 + 3)^2$
$= 101^2$
$= 10201$
**Answer:** $10201$

Maybe Q6 · Statistics · 5 marks
Why it might be Advanced: Median and mode conditions on unknown frequency; boundary and tie traps (hardest part (b))
A farmer keeps a flock of hens. For one week, she counted the number of eggs laid by each hen. Her results are shown in the table.
$$\begin{array}{|l|c|c|c|c|c|} \hline \text{Number of eggs laid} & 3 & 4 & 5 & 6 & 7 \\ \hline \text{Number of hens} & 2 & 5 & y & 9 & 4 \\ \hline \end{array}$$
(a) Given that $y = 12$, calculate the mean number of eggs laid by a hen. [2]
Answer: Mean $= 5.25$ eggs
(b) Given instead that the median number of eggs laid is $5$, find the least possible value of $y$. [2]
Answer: Least possible value of $y = 7$
(c) Given instead that the mode is $6$ eggs, write down the largest possible value of $y$. [1]
Answer: Largest possible value of $y = 8$
Worked solution
(a)
Number of hens $= 2 + 5 + 12 + 9 + 4 = 32$
Total eggs $= 3(2) + 4(5) + 5(12) + 6(9) + 7(4)$
$= 6 + 20 + 60 + 54 + 28$
$= 168$
Mean $= \dfrac{168}{32}$
$= 5.25$
**Answer:** mean $= 5.25$ eggs
(b)
Hens with fewer than 5 eggs $= 2 + 5 = 7$
Hens with more than 5 eggs $= 9 + 4 = 13$
Number of hens $= 20 + y$
For the median to be 5, the middle hen(s) must all fall in the "5 eggs" group.
Try $y = 6$: $26$ hens, middle hens are the 13th and 14th.
13th hen laid 5 eggs, 14th hen laid 6 eggs, so median $= 5.5$ (not 5).
Try $y = 7$: $27$ hens, middle hen is the 14th.
Hens up to the "5 eggs" group $= 7 + 7 = 14$, so the 14th hen laid 5 eggs.
Median $= 5$ ✓ (and any $y < 6$ gives a median of 6)
**Answer:** least possible value of $y = 7$
(c)
The mode is 6, so 6 eggs must have the highest frequency, 9.
$y$ must be less than 9 (if $y = 9$ there are two modes).
**Answer:** largest possible value of $y = 8$
