Core Q1 · Binomial Theorem · 4 marks
Why Core: two pairs make the x to the 9 term, and they cancel (hardest part (ii))
(i) Find the first three terms in the expansion of $\left(x^2 - \frac{2}{x}\right)^6$ in descending powers of $x$, simplifying each term. [2]
Answer: $x^{12} - 12x^9 + 60x^6$
(ii) Hence explain why the expansion of $(x^3 + 5)\left(x^2 - \frac{2}{x}\right)^6$ has no term in $x^9$. [2]
Answer: Coefficient of $x^9$ $= 1(60) + 5(-12) = 0$, so there is no $x^9$ term.
Worked solution
(i)
$\left(x^2 - \frac{2}{x}\right)^6 = (x^2)^6 + \binom{6}{1}(x^2)^5\left(-\frac{2}{x}\right) + \binom{6}{2}(x^2)^4\left(-\frac{2}{x}\right)^2 + \dots$
$= x^{12} + 6(x^{10})\left(-\frac{2}{x}\right) + 15(x^8)\left(\frac{4}{x^2}\right) + \dots$
$= x^{12} - 12x^9 + 60x^6 + \dots$
**Answer:** $x^{12} - 12x^9 + 60x^6$

(ii)
The powers in the expansion drop by 3 each time: $x^{12}, x^9, x^6, x^3, \dots$
An $x^9$ term in the product comes from two pairs only:
$x^3 \times 60x^6 = 60x^9$
$5 \times (-12x^9) = -60x^9$
Every later term is $x^3$ or lower, so it cannot make $x^9$.
Coefficient of $x^9 = 60 - 60 = 0$
**Answer:** the coefficient of $x^9$ is $0$, so there is no term in $x^9$.

Core Q2 · Differentiation (Maximum and Minimum) · 5 marks
Why Core: gradient negative on both sides means a point of inflexion (hardest part (b))
The side view of a slide at a water park is modelled by the curve
$$y = 7 - 3x + \frac{3}{2}x^2 - \frac{1}{4}x^3, \quad 0 \le x \le 4,$$
where $x$ metres is the horizontal distance from the top of the slide and $y$ metres is the height of the slide above the ground.
There is exactly one point on the slide at which the slide is level.
(a) Use differentiation to find the coordinates of this point. [3]
Answer: $(2, 5)$
(b) By working out the gradient of the slide a little before and a little after this point, decide whether the point is a maximum point, a minimum point or a stationary point of inflexion. [2]
Answer: Stationary point of inflexion (the gradient is negative on both sides of $x = 2$)
Worked solution
**(a)**
The slide is level where the gradient is zero.
$y = 7 - 3x + \frac{3}{2}x^2 - \frac{1}{4}x^3$
$\frac{dy}{dx} = -3 + 3x - \frac{3}{4}x^2$
Set $\frac{dy}{dx} = 0$:
$-3 + 3x - \frac{3}{4}x^2 = 0$
Multiply through by $-\frac{4}{3}$:
$x^2 - 4x + 4 = 0$
$(x - 2)^2 = 0$
$x = 2$
Substitute $x = 2$ into the equation of the curve:
$y = 7 - 3(2) + \frac{3}{2}(2)^2 - \frac{1}{4}(2)^3$
$y = 7 - 6 + 6 - 2$
$y = 5$
**Answer:** $(2, 5)$

**(b)**
From (a), $\frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{3}{4}(x - 2)^2$.
Take a value of $x$ just below $2$ and a value just above $2$.
At $x = 1.5$: $\frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{3}{4}(-0.5)^2 = -0.1875$, which is negative.
At $x = 2$: $\frac{dy}{dx} = 0$.
At $x = 2.5$: $\frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{3}{4}(0.5)^2 = -0.1875$, which is negative.
In increasing $x$ the gradient goes negative, zero, negative.
The gradient does not change sign, so the slide goes down, levels off, then goes down again.
**Answer:** $(2, 5)$ is a stationary point of inflexion.

Why Core: quotient rule with chain rule on top and bottom, then tidy up (hardest part whole)
Given that $y = \frac{\sin^3 2x}{(1 - 4x)^2}$, find $\frac{dy}{dx}$. Simplify your answer and write it as one fraction.
Worked solution
Quotient rule with $u = \sin^3 2x$ and $v = (1 - 4x)^2$.

$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{v\frac{du}{dx} - u\frac{dv}{dx}}{v^2}$
$= \frac{(1 - 4x)^2 \cdot 6\sin^2 2x\cos 2x - \sin^3 2x \cdot \left(-8(1 - 4x)\right)}{(1 - 4x)^4}$
$= \frac{6(1 - 4x)^2\sin^2 2x\cos 2x + 8(1 - 4x)\sin^3 2x}{(1 - 4x)^4}$
Take out the common factor $2(1 - 4x)\sin^2 2x$:
$= \frac{2(1 - 4x)\sin^2 2x\left[3(1 - 4x)\cos 2x + 4\sin 2x\right]}{(1 - 4x)^4}$
Cancel one factor of $(1 - 4x)$:
**Answer:** $\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{2\sin^2 2x\left[3(1 - 4x)\cos 2x + 4\sin 2x\right]}{(1 - 4x)^3}$

Core Q4 · Differentiation (Techniques) · 4 marks
Why Core: dy/dt equals dy/dx times dx/dt (hardest part (b))
A curve has equation $y = \ln(4 + 3\sin 2x)$.
(a) Differentiate $y$ with respect to $x$. [2]
Answer: $\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{6\cos 2x}{4 + 3\sin 2x}$
(b) A particle $Q$ travels along the curve. At the instant when $x = \frac{\pi}{6}$, the $y$-coordinate of $Q$ is increasing at $0.3$ units per second. Find the rate at which the $x$-coordinate of $Q$ is changing at this instant. [2]
Answer: $0.660$ units/s
Worked solution
(a)
$y = \ln(4 + 3\sin 2x)$
Differentiate the bracket: $\frac{d}{dx}(4 + 3\sin 2x) = 6\cos 2x$
$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{1}{4 + 3\sin 2x} \times 6\cos 2x$
**Answer:** $\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{6\cos 2x}{4 + 3\sin 2x}$

(b)
Given: $\frac{dy}{dt} = 0.3$ (increasing, so positive). Wanted: $\frac{dx}{dt}$.
When $x = \frac{\pi}{6}$: $\cos\frac{\pi}{3} = \frac{1}{2}$ and $\sin\frac{\pi}{3} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$
$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{6 \times \frac{1}{2}}{4 + \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}}$
$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{6}{8 + 3\sqrt{3}} = 0.45468$
$\frac{dy}{dt} = \frac{dy}{dx} \times \frac{dx}{dt}$
$0.3 = 0.45468 \times \frac{dx}{dt}$
$\frac{dx}{dt} = \frac{0.3}{0.45468} = 0.65981$
**Answer:** the $x$-coordinate is increasing at $0.660$ units/s (3 s.f.)

Core Q5 · Indices · 5 marks
Why Core: multiply by u, factorise, reject the negative value of e to a power (hardest part whole)
The real number $x$ satisfies $2e^{3x} - \dfrac{15}{e^{3x}} = 7$.
By letting $u = e^{3x}$, form a quadratic equation in $u$ and hence find the exact value of $x$, explaining why there is only one such value.
Worked solution
$2e^{3x} - \dfrac{15}{e^{3x}} = 7$
Let $u = e^{3x}$:
$2u - \dfrac{15}{u} = 7$
Multiply every term by $u$:
$2u^2 - 15 = 7u$
$2u^2 - 7u - 15 = 0$
$(2u + 3)(u - 5) = 0$
$u = -\dfrac{3}{2}$ or $u = 5$
Since $e^{3x} > 0$ for all real $x$, $u = -\dfrac{3}{2}$ is rejected, so there is only one value of $x$.
$e^{3x} = 5$
$3x = \ln 5$
$x = \dfrac{1}{3}\ln 5$
**Answer:** $x = \dfrac{1}{3}\ln 5$

Core Q6 · Nature of Roots · 4 marks
Why Core: at least one common point means discriminant at least zero (hardest part whole)
A curve has equation $y = 2x^2 + kx + 7$, where $k$ is a constant. A straight line has equation $y + 5x = -1$.

Given that the line and the curve have at least one point in common, find the set of possible values of $k$.
Worked solution
Line: $y = -5x - 1$ ___(1)
Curve: $y = 2x^2 + kx + 7$ ___(2)

Sub (1) into (2):
$2x^2 + kx + 7 = -5x - 1$
$2x^2 + (k + 5)x + 8 = 0$

At least one common point $\Rightarrow$ the equation has real roots $\Rightarrow$ $b^2 - 4ac \geq 0$:
$(k + 5)^2 - 4(2)(8) \geq 0$
$k^2 + 10k + 25 - 64 \geq 0$
$k^2 + 10k - 39 \geq 0$
$(k + 13)(k - 3) \geq 0$

The graph of $(k + 13)(k - 3)$ is a U-shape cutting the $k$-axis at $-13$ and $3$, so it is $\geq 0$ outside the roots.

**Answer:** $k \leq -13$ or $k \geq 3$

Core Q7 · Nature of Roots · 7 marks
Why Core: two distinct points means discriminant greater than zero (hardest part (c))
The equation of a curve is $y = 2x^2 + (p + 5)x + p + 11$, where $p$ is a real constant.
(a) Find, in terms of $p$, the discriminant of $2x^2 + (p + 5)x + p + 11$, simplifying your answer. [2]
Answer: $p^2 + 2p - 63$
(b) Express your answer to part (a) in the form $(p + a)^2 - b$, where $a$ and $b$ are integers. [2]
Answer: $(p + 1)^2 - 64$
(c) Hence find the range of values of $p$ for which the curve cuts the $x$-axis at two distinct points. [3]
Answer: $p < -9$ or $p > 7$
Worked solution
(a)
Here $a = 2$, $b = p + 5$, $c = p + 11$.
Discriminant $= b^2 - 4ac$
$= (p + 5)^2 - 4(2)(p + 11)$
$= p^2 + 10p + 25 - 8p - 88$
$= p^2 + 2p - 63$
**Answer:** $p^2 + 2p - 63$

(b)
Complete the square on the $p$ terms: half of $2$ is $1$.
$p^2 + 2p - 63 = (p + 1)^2 - 1^2 - 63$
$= (p + 1)^2 - 64$
So $a = 1$, $b = 64$.
**Answer:** $(p + 1)^2 - 64$

(c)
Cuts the $x$-axis at two distinct points means two distinct real roots, so discriminant $> 0$.
$(p + 1)^2 - 64 > 0$
$(p + 1)^2 > 64$
$p + 1 > 8$ or $p + 1 < -8$
$p > 7$ or $p < -9$
Check: $(p + 9)(p - 7) = p^2 + 2p - 63$, critical values $-9$ and $7$; a positive $p^2$ coefficient means the expression is positive outside the roots.
**Answer:** $p < -9$ or $p > 7$

Core Q8 · Polynomials · 5 marks
Why Core: use f(2) = 2 f(-1) and solve for k (hardest part (b))
(a) Find the remainder when $2x^{3} + 5x^{2} - 3x - 4$ is divided by $x^{2} - 3$. [2]
Answer: $3x + 11$
(b) It is given that $f(x) = x^{3} + kx^{2} + 2$, where $k$ is a constant. The remainder when $f(x)$ is divided by $x - 2$ is twice the remainder when $f(x)$ is divided by $x + 1$. Find the value of $k$. [3]
Answer: $k = -4$
Worked solution
(a) Divide by the quadratic — the remainder is at most linear.
First term of the quotient: $2x^{3} \div x^{2} = 2x$
$2x(x^{2} - 3) = 2x^{3} - 6x$
Subtract: $(2x^{3} + 5x^{2} - 3x - 4) - (2x^{3} - 6x) = 5x^{2} + 3x - 4$
Next term of the quotient: $5x^{2} \div x^{2} = 5$
$5(x^{2} - 3) = 5x^{2} - 15$
Subtract: $(5x^{2} + 3x - 4) - (5x^{2} - 15) = 3x + 11$
So $2x^{3} + 5x^{2} - 3x - 4 = (x^{2} - 3)(2x + 5) + 3x + 11$
Check: $(x^{2} - 3)(2x + 5) = 2x^{3} + 5x^{2} - 6x - 15$, and adding $3x + 11$ gives back $2x^{3} + 5x^{2} - 3x - 4$ ✓
**Answer: remainder $= 3x + 11$**

(b) Remainder theorem: the remainder when divided by $(x - a)$ is $f(a)$.
Divided by $x - 2$: $f(2) = 2^{3} + k(2)^{2} + 2 = 10 + 4k$
Divided by $x + 1$: $f(-1) = (-1)^{3} + k(-1)^{2} + 2 = 1 + k$
First remainder is twice the second: $10 + 4k = 2(1 + k)$
$10 + 4k = 2 + 2k$
$2k = -8$
$k = -4$
Check: $f(2) = 8 - 16 + 2 = -6$ and $f(-1) = -1 - 4 + 2 = -3$, and $-6 = 2(-3)$ ✓
**Answer: $k = -4$**

Core Q10 · Simultaneous Equations · 3 marks
Why Core: substitute the straight line into the curve, reject the negative width (hardest part whole)
A rectangular card has width $w$ cm and length $l$ cm. The dimensions of the card satisfy $4l = w^2$ and $l - 2w = 5$. Find the area of the card.
Worked solution
$4l = w^2 \quad \cdots (1)$
$l - 2w = 5 \quad \cdots (2)$
From $(2)$: $l = 2w + 5$
Substitute into $(1)$: $4(2w + 5) = w^2$
$8w + 20 = w^2$
$w^2 - 8w - 20 = 0$
$(w - 10)(w + 2) = 0$

Substitute $w = 10$ into $(2)$: $l = 2(10) + 5 = 25$
Check in $(1)$: $4(25) = 100 = 10^2$
Area $= 10 \times 25 = 250$
**Answer:** $250\text{ cm}^2$

Core Q12 · Trigonometry (Graphs) · 4 marks
Why Core: minus sign flips the cosine, so the curve starts at its lowest point (hardest part (b))
A curve has equation $y = 5 - 4\cos 3x$.
(a) State the amplitude of the curve and its period in degrees. [2]
Answer: Amplitude $= 4$, period $= 120^\circ$
(b) Sketch the curve for $0^\circ \leq x \leq 180^\circ$, labelling the coordinates of its maximum and minimum points. [2]
Answer: Cosine curve reflected about its centre line $y = 5$, one and a half cycles: starts at the minimum point $(0^\circ, 1)$, rises to the maximum point $(60^\circ, 9)$, falls to the minimum point $(120^\circ, 1)$ and ends at the maximum point $(180^\circ, 9)$. It crosses $y = 5$ at $x = 30^\circ, 90^\circ, 150^\circ$ and lies wholly above the $x$-axis.
Worked solution
(a)
Compare $y = 5 - 4\cos 3x$ with $y = a\cos bx + c$: $a = -4$, $b = 3$, $c = 5$.
Amplitude $= |a| = |-4| = 4$ (the amplitude is never negative).
Period $= \frac{360^\circ}{b} = \frac{360^\circ}{3} = 120^\circ$.
**Answer:** amplitude $= 4$, period $= 120^\circ$

(b)
Centre line: $y = 5$.
Maximum value $= 5 + 4 = 9$.
Minimum value $= 5 - 4 = 1$.
Number of cycles in the domain $= \frac{180^\circ}{120^\circ} = 1\frac{1}{2}$.
At $x = 0^\circ$: $y = 5 - 4\cos 0^\circ = 5 - 4 = 1$, so the curve starts at its minimum (the negative sign flips the cosine curve).
Each quarter of a cycle is $30^\circ$, so the key points are:
$x = 0^\circ$: $y = 1$ (minimum)
$x = 30^\circ$: $y = 5$
$x = 60^\circ$: $y = 9$ (maximum)
$x = 90^\circ$: $y = 5$
$x = 120^\circ$: $y = 1$ (minimum)
$x = 150^\circ$: $y = 5$
$x = 180^\circ$: $y = 5 - 4\cos 540^\circ = 5 + 4 = 9$ (maximum)
Join the points with a smooth curve and stop exactly at $x = 180^\circ$.
The minimum value is $1$, so the curve never meets the $x$-axis.
**Answer:** a smooth curve from $(0^\circ, 1)$ up to $(60^\circ, 9)$, down to $(120^\circ, 1)$ and up to $(180^\circ, 9)$; maximum points $(60^\circ, 9)$ and $(180^\circ, 9)$, minimum points $(0^\circ, 1)$ and $(120^\circ, 1)$.

Exam Q13 · Binomial Theorem · 9 marks
Why Exam: collect the two x terms, set to zero, cancel powers of 3 (hardest part (b)(i))
It is given that $n$ is a positive integer.
(a) Find the first three terms in the expansion of $\left(3 + \frac{x}{3}\right)^n$ in ascending powers of $x$, giving each coefficient in terms of $n$ as a multiple of a power of $3$. [4]
Answer: $3^n + n\cdot 3^{n-2}x + \frac{n(n-1)}{2}\cdot 3^{n-4}x^2$
(b) When $(3 - 2x)\left(3 + \frac{x}{3}\right)^n$ is expanded in ascending powers of $x$, there is no term in $x$. [5]
Answer: (i) $n = 6$ (shown); (ii) constant term $2187$, coefficient of $x^2$ is $-567$
Worked solution
(a)
$\left(3 + \frac{x}{3}\right)^n = 3^n + \binom{n}{1}3^{n-1}\left(\frac{x}{3}\right) + \binom{n}{2}3^{n-2}\left(\frac{x}{3}\right)^2 + \dots$
First term: $3^n$
Second term: $n\cdot 3^{n-1}\cdot\frac{x}{3} = n\cdot 3^{n-2}x$
Third term: $\frac{n(n-1)}{2}\cdot 3^{n-2}\cdot\frac{x^2}{9} = \frac{n(n-1)}{2}\cdot 3^{n-4}x^2$
**Answer:** $3^n + n\cdot 3^{n-2}x + \frac{n(n-1)}{2}\cdot 3^{n-4}x^2 + \dots$

(b)(i)
$(3 - 2x)\left(3^n + n\cdot 3^{n-2}x + \frac{n(n-1)}{2}\cdot 3^{n-4}x^2 + \dots\right)$
Terms in $x$ come from two pairings: $3 \times n\cdot 3^{n-2}x$ and $-2x \times 3^n$.
Coefficient of $x$: $3\cdot n\cdot 3^{n-2} - 2\cdot 3^n = 0$
$n\cdot 3^{n-1} = 2\cdot 3^n$
Divide both sides by $3^{n-1}$: $n = 2\cdot 3 = 6$
**Answer:** $n = 6$ (shown)

(b)(ii)
With $n = 6$: $\left(3 + \frac{x}{3}\right)^6 = 729 + 6\cdot 81x + 15\cdot 9x^2 + \dots = 729 + 486x + 135x^2 + \dots$
Constant term: $3 \times 729 = 2187$
Terms in $x^2$ come from $3 \times 135x^2$ and $-2x \times 486x$.
Coefficient of $x^2$: $3(135) - 2(486) = 405 - 972 = -567$
**Answer:** constant term $= 2187$, coefficient of $x^2 = -567$

Exam Q14 · Circles · 10 marks
Why Exam: centre is where the perpendicular bisector meets the given line (hardest part (a))
$PQ$ is a chord of a circle $S$, where $P$ is the point $(1, 6)$ and $Q$ is the point $(7, -2)$. The line $y = 2x - 11$ passes through the centre of $S$.
(a) By first finding the equation of the perpendicular bisector of $PQ$, determine the coordinates of the centre of $S$ and the exact value of its radius. [9]
Answer: Centre $= (8, 5)$, radius $= 5\sqrt{2}$
(b) The circle $S$ is reflected in the line $x = 3$ to give the circle $T$. State the equation of $T$. [1]
Answer: $(x + 2)^2 + (y - 5)^2 = 50$
Worked solution
(a) The centre of a circle lies on the perpendicular bisector of any chord.
Midpoint of $PQ = \left(\frac{1 + 7}{2}, \frac{6 + (-2)}{2}\right) = (4, 2)$
Gradient of $PQ = \frac{-2 - 6}{7 - 1} = -\frac{4}{3}$
Gradient of perpendicular bisector $= \frac{3}{4}$ (since $m_1 m_2 = -1$)
Equation of perpendicular bisector: $y - 2 = \frac{3}{4}(x - 4)$
$y = \frac{3}{4}x - 1$
The centre also lies on $y = 2x - 11$, so solve the two equations together.
$2x - 11 = \frac{3}{4}x - 1$
$\frac{5}{4}x = 10$
$x = 8$
$y = 2(8) - 11 = 5$
Centre $= (8, 5)$
Radius $=$ distance from the centre to $P$
$= \sqrt{(8 - 1)^2 + (5 - 6)^2}$
$= \sqrt{49 + 1}$
$= \sqrt{50}$
$= 5\sqrt{2}$
