Likely Q1 · Algebra (Factorization), Indices · 6 marks
Why it might be Advanced: Telescoping powers of 4 mapped onto factorised identity, unsignposted pattern (hardest part (b))
(a) [3]
(i) By factorisation, show that $(a + 2)^{4} - (a + 1)(a + 2)^{3} - (a + 1)(a + 2)^{2} - (a + 1)(a + 2) = a + 2$. [2]
Answer: Shown: $(a+2)^4 - (a+1)(a+2)^3 = (a+2)^3[(a+2)-(a+1)] = (a+2)^3$; $(a+2)^3 - (a+1)(a+2)^2 = (a+2)^2$; $(a+2)^2 - (a+1)(a+2) = (a+2)[(a+2)-(a+1)] = a+2$.
(ii) Given that $(a + 2)^{n} - (a + 1)(a + 2)^{5} - (a + 1)(a + 2)^{4} - (a + 1)(a + 2)^{3} - (a + 1)(a + 2)^{2} - (a + 1)(a + 2) = a + 2$, state the value of $n$. [1]
Answer: $n = 6$
(b) Hence, find the exact value of $$4^{60} - 3(4^{59}) - 3(4^{58}) - 3(4^{57}) - \ldots - 3(4^{21}),$$ giving your answer as a power of $2$. [3]
Answer: $2^{42}$
Worked solution
(a)(i)
Work from the left, two terms at a time. Each pair has the common factor $(a+2)$ to a power.
$(a+2)^4 - (a+1)(a+2)^3 = (a+2)^3[(a+2) - (a+1)]$
$= (a+2)^3(1)$
$= (a+2)^3$
So the left side becomes $(a+2)^3 - (a+1)(a+2)^2 - (a+1)(a+2)$.
$(a+2)^3 - (a+1)(a+2)^2 = (a+2)^2[(a+2) - (a+1)]$
$= (a+2)^2$
So the left side becomes $(a+2)^2 - (a+1)(a+2)$.
$(a+2)^2 - (a+1)(a+2) = (a+2)[(a+2) - (a+1)]$
$= a + 2$ (shown)

(a)(ii)
Each subtraction of $(a+1)(a+2)^r$ lowers the power of $(a+2)$ by one.
The largest power subtracted is $(a+2)^5$, so the first term must be one power higher.
**Answer:** $n = 6$

(b)
Compare with part (a): take $a = 2$, so $a + 2 = 4$ and $a + 1 = 3$.
$4^{60} - 3(4^{59}) = 4^{59}[4 - 3] = 4^{59}$
$4^{59} - 3(4^{58}) = 4^{58}$
Each step drops the power of $4$ by one.
The last term subtracted is $3(4^{21})$, so the chain stops at $4^{22} - 3(4^{21}) = 4^{21}$.
Expression $= 4^{21}$
$= (2^2)^{21}$
$= 2^{42}$
**Answer:** $2^{42}$

Likely Q2 · Algebra (Fractions), Algebra (Quadratic Equations), Algebra (Identities) · 12 marks
Why it might be Advanced: Square w+2/w, then choose negative root for negative w (hardest part (c))
(a) [4]
(i) Write $\dfrac{\frac{3}{m} + \frac{2}{n}}{\frac{1}{m} - \frac{1}{n}}$ as a single fraction in its simplest form. [2]
Answer: $\dfrac{2m + 3n}{n - m}$
(ii) The value of $\dfrac{\frac{3}{m} + \frac{2}{n}}{\frac{1}{m} - \frac{1}{n}}$ is $4$. Hence, find the ratio $m : n$. [2]
Answer: $m : n = 1 : 6$
(b) [4]
(i) By factorising, solve the equation $6x^2 + 7x - 20 = 0$. [2]
Answer: $x = \dfrac{4}{3}$ or $x = -\dfrac{5}{2}$
(ii) Use your answers to part (i) to solve the equation $6(2t + 1)^2 + 7(2t + 1) - 20 = 0$. [2]
Answer: $t = \dfrac{1}{6}$ or $t = -\dfrac{7}{4}$
(c) A negative number $w$ satisfies $w^2 + \dfrac{4}{w^2} = 45$. [4]
(i) Show that $\left(w - \dfrac{2}{w}\right)^2 = 41$. [2]
Answer: Shown: $\left(w - \frac{2}{w}\right)^2 = w^2 - 4 + \frac{4}{w^2} = 45 - 4 = 41$.
(ii) Find the value of $w + \dfrac{2}{w}$. [2]
Answer: $w + \dfrac{2}{w} = -7$
Worked solution
(a)(i) Multiply the top and the bottom by $mn$ to clear the small fractions.

$\dfrac{\frac{3}{m} + \frac{2}{n}}{\frac{1}{m} - \frac{1}{n}} = \dfrac{\left(\frac{3}{m} + \frac{2}{n}\right) \times mn}{\left(\frac{1}{m} - \frac{1}{n}\right) \times mn}$

$= \dfrac{3n + 2m}{n - m}$

**Answer:** $\dfrac{2m + 3n}{n - m}$

(a)(ii) From part (i): $\dfrac{2m + 3n}{n - m} = 4$

$2m + 3n = 4(n - m)$

$2m + 3n = 4n - 4m$

$6m = n$

$\dfrac{m}{n} = \dfrac{1}{6}$

**Answer:** $m : n = 1 : 6$

(b)(i) $6x^2 + 7x - 20 = 0$

$(3x - 4)(2x + 5) = 0$

$3x - 4 = 0$ or $2x + 5 = 0$

**Answer:** $x = \dfrac{4}{3}$ or $x = -\dfrac{5}{2}$

(b)(ii) The equation is the one in part (i) with $x$ replaced by $2t + 1$.

So $2t + 1 = \dfrac{4}{3}$ or $2t + 1 = -\dfrac{5}{2}$

$2t = \dfrac{1}{3}$ or $2t = -\dfrac{7}{2}$

**Answer:** $t = \dfrac{1}{6}$ or $t = -\dfrac{7}{4}$

(c)(i) $\left(w - \dfrac{2}{w}\right)^2 = w^2 - 2(w)\left(\dfrac{2}{w}\right) + \dfrac{4}{w^2}$

$= w^2 + \dfrac{4}{w^2} - 4$

$= 45 - 4$

$= 41$ (Shown)

(c)(ii) $\left(w + \dfrac{2}{w}\right)^2 = w^2 + 2(w)\left(\dfrac{2}{w}\right) + \dfrac{4}{w^2}$

$= w^2 + \dfrac{4}{w^2} + 4$

$= 45 + 4$

$= 49$

$w + \dfrac{2}{w} = \pm 7$

Since $w < 0$, $\dfrac{2}{w} < 0$ too, so $w + \dfrac{2}{w}$ is negative.

**Answer:** $w + \dfrac{2}{w} = -7$

Likely Q3 · Algebra (Quadratic Graphs), Algebra (Subject of Formula) · 4 marks
Why it might be Advanced: v appears twice: complete the square first to make it the subject (hardest part (ii))
(i) Write $v^{2} - 14v + 60$ as $(v - h)^{2} + k$, stating the value of each of the constants $h$ and $k$. [1]
Answer: $(v - 7)^{2} + 11$, so $h = 7$ and $k = 11$
(ii) The quantities $w$, $v$ and $m$ are connected by the formula $w = v^{2} - 14v + m$.
Rearrange the formula to express $v$ in terms of $w$ and $m$. [3]
Answer: $v = 7 \pm \sqrt{w - m + 49}$
Worked solution
(i)
Halve the coefficient of $v$: $-14 \div 2 = -7$.
$v^{2} - 14v + 60 = (v - 7)^{2} - 7^{2} + 60$
$= (v - 7)^{2} - 49 + 60$
$= (v - 7)^{2} + 11$
**Answer:** $(v - 7)^{2} + 11$, so $h = 7$ and $k = 11$

(ii)
$v$ appears twice, so complete the square first to make it appear once.
$w = v^{2} - 14v + m$
$w = (v - 7)^{2} - 49 + m$
Move everything except the bracket to the other side.
$(v - 7)^{2} = w - m + 49$
Take the square root of the whole of each side, and keep the $\pm$.
$v - 7 = \pm \sqrt{w - m + 49}$
$v = 7 \pm \sqrt{w - m + 49}$
**Answer:** $v = 7 \pm \sqrt{w - m + 49}$

Likely Q4 · Algebra (Simultaneous Equations), Algebra (Expressions) · 4 marks
Why it might be Advanced: Cyclic ratios with k≠2: add equations, factor (k−2) to get sum zero (hardest part (a))
The non-zero real numbers $p$, $q$ and $r$ are such that $\frac{p+q}{r} = \frac{q+r}{p} = \frac{r+p}{q} = k$, where $k$ is a constant and $k \neq 2$.
(a) Show that $p + q + r = 0$. [2]
Answer: Shown.
(b) It is also given that $\frac{5}{q+r} + \frac{p-q-r}{p} = \frac{1}{3}$. Find the value of $p$. [2]
Answer: $p = 3$
Worked solution
(a)
Write each fraction as an equation in $k$.
$p + q = kr$
$q + r = kp$
$r + p = kq$
Add the three equations.
$2p + 2q + 2r = kp + kq + kr$
$2(p + q + r) = k(p + q + r)$
Bring everything to one side and factorise.
$(k - 2)(p + q + r) = 0$
Since $k \neq 2$, $k - 2 \neq 0$.
So $p + q + r = 0$.
**Answer:** Shown.

(b)
From (a), $q + r = -p$.
Replace $q + r$ in both fractions.
$\frac{5}{-p} + \frac{p - (-p)}{p} = \frac{1}{3}$
$-\frac{5}{p} + \frac{2p}{p} = \frac{1}{3}$
$-\frac{5}{p} + 2 = \frac{1}{3}$
$\frac{5}{p} = 2 - \frac{1}{3}$
$\frac{5}{p} = \frac{5}{3}$
$p = 3$
**Answer:** $p = 3$

Likely Q5 · Algebra (Simultaneous Equations), Numbers (Ratio) · 3 marks
Why it might be Advanced: Find unloaded cartons by integer search of 4x+9y=17 before ratio (hardest part whole)
A delivery van is loaded with cartons of two sizes only. Each small carton has a mass of $4$ kg and each large carton has a mass of $9$ kg. At the start of the day, the ratio of the number of small cartons to the number of large cartons is $3:2$. At the first stop, cartons with a total mass of $17$ kg are unloaded. The ratio of the number of small cartons to the number of large cartons left in the van is then $10:7$. Find the number of small cartons in the van at the start of the day.
Worked solution
First find which cartons were unloaded.
Let $x$ small cartons and $y$ large cartons be unloaded.
$4x + 9y = 17$
$y = 0$ gives $4x = 17$, not a whole number.
$y = 1$ gives $4x = 8$, so $x = 2$.
$y = 2$ gives $18 > 17$, not possible.
So $2$ small cartons and $1$ large carton were unloaded.

Let the numbers at the start be $3k$ small and $2k$ large.
After unloading: $3k - 2$ small and $2k - 1$ large.
$\frac{3k - 2}{2k - 1} = \frac{10}{7}$
$7(3k - 2) = 10(2k - 1)$
$21k - 14 = 20k - 10$
$k = 4$
Number of small cartons at the start $= 3 \times 4 = 12$

Check: $12$ small and $8$ large become $10$ small and $7$ large, which is $10:7$.

**Answer:** $12$ small cartons

Likely Q7 · Indices · 3 marks
Why it might be Advanced: Combine 2^(4n) and 81^n into 6^(4n); unusual common-index move (hardest part whole)
It is given that $2^{4n} \times 81^{n} = \dfrac{1}{216}$.

Find the value of $n$.
Worked solution
Write $81$ as a power of $3$.
$81^{n} = (3^{4})^{n} = 3^{4n}$

Both powers are now $4n$, so combine the bases.
$2^{4n} \times 3^{4n} = (2 \times 3)^{4n} = 6^{4n}$

$216 = 6^{3}$, so $\dfrac{1}{216} = 6^{-3}$

Same base on both sides, so equate the powers.
$6^{4n} = 6^{-3}$
$4n = -3$
$n = -\dfrac{3}{4}$

**Answer:** $n = -\dfrac{3}{4}$

Likely Q8 · Indices, Algebra (Simultaneous Equations) · 2 marks
Why it might be Advanced: Treat 3^n as the unknown, then reject 3^n=0 in a quadratic-like step (hardest part whole)
A carpenter charges $\$C$ for a square tabletop of side $d$ metres, where $C = kd^{n} + 15$ and $k$ and $n$ are constants.
A tabletop of side $3$ m costs $\$33$ and a tabletop of side $9$ m costs $\$501$.
Find the value of $k$ and the value of $n$.
Worked solution
When $d = 3$, $C = 33$:
$33 = k(3^{n}) + 15$
$k(3^{n}) = 18$
$k = \dfrac{18}{3^{n}}$ ---(1)
When $d = 9$, $C = 501$:
$501 = k(9^{n}) + 15$
$k(9^{n}) = 486$
Since $9^{n} = (3^{2})^{n} = (3^{n})^{2}$,
$k = \dfrac{486}{(3^{n})^{2}}$ ---(2)
Equate (1) and (2):
$\dfrac{18}{3^{n}} = \dfrac{486}{(3^{n})^{2}}$
$18(3^{n})^{2} = 486(3^{n})$
$18(3^{n})^{2} - 486(3^{n}) = 0$
$18(3^{n})(3^{n} - 27) = 0$
$3^{n} = 0$ (rejected, since $3^{n} > 0$) or $3^{n} = 27$
$3^{n} = 3^{3}$
$n = 3$
Substitute into (1):
$k = \dfrac{18}{27} = \dfrac{2}{3}$
Check: $\dfrac{2}{3}(9^{3}) + 15 = 486 + 15 = 501$.
**Answer:** $k = \dfrac{2}{3}$, $n = 3$

Likely Q9 · Mensuration · 9 marks
Why it might be Advanced: Flipped solid: sand fills dome first, then a similar-cone cavity above (hardest part (c))
A sealed hollow glass ornament is formed by joining a cone to a hemisphere along their circular rims. The rim has radius 7 cm and the slant edge of the cone is 25 cm long. The ornament is partly filled with fine coloured sand. The thickness of the glass may be ignored.
(a) Find the total area of glass on the outside of the ornament. [2]
Answer: $273\pi \approx 858$ cm$^2$
(b) In Diagram 1 the ornament sits in a stand with the tip of the cone pointing straight down. The level surface of the sand is 21 cm vertically above the tip. Find the volume of sand inside the ornament. [3]
Answer: $825$ cm$^3$
(c) The ornament is then turned over so that the dome is at the bottom and the tip of the cone points straight up, as in Diagram 2. The sand settles with a level surface. Find the depth of the sand, measured vertically from the lowest point of the dome. [4]
Answer: $7.71$ cm
Worked solution
(a) The outside is the curved surface of the cone and the curved surface of the hemisphere. The rim is a join, not a face.
Curved surface of cone $= \pi r l = \pi(7)(25) = 175\pi$
Curved surface of hemisphere $= 2\pi r^2 = 2\pi(7)^2 = 98\pi$
Total area $= 175\pi + 98\pi = 273\pi$
**Answer:** $273\pi \approx 858$ cm$^2$

(b) The volume needs the vertical height of the cone, not the slant edge.
Height of cone $= \sqrt{25^2 - 7^2} = \sqrt{576} = 24$ cm
The sand is 21 cm deep, which is less than 24 cm, so the sand forms a smaller cone similar to the whole cone.
Let the radius of the sand surface be $r$ cm.
$\dfrac{r}{7} = \dfrac{21}{24}$
$r = 6.125$
Volume of sand $= \dfrac{1}{3}\pi (6.125)^2 (21)$
$= 262.609\pi$
$= 825.01$
**Answer:** $825$ cm$^3$

(c) With the dome at the bottom, the sand fills the dome first.
Volume of hemisphere $= \dfrac{2}{3}\pi (7)^3 = 718.38$ cm$^3$
This is less than $825.01$ cm$^3$, so the rest of the sand goes into the cone.
Sand in the cone $= 825.01 - 718.38 = 106.63$ cm$^3$
Volume of whole cone $= \dfrac{1}{3}\pi (7)^2 (24) = 392\pi = 1231.50$ cm$^3$
Empty space at the top of the cone $= 1231.50 - 106.63 = 1124.87$ cm$^3$
The empty space is a cone similar to the whole cone. Let its height be $h$ cm.
$\left(\dfrac{h}{24}\right)^3 = \dfrac{1124.87}{1231.50}$
$\dfrac{h}{24} = \sqrt[3]{0.91341} = 0.97026$
$h = 23.286$
Height of sand inside the cone $= 24 - 23.286 = 0.714$ cm
Depth of sand $= 7 + 0.714 = 7.714$
**Answer:** $7.71$ cm

Likely Q10 · Mensuration, Vectors · 12 marks
Why it might be Advanced: Overlap cancels: square minus triangle equals M minus N, no pentagon area needed (hardest part (a))
(a) A badge is designed by laying a triangular piece of card over a square piece of card, as shown in Figure 1.
$PQRS$ is the square and its sides are 11 cm long.
$PTU$ is the triangle. Its right angle is at $P$, the vertex $U$ lies on the side $PS$, and the vertex $T$ lies on $PQ$ extended beyond $Q$, with $PT = 16$ cm.
The edge $UT$ crosses $QR$ at $V$.
Region $M$ is the part of the square that the triangle does not cover. Region $N$ is the part of the triangle that sticks out of the square.
The area of region $M$ is 45 cm$^{2}$ greater than the area of region $N$.
Calculate the length of $PU$. [3]
Answer: $PU = 9.5$ cm
(b) Figure 2 shows a parallelogram $OPQR$ with $\overrightarrow{OP} = \mathbf{p}$ and $\overrightarrow{OR} = \mathbf{r}$.
The point $N$ lies on the diagonal $PR$ and divides it so that $PN : NR = 1 : 3$. [0]
(i) Find $\overrightarrow{ON}$ in terms of $\mathbf{p}$ and $\mathbf{r}$, giving your answer in its simplest form. [2]
Answer: $\overrightarrow{ON} = \frac{3}{4}\mathbf{p} + \frac{1}{4}\mathbf{r}$
(ii) Find $\overrightarrow{NQ}$ in terms of $\mathbf{p}$ and $\mathbf{r}$, giving your answer in its simplest form. [2]
Answer: $\overrightarrow{NQ} = \frac{1}{4}\mathbf{p} + \frac{3}{4}\mathbf{r}$
(iii) The point $T$ lies on $OP$ and $\overrightarrow{OT} = \frac{2}{3}\mathbf{p}$.
Prove that the points $T$, $N$ and $Q$ are collinear. [3]
Answer: Shown: $\overrightarrow{TN} = \frac{1}{12}\mathbf{p} + \frac{1}{4}\mathbf{r} = \frac{1}{3}\overrightarrow{NQ}$, and $N$ is a common point, so $T$, $N$ and $Q$ are collinear.
(iv) State the value of $\frac{\text{area of triangle } OTN}{\text{area of triangle } ONQ}$. [1]
Answer: $\frac{1}{3}$
(v) State the value of $\frac{\text{area of triangle } TPN}{\text{area of parallelogram } OPQR}$. [1]
Answer: $\frac{1}{24}$
Worked solution
**(a)**
The square and the triangle overlap in the unshaded pentagon $PQVU$.
Area of square $=$ area of $PQVU$ $+$ area of $M$
Area of triangle $PTU$ $=$ area of $PQVU$ $+$ area of $N$
Subtract: the overlap cancels.
Area of square $-$ area of triangle $PTU$ $=$ area of $M$ $-$ area of $N$
$11^{2} - \frac{1}{2}(16)(PU) = 45$
$121 - 8\,PU = 45$
$8\,PU = 76$
**Answer:** $PU = 9.5$ cm

**(b)(i)**
$PN : NR = 1 : 3$, so $PR$ has $1 + 3 = 4$ parts and $\overrightarrow{PN} = \frac{1}{4}\overrightarrow{PR}$.
$\overrightarrow{PR} = \overrightarrow{PO} + \overrightarrow{OR} = -\mathbf{p} + \mathbf{r}$
$\overrightarrow{ON} = \overrightarrow{OP} + \overrightarrow{PN}$
$= \mathbf{p} + \frac{1}{4}(\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{p})$
**Answer:** $\overrightarrow{ON} = \frac{3}{4}\mathbf{p} + \frac{1}{4}\mathbf{r}$

**(b)(ii)**
$OPQR$ is a parallelogram, so $\overrightarrow{PQ} = \overrightarrow{OR} = \mathbf{r}$.
$\overrightarrow{OQ} = \overrightarrow{OP} + \overrightarrow{PQ} = \mathbf{p} + \mathbf{r}$
$\overrightarrow{NQ} = \overrightarrow{OQ} - \overrightarrow{ON}$
$= (\mathbf{p} + \mathbf{r}) - \left(\frac{3}{4}\mathbf{p} + \frac{1}{4}\mathbf{r}\right)$
**Answer:** $\overrightarrow{NQ} = \frac{1}{4}\mathbf{p} + \frac{3}{4}\mathbf{r}$

**(b)(iii)**
$\overrightarrow{TN} = \overrightarrow{ON} - \overrightarrow{OT}$
$= \frac{3}{4}\mathbf{p} + \frac{1}{4}\mathbf{r} - \frac{2}{3}\mathbf{p}$
$= \frac{1}{12}\mathbf{p} + \frac{1}{4}\mathbf{r}$
$= \frac{1}{3}\left(\frac{1}{4}\mathbf{p} + \frac{3}{4}\mathbf{r}\right)$
$= \frac{1}{3}\overrightarrow{NQ}$
$\overrightarrow{TN}$ is a scalar multiple of $\overrightarrow{NQ}$, so $TN$ is parallel to $NQ$.
$N$ is a common point.
**Answer:** $T$, $N$ and $Q$ lie on a straight line (shown).

**(b)(iv)**
Triangles $OTN$ and $ONQ$ have the same height from $O$ to the line $TQ$.
So their areas are in the ratio of their bases, $TN : NQ = 1 : 3$.
**Answer:** $\frac{1}{3}$

**(b)(v)**
$TP = \frac{1}{3}OP$, so area of $TPN = \frac{1}{3} \times$ area of $OPN$ (same height from $N$).
$PN = \frac{1}{4}PR$, so area of $OPN = \frac{1}{4} \times$ area of $OPR$ (same height from $O$).
Area of $OPR = \frac{1}{2} \times$ area of parallelogram $OPQR$.
$\frac{1}{3} \times \frac{1}{4} \times \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{24}$
**Answer:** $\frac{1}{24}$

Without using a calculator, find the exact value of

Only the first denominator and the last numerator are left.
$= \frac{50}{4}$
$= \frac{25}{2}$

Likely Q12 · Numbers (HCF and LCM) · 2 marks
Why it might be Advanced: Find sides from given HCF and LCM with size constraint (hardest part whole)
A rectangular card has a length and a breadth that are each a whole number of centimetres.
The card can be cut exactly into identical squares, with no card left over. The largest possible side of such a square is 28 cm.
Copies of the card, all placed the same way round, are laid side by side to form a square. The smallest possible side of this square is 336 cm.
The length and the breadth of the card are each more than 50 cm.
Find the length and the breadth of the card.
Worked solution
The largest square that cuts the card exactly has a side equal to the HCF of the length and the breadth.
The smallest square made from copies of the card has a side equal to the LCM of the length and the breadth.
HCF $= 28 = 2^{2} \times 7$
LCM $= 336 = 2^{4} \times 3 \times 7$
Both sides must carry the HCF, so each side has at least $2^{2} \times 7$.
Between them, the two sides must supply $2^{4}$ and $3$ to make the LCM.
The extra factors are $2^{2}$ and $3$. They cannot both go to the same side, or that side would be $336$ and the other would be $28$, which is not more than $50$.
They also cannot be split so that both sides gain a common factor, or the HCF would be bigger than $28$.
So one side takes the extra $2^{2}$ and the other side takes the $3$.
One side $= 2^{4} \times 7 = 112$
Other side $= 2^{2} \times 3 \times 7 = 84$
Check: both $112$ and $84$ are more than $50$.
**Answer:** the length is $112$ cm and the breadth is $84$ cm.

Stage loop: the robot goes straight from $D$ to the corner $P$, then along the edges $PQ$, $QR$, $RS$, $ST$ and $TU$ of the stage, and then straight from $U$ back to $D$.
(a) The fountain has radius $5$ m and $OD = 13$ m. The straight parts of the fountain loop touch the fountain at $M$ and $N$. Show that the length of the fountain loop is $43.7$ m, correct to 3 significant figures. [4]
Answer: Shown: $DM = DN = 12$ m, major arc $MKN = 5(2\pi - 2\cos^{-1}\tfrac{5}{13}) \approx 19.656$ m, loop $= 24 + 19.656 = 43.656 \approx 43.7$ m (3 s.f.)
(b) For the stage, $DP = 6$ m, $PQ = 7$ m and angle $DPQ = 160^\circ$. Find the length of the stage loop, and state, with working, which of the two loops is shorter. [4]
Answer: Stage loop $\approx 45.5$ m; the fountain loop is shorter, by about $1.89$ m
(c) One night the robot completes the stage loop in exactly 1 minute 20 seconds. Calculate its average speed in metres per second. [1]
Answer: $0.569$ m/s (3 s.f.)
Worked solution
(a) Fountain loop
The shortest loop is: tangent $DM$, then the major arc $MKN$, then tangent $ND$.
Tangent $\perp$ radius, so angle $OMD = 90^\circ$.
$DM = \sqrt{13^2 - 5^2} = \sqrt{144} = 12$ m
Tangents from one outside point are equal, so $DN = 12$ m.
$\cos \angle MOD = \dfrac{5}{13}$
$\angle MOD = 1.17601$ rad
$\angle MON = 2 \times 1.17601 = 2.35201$ rad
Reflex angle $MON = 2\pi - 2.35201 = 3.93118$ rad
Major arc $MKN = r\theta = 5 \times 3.93118 = 19.6559$ m
Loop $= 12 + 12 + 19.6559 = 43.6559$ m
**Answer:** fountain loop $= 43.7$ m (3 s.f.) (shown)

(b) Stage loop
Interior angle of a regular hexagon $= \dfrac{(6-2) \times 180^\circ}{6} = 120^\circ$
So angle $QPU = 120^\circ$.
Angle $DPU = 160^\circ - 120^\circ = 40^\circ$
In triangle $DPU$: $DP = 6$, $PU = 7$, included angle $40^\circ$, so use the cosine rule.
$DU^2 = 6^2 + 7^2 - 2(6)(7)\cos 40^\circ$
$DU^2 = 85 - 84\cos 40^\circ = 20.6523$
$DU = 4.5445$ m
Stage loop $= DP + 5 \text{ sides} + UD$
$= 6 + 5(7) + 4.5445$
$= 45.5445$ m
$45.5445 - 43.6559 = 1.89$ m
**Answer:** stage loop $= 45.5$ m (3 s.f.); the fountain loop is shorter, by about $1.89$ m.

(c) Average speed
1 minute 20 seconds $= 80$ s
Average speed $= \dfrac{\text{total distance}}{\text{total time}} = \dfrac{45.5445}{80}$
**Answer:** $0.569$ m/s (3 s.f.)
