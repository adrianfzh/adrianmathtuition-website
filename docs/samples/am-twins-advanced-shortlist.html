Likely Q1 · Integration (Area) · 9 marks
Why it might be Advanced: Bound a curve's area using triangle and rectangle, needs concavity argument. (hardest part (b) bounding the integral)
The curve $y = \ln(7 - kx)$, where $k$ is a positive integer, passes through the point $P(1, \ln 3)$.
(a) Show that $k = 4$. [2]
Answer: $k = 4$ (shown)
(b) Without evaluating the integral, show that $2\ln 3 < \displaystyle\int_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{3}{2}} \ln(7 - 4x)\, dx < 4\ln 3$. [4]
Answer: Shown: the region lies above the triangle of area $2\ln 3$ and inside the rectangle of area $4\ln 3$
(c) Find the area of the region enclosed by the curve $x = \frac{1}{4}(7 - e^{y})$, the $y$-axis and the line $y = \ln 15$. [3]
Answer: $2 - \frac{7}{4}\ln\frac{15}{7} \approx 0.666$ units$^2$
Worked solution
(a)
Substitute $P(1, \ln 3)$ into the curve.
$\ln 3 = \ln(7 - k)$
$7 - k = 3$
**Answer:** $k = 4$ (shown)

(b)
Let $y = \ln(7 - 4x)$.
At $x = -\frac{1}{2}$: $y = \ln 9 = 2\ln 3$
At $x = \frac{3}{2}$: $y = \ln 1 = 0$
$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{-4}{7 - 4x} < 0$ for $-\frac{1}{2} \le x \le \frac{3}{2}$, so the curve decreases from $2\ln 3$ to $0$.
$\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = \frac{-16}{(7 - 4x)^2} < 0$, so the curve bends downwards and lies above the straight line joining $(-\frac{1}{2}, 2\ln 3)$ and $(\frac{3}{2}, 0)$.
Lower bound: the triangle under that straight line.
Area of triangle $= \frac{1}{2} \times 2 \times 2\ln 3 = 2\ln 3$
Upper bound: the rectangle of width $2$ and height $2\ln 3$, since $y \le 2\ln 3$ on the interval.
Area of rectangle $= 2 \times 2\ln 3 = 4\ln 3$
The area under the curve lies between the two.
**Answer:** $2\ln 3 < \int_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{3}{2}} \ln(7 - 4x)\, dx < 4\ln 3$ (shown)

(c)
The curve meets the $y$-axis when $x = 0$:
$7 - e^{y} = 0$
$y = \ln 7$
For $\ln 7 \le y \le \ln 15$, $e^{y} \ge 7$, so $x \le 0$: the region is on the left of the $y$-axis.
Area $= \left| \int_{\ln 7}^{\ln 15} \frac{1}{4}(7 - e^{y})\, dy \right|$
$= \frac{1}{4} \int_{\ln 7}^{\ln 15} (e^{y} - 7)\, dy$
$= \frac{1}{4} \left[ e^{y} - 7y \right]_{\ln 7}^{\ln 15}$
$= \frac{1}{4} \left[ (15 - 7\ln 15) - (7 - 7\ln 7) \right]$
$= \frac{1}{4} \left( 8 - 7\ln\frac{15}{7} \right)$
$= 2 - \frac{7}{4}\ln\frac{15}{7}$
**Answer:** $2 - \frac{7}{4}\ln\frac{15}{7} \approx 0.666$ units$^2$

Likely Q2 · Logarithms · 7 marks
Why it might be Advanced: log p, log q as unknowns; one equation is a product of logs (hardest part log p, log q as unknowns; one equation is a product of logs)
The positive numbers $p$ and $q$ are such that
$$\log_3\left(\dfrac{p^2}{9q}\right) = 3 \quad\text{and}\quad (\log_3 p)(\log_3 q) = 3.$$
Find the exact values of $p$ and $q$.
Worked solution
Break up the first equation with the log laws.
$\log_3 p^2 - \log_3 9 - \log_3 q = 3$
$2\log_3 p - 2 - \log_3 q = 3$
$2\log_3 p - \log_3 q = 5$
Let $a = \log_3 p$ and $b = \log_3 q$.
$2a - b = 5$, so $b = 2a - 5$.
The second equation is $ab = 3$.
Substitute $b = 2a - 5$:
$a(2a - 5) = 3$
$2a^2 - 5a - 3 = 0$
$(2a + 1)(a - 3) = 0$
$a = 3$ or $a = -\dfrac{1}{2}$
When $a = 3$: $b = 2(3) - 5 = 1$.
$p = 3^3 = 27$, $q = 3^1 = 3$
When $a = -\dfrac{1}{2}$: $b = 2\left(-\dfrac{1}{2}\right) - 5 = -6$.
$p = 3^{-\frac{1}{2}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$, $q = 3^{-6} = \dfrac{1}{729}$
A negative logarithm is fine: all four values are positive, so both pairs are accepted.
**Answer:** $p = 27,\ q = 3$ or $p = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}},\ q = \dfrac{1}{729}$

Likely Q3 · Partial Fractions · 5 marks

Given that $\frac{3}{(3x-1)(3x+2)} \equiv \frac{A}{3x-1} + \frac{B}{3x+2}$, find the value of each of the constants $A$ and $B$. Using your result, evaluate exactly the sum $\frac{1}{10} + \frac{1}{40} + \frac{1}{88} + \cdots + \frac{1}{59 \times 62}$, giving your answer as a fraction in its lowest terms.
Worked solution
$\frac{3}{(3x-1)(3x+2)} = \frac{A}{3x-1} + \frac{B}{3x+2}$
$3 = A(3x+2) + B(3x-1)$
Put $x = \frac{1}{3}$: $3 = 3A$, so $A = 1$
Put $x = -\frac{2}{3}$: $3 = -3B$, so $B = -1$
$\frac{3}{(3x-1)(3x+2)} = \frac{1}{3x-1} - \frac{1}{3x+2}$
The terms are $\frac{1}{2 \times 5}, \frac{1}{5 \times 8}, \frac{1}{8 \times 11}, \ldots, \frac{1}{59 \times 62}$, i.e. $\frac{1}{(3x-1)(3x+2)}$ for $x = 1, 2, \ldots, 20$
Each term $= \frac{1}{3}\left(\frac{1}{3x-1} - \frac{1}{3x+2}\right)$
Sum $= \frac{1}{3}\left[\left(\frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{5}\right) + \left(\frac{1}{5} - \frac{1}{8}\right) + \cdots + \left(\frac{1}{59} - \frac{1}{62}\right)\right]$
The middle terms cancel in pairs.
$= \frac{1}{3}\left(\frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{62}\right)$
$= \frac{1}{3} \times \frac{30}{62}$
$= \frac{5}{31}$
**Answer:** $A = 1$, $B = -1$; sum $= \frac{5}{31}$

Likely Q4 · Polynomials · 3 marks
Why it might be Advanced: three integer roots: product fixed, maximise q over factorisations (hardest part three integer roots: product fixed, maximise q over factorisations)
The curve $y = x^3 + px^2 + qx - 9$, where $p$ and $q$ are constants, crosses the $x$-axis at exactly three points. The $x$-coordinate of each of these points is an integer. Find the greatest possible value of $q$.
Worked solution
Let the curve cross the $x$-axis at $x = \alpha$, $x = \beta$ and $x = \gamma$, three different integers.
The coefficient of $x^3$ is $1$, so
$x^3 + px^2 + qx - 9 = (x - \alpha)(x - \beta)(x - \gamma)$
Comparing the constant terms: $-\alpha\beta\gamma = -9$, so $\alpha\beta\gamma = 9$.
Comparing the coefficients of $x$: $q = \alpha\beta + \beta\gamma + \gamma\alpha$.
Each root is a factor of $9$, so each is one of $\pm 1$, $\pm 3$, $\pm 9$.
Ignoring signs, the three sizes multiply to $9$: they are $1, 1, 9$ or $1, 3, 3$.
The roots are different, so two roots of the same size must be opposite in sign.
Case 1: roots $1$, $-1$ and $\gamma$. Then $(1)(-1)\gamma = 9$, so $\gamma = -9$.
$y = (x - 1)(x + 1)(x + 9) = (x^2 - 1)(x + 9) = x^3 + 9x^2 - x - 9$, so $q = -1$.
Case 2: roots $3$, $-3$ and $\gamma$. Then $(3)(-3)\gamma = 9$, so $\gamma = -1$.
$y = (x - 3)(x + 3)(x + 1) = (x^2 - 9)(x + 1) = x^3 + x^2 - 9x - 9$, so $q = -9$.
These are the only two cases, and $-1 > -9$.
**Answer:** the greatest possible value of $q$ is $-1$

Likely Q5 · Polynomials · 9 marks
Why it might be Advanced: Negative gradient everywhere becomes discriminant condition on derived quadratic (hardest part a)
A curve has equation $y = -4x^3 + px^2 + qx + 5$, where $p$ and $q$ are constants.
(a) The gradient of the curve is negative at every point, so that $y$ decreases as $x$ increases for all real values of $x$. Find the condition that $p$ and $q$ must satisfy. [4]
Answer: $p^2 < -12q$
(b) It is now given that $p = 12$ and $q = 2$. The line $y = 7x + 11$ meets the curve at three points. Find the $x$-coordinate of each of these points. [5]
Answer: $x = 2$ or $-\frac{1}{2}$ or $\frac{3}{2}$
Worked solution
**(a)**
$y = -4x^3 + px^2 + qx + 5$
$\frac{dy}{dx} = -12x^2 + 2px + q$
For $y$ to be always decreasing, $\frac{dy}{dx} < 0$ for all real $x$.
$\frac{dy}{dx}$ is a quadratic in $x$. It stays negative when two things hold.
The coefficient of $x^2$ is $-12$, which is negative, so the quadratic opens downwards.
It must also never touch the $x$-axis, so its discriminant is negative.
$b^2 - 4ac < 0$
$(2p)^2 - 4(-12)(q) < 0$
$4p^2 + 48q < 0$
$p^2 + 12q < 0$
**Answer:** $p^2 < -12q$

**(b)**
With $p = 12$ and $q = 2$, the curve is $y = -4x^3 + 12x^2 + 2x + 5$.
At the points where the line meets the curve, the two $y$ values are equal.
$-4x^3 + 12x^2 + 2x + 5 = 7x + 11$
$-4x^3 + 12x^2 - 5x - 6 = 0$
$4x^3 - 12x^2 + 5x + 6 = 0$
Let $f(x) = 4x^3 - 12x^2 + 5x + 6$.
$f(2) = 4(8) - 12(4) + 5(2) + 6 = 32 - 48 + 10 + 6 = 0$
So $(x - 2)$ is a factor of $f(x)$.
$4x^3 - 12x^2 + 5x + 6 = (x - 2)(4x^2 + kx - 3)$
Compare the coefficients of $x^2$: $-12 = k - 8$, so $k = -4$.
$f(x) = (x - 2)(4x^2 - 4x - 3)$
$f(x) = (x - 2)(2x + 1)(2x - 3)$
$(x - 2)(2x + 1)(2x - 3) = 0$
**Answer:** $x = 2$ or $x = -\frac{1}{2}$ or $x = \frac{3}{2}$

Likely Q6 · Quadratic Functions · 6 marks

Answer: $-12 < p < 24$; smallest integer $p = -11$
Worked solution
**(i)**
For $-2x^{2} + 6x - 7$: $a = -2$, $b = 6$, $c = -7$.
$b^{2} - 4ac = 6^{2} - 4(-2)(-7)$
$= 36 - 56$
$= -20$
Since $b^{2} - 4ac < 0$, the curve $y = -2x^{2} + 6x - 7$ never meets the $x$-axis.
Since the coefficient of $x^{2}$ is $-2 < 0$, the curve opens downwards, so it lies entirely below the $x$-axis.
**Answer:** $-2x^{2} + 6x - 7$ is negative for every real value of $x$.

**(ii)**
$\dfrac{x^{2} + px + 20}{-2x^{2} + 6x - 7} < 1$

$x^{2} + px + 20 > -2x^{2} + 6x - 7$

$(p - 6)^{2} - 4(3)(27) < 0$
$(p - 6)^{2} - 324 < 0$
$(p - 6 - 18)(p - 6 + 18) < 0$
$(p - 24)(p + 12) < 0$
$-12 < p < 24$
**Answer:** $-12 < p < 24$; the smallest integer value of $p$ is $-11$.

Likely Q7 · Trigonometry (Applications) · 3 marks
Why it might be Advanced: incircle radius via area and semiperimeter; no diagram given (hardest part incircle radius via area and semiperimeter; no diagram given)
A triangular courtyard has walls of lengths 8 m, 14 m and 18 m. A circular pond is to be built inside the courtyard so that its edge just touches all three walls. Find the radius of the pond, giving your answer in exact form.
Worked solution
Let the angle between the 8 m and 14 m walls be $\theta$. It faces the 18 m wall.
By the cosine rule:
$\cos\theta = \frac{8^{2} + 14^{2} - 18^{2}}{2(8)(14)} = \frac{-64}{224} = -\frac{2}{7}$
$\theta$ is obtuse, but $\sin\theta$ is still positive:
$\sin\theta = \sqrt{1 - \left(-\frac{2}{7}\right)^{2}} = \sqrt{\frac{45}{49}} = \frac{3\sqrt{5}}{7}$
Area of courtyard $= \frac{1}{2}(8)(14)\sin\theta = 56 \times \frac{3\sqrt{5}}{7} = 24\sqrt{5}$ m$^{2}$
The pond is the inscribed circle. Join its centre to the three corners: this cuts the courtyard into three triangles, each of height $r$.
Area $= \frac{1}{2}r(8 + 14 + 18) = 20r$
$20r = 24\sqrt{5}$
$r = \frac{24\sqrt{5}}{20} = \frac{6\sqrt{5}}{5}$
**Answer:** radius $= \frac{6\sqrt{5}}{5}$ m

Likely Q12 · Trigonometry (Identities) · 5 marks
Why it might be Advanced: Unled identity: factorise cube difference, cos2θ, and clear sec/cosec fractions (hardest part whole)
Show that the identity $\dfrac{\cos 2\theta}{\cos^3\theta - \sin^3\theta} = \dfrac{\sec\theta + \operatorname{cosec}\theta}{1 + \sec\theta\operatorname{cosec}\theta}$ holds for all values of $\theta$ for which both sides are defined.
Worked solution
Work on the left-hand side only.
LHS $= \dfrac{\cos 2\theta}{\cos^3\theta - \sin^3\theta}$
Use $\cos 2\theta = \cos^2\theta - \sin^2\theta$ on top, and the difference of two cubes below:
$= \dfrac{\cos^2\theta - \sin^2\theta}{(\cos\theta - \sin\theta)(\cos^2\theta + \cos\theta\sin\theta + \sin^2\theta)}$

$= \dfrac{(\cos\theta - \sin\theta)(\cos\theta + \sin\theta)}{(\cos\theta - \sin\theta)(1 + \sin\theta\cos\theta)}$
Cancel the common factor $(\cos\theta - \sin\theta)$:
$= \dfrac{\cos\theta + \sin\theta}{1 + \sin\theta\cos\theta}$
Divide the numerator and the denominator by $\sin\theta\cos\theta$:
$= \dfrac{\dfrac{1}{\sin\theta} + \dfrac{1}{\cos\theta}}{\dfrac{1}{\sin\theta\cos\theta} + 1}$
$= \dfrac{\operatorname{cosec}\theta + \sec\theta}{\sec\theta\operatorname{cosec}\theta + 1}$
$= \dfrac{\sec\theta + \operatorname{cosec}\theta}{1 + \sec\theta\operatorname{cosec}\theta}$ = RHS (proved)
**Answer:** Proved
