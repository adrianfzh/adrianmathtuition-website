Likely Q1 · Algebra (Expressions), Algebra (Factorization) · 3 marks
Why it might be Advanced: Group into (3a-b)^2-(3a-b); consecutive integers give even product (hardest part whole)
A factory packs stickers into boxes. The number of stickers in one box is given by $9a^{2} - 6ab + b^{2} - 3a + b$, where $a$ and $b$ are positive integers with $b < a$.

By factorising this expression, explain why the stickers in a box can always be shared equally between two children with none left over.
Worked solution
Group the first three terms and the last two terms.
$9a^{2} - 6ab + b^{2} - 3a + b = (9a^{2} - 6ab + b^{2}) - (3a - b)$
The first group is a perfect square.
$= (3a - b)^{2} - (3a - b)$
Take out the common factor $(3a - b)$.
$= (3a - b)(3a - b - 1)$
Since $a$ and $b$ are integers, $(3a - b)$ and $(3a - b - 1)$ are integers that differ by 1, so they are consecutive integers.
Of two consecutive integers, one is even and the other is odd.
Even $\times$ odd $=$ even.
So $9a^{2} - 6ab + b^{2} - 3a + b$ is always an even number, i.e. a multiple of 2.
**Answer:** The number of stickers is $(3a - b)(3a - b - 1)$, which is always even, so it can always be shared equally between two children with none left over. (Shown)

Likely Q4 · Coordinate Geometry (Lines) · 5 marks
Why it might be Advanced: Two identical slabs stacked: set up unknown length and width from given corners (hardest part (a))
(a) A designer draws two identical rectangular paving slabs on a coordinate plan. One slab lies flat with its longer sides horizontal. The other slab stands on top of it with its longer sides vertical, and the right-hand edges of the two slabs are in one straight vertical line. The bottom left corner of the flat slab is $P(6, 8)$ and the top right corner of the standing slab is $Q(41, 57)$. $R$ is the bottom left corner of the standing slab. Find the coordinates of $R$. [2]
Answer: $R(27, 22)$
(b) The grid shows the points $S(-1, -5)$ and $T(3, 1)$, and the origin $O$. A straight line is drawn through $S$ and $T$. [3]
(i) Calculate the gradient of the line $ST$. [1]
Answer: $\frac{3}{2}$
(ii) Write down the coordinates of the point where the line $ST$ crosses the $y$-axis. [1]
Answer: $(0, -3.5)$
(iii) Give the equation of the line $ST$. [1]
Answer: $y = \frac{3}{2}x - \frac{7}{2}$
Worked solution
(a)
The two slabs are identical, so each has the same long side and the same short side.
Going across from $P$ to $Q$ covers only the long side of the flat slab.
Long side $= 41 - 6 = 35$
Going up from $P$ to $Q$ covers the short side of the flat slab, then the long side of the standing slab.
Short side $+$ long side $= 57 - 8 = 49$
Short side $= 49 - 35 = 14$
$R$ is one short side to the left of the right-hand edge.
$x$-coordinate of $R = 41 - 14 = 27$
$R$ is one short side above the bottom of the flat slab.
$y$-coordinate of $R = 8 + 14 = 22$
**Answer:** $R(27, 22)$

(b)(i)
Gradient $= \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$
$= \frac{1 - (-5)}{3 - (-1)}$
$= \frac{6}{4}$
$= \frac{3}{2}$
**Answer:** $\frac{3}{2}$

(b)(ii)
From $S(-1, -5)$, move 1 unit to the right to reach the $y$-axis.
The gradient is $\frac{3}{2}$, so the line rises $1.5$ units.
$y = -5 + 1.5 = -3.5$
**Answer:** $(0, -3.5)$

(b)(iii)
Use $y = mx + c$ with $m = \frac{3}{2}$ and $c = -3.5 = -\frac{7}{2}$.
$y = \frac{3}{2}x - \frac{7}{2}$
Check with $T(3, 1)$: $\frac{3}{2}(3) - \frac{7}{2} = \frac{9}{2} - \frac{7}{2} = 1$ ✓
**Answer:** $y = \frac{3}{2}x - \frac{7}{2}$

Likely Q5 · Mensuration · 4 marks
Why it might be Advanced: Equal volume does not mean the sphere fits; compare diameter with cube side (hardest part whole)
A craft shop sells a solid spherical candle made from $1200$ cm$^{3}$ of wax. The shop packs its candles in cubical gift boxes, each with an internal capacity of $1331$ cm$^{3}$.

The shop assistant says, "The box holds more than the amount of wax in the candle, so the candle will go into the box."

Show, with calculations, that the shop assistant is wrong.
Worked solution
A bigger capacity does not mean the candle goes in. Compare the widths.

Internal side of box $= \sqrt[3]{1331}$
$= 11$ cm

Let the radius of the candle be $r$ cm.
$\dfrac{4}{3}\pi r^{3} = 1200$
$r^{3} = \dfrac{3 \times 1200}{4\pi}$
$r^{3} = 286.48$
$r = \sqrt[3]{286.48}$
$r = 6.592$ cm

Diameter of candle $= 2 \times 6.592$
$= 13.2$ cm (3 s.f.)

$13.2$ cm $> 11$ cm

**Answer:** The diameter of the candle ($13.2$ cm) is greater than the internal side of the box ($11$ cm), so the candle cannot go into the box. The shop assistant is wrong.

Likely Q6 · Mensuration, Pythagoras' Theorem · 9 marks
Why it might be Advanced: Touching balls form equilateral triangle; height via 60 degrees geometry, unsignposted (hardest part (b))
Three identical solid bronze balls rest in a rectangular tray $PQRS$. Diagram 1 shows the view from directly above. $A$, $B$ and $C$ are the centres of the balls, and each ball has radius $5$ cm. The balls with centres $A$ and $B$ touch each other, and both touch the side $SR$. The ball with centre $A$ touches the side $PS$ and the ball with centre $B$ touches the side $QR$. The third ball, with centre $C$, touches both of the other balls and the side $PQ$.
(a) Find the volume of one bronze ball. [2]
Answer: $524$ cm$^{3}$
(b) Show that the width $PS$ of the tray is $18.66$ cm, correct to two decimal places. [2]
Answer: Shown: $AC = 10$ cm, half of $AB = 5$ cm, so distance from $C$ to $AB$ $= \sqrt{10^{2} - 5^{2}} = \sqrt{75}$ cm, and $PS = 5 + \sqrt{75} + 5 = 18.6602\ldots \approx 18.66$ cm.
(c) A craftsman melts all three balls and uses all the bronze to cast one solid cone, shown in Diagram 2. The base radius of the cone is $\frac{2}{3}$ of its height. Calculate the height, $h$, of the cone. [3]
Answer: $15$ cm
(d) Calculate the slant height, $l$, of the cone. [2]
Answer: $18.0$ cm
Worked solution
(a)
Volume of a sphere $= \frac{4}{3}\pi r^{3}$
$= \frac{4}{3}\pi (5)^{3}$
$= \frac{500}{3}\pi$
$= 523.59\ldots$
**Answer:** $524$ cm$^{3}$ (3 s.f.)

(b)
Join $A$, $B$ and $C$. Let $M$ be the midpoint of $AB$.
Balls that touch have their centres two radii apart, so $AC = 5 + 5 = 10$ cm and $AB = 10$ cm.
$AM = 10 \div 2 = 5$ cm.
Triangle $AMC$ is right-angled at $M$.
By Pythagoras' theorem, $CM^{2} = AC^{2} - AM^{2}$
$CM^{2} = 10^{2} - 5^{2} = 75$
$CM = \sqrt{75} = 8.6602\ldots$ cm
Distance from $SR$ up to $AB$ $= 5$ cm (one radius).
Distance from $C$ up to $PQ$ $= 5$ cm (one radius).
$PS = 5 + \sqrt{75} + 5$
$= 18.6602\ldots$
**Answer:** $PS \approx 18.66$ cm (2 d.p.) (shown)

(c)
The bronze is only reshaped, so volume of cone $=$ total volume of the three balls.
Total volume $= 3 \times \frac{500}{3}\pi = 500\pi$ cm$^{3}$
Base radius of cone $= \frac{2}{3}h$
$\frac{1}{3}\pi \left(\frac{2}{3}h\right)^{2} h = 500\pi$
$\frac{1}{3} \times \frac{4}{9}h^{3} = 500$
$\frac{4}{27}h^{3} = 500$
$h^{3} = 500 \times \frac{27}{4} = 3375$
$h = \sqrt[3]{3375} = 15$
**Answer:** $h = 15$ cm

(d)
Base radius $= \frac{2}{3} \times 15 = 10$ cm
The height, the base radius and the slant height form a right-angled triangle.
By Pythagoras' theorem, $l^{2} = 15^{2} + 10^{2}$
$l^{2} = 325$
$l = \sqrt{325} = 18.027\ldots$
**Answer:** $l = 18.0$ cm (3 s.f.)

Likely Q7 · Numbers (Prime Factorization) · 2 marks
Why it might be Advanced: Pack size 756/m must divide 1200: reversed factor condition, not usual least multiplier (hardest part (b))
A printing shop ties its leaflets in bundles of $756$ and sends them out in cartons that hold exactly $1200$ leaflets each.
It is given that $756 = 2^{2} \times 3^{3} \times 7$ and $1200 = 2^{4} \times 3 \times 5^{2}$.
(a) The shop opens $n$ bundles and packs all the leaflets into cartons. Every carton used is completely full and no leaflet is left over. Find the smallest possible value of $n$. [1]
Answer: $n = 100$
(b) Each bundle is instead split into $m$ equal packs, so that one pack holds $\frac{756}{m}$ leaflets. A carton must be filled exactly by a whole number of these packs. Find the smallest possible value of $m$. [1]
Answer: $m = 63$
Worked solution
(a) Full cartons with nothing left over means $756n$ is a multiple of $1200$.
$756n = 2^{2} \times 3^{3} \times 7 \times n$
$1200 = 2^{4} \times 3 \times 5^{2}$
Compare the powers of each prime.
$2$: have $2^{2}$, need $2^{4}$, so $n$ supplies $2^{2}$.
$3$: have $3^{3}$, need $3$, so nothing more is needed.
$5$: have none, need $5^{2}$, so $n$ supplies $5^{2}$.
Smallest $n = 2^{2} \times 5^{2}$
**Answer:** $n = 100$

(b) A whole number of packs fills a carton exactly, so $\frac{756}{m}$ is a factor of $1200$.
$756 = 2^{2} \times 3^{3} \times 7$
$1200 = 2^{4} \times 3 \times 5^{2}$
For $m$ to be smallest, $\frac{756}{m}$ must be the largest number that divides both.
Keep only what $1200$ also has: $2^{2} \times 3 = 12$.
So $m$ removes the rest: $3^{2} \times 7$.
$m = \frac{756}{12}$
**Answer:** $m = 63$

Likely Q8 · Proportion · 4 marks
Why it might be Advanced: Fixed charge must be removed before applying proportion to d squared (hardest part whole)
A bakery sells round cakes of different sizes. The price of a cake, $\$P$, is made up of a fixed charge of $\$3$ for the box and an amount that is directly proportional to the square of the diameter, $d$ cm, of the cake.

A cake of a certain diameter is priced at $\$35$.

Without finding the diameter of this cake, calculate the price of a cake whose diameter is three-quarters of the diameter of this cake.
Worked solution
Take away the fixed charge first: only $P - 3$ is proportional to $d^2$.
$P - 3 = kd^2$, where $k$ is a constant.

For the first cake, $P = 35$:
$35 - 3 = kd^2$
$kd^2 = 32$

The new diameter is $\dfrac{3}{4}d$.
New value of $P - 3 = k\left(\dfrac{3}{4}d\right)^2$
$= \dfrac{9}{16}kd^2$
$= \dfrac{9}{16} \times 32$
$= 18$

Put the fixed charge back:
$P = 18 + 3 = 21$

**Answer:** $\$21$

(a) $9$ identical pumps can empty a flooded basement in $20$ hours. Assuming that every pump removes water at the same steady rate, find the number of hours that $12$ of these pumps would need to empty the same basement. [1]
Answer: $15$ hours
(b) A camera takes photographs whose sides are in the ratio width : height $= 8 : 5$. A rectangular greeting card has sides in the ratio width : height $= 12 : 11$. A photograph is enlarged, without changing its shape, and printed at the top of the card so that it stretches across the whole width of the card. It does not reach the bottom of the card, so a blank strip is left below it for a message, as shown in the diagram. Find the ratio height of card covered by the photograph : height of card left blank. Give your answer in the form $a : b$, where $a$ and $b$ are integers. [2]
Answer: $15 : 7$
Worked solution
**(a)**
More pumps means less time, so the number of pumps and the time are in inverse proportion.
$9$ pumps take $20$ hours.
$1$ pump takes $9 \times 20 = 180$ hours.
$12$ pumps take $\dfrac{180}{12} = 15$ hours.
**Answer:** $15$ hours

**(b)**
The photograph fills the width of the card, so make the two widths the same number.
The widths are $8$ and $12$. Their lowest common multiple is $24$.
Photograph: width : height $= 8 : 5 = 24 : 15$ (multiply both by $3$).
Card: width : height $= 12 : 11 = 24 : 22$ (multiply both by $2$).
When the width is $24$ units, the photograph is $15$ units tall and the card is $22$ units tall.
Height left blank $= 22 - 15 = 7$ units.
Height covered : height left blank $= 15 : 7$.
**Answer:** $15 : 7$

Likely Q10 · Trigonometry · 2 marks
Why it might be Advanced: cos(90-θ) equals sin θ: complementary-angle link, undrilled at Sec 2 (hardest part whole)
The diagram shows the side view of a loading ramp $PR$ resting on horizontal ground $PQ$, with a vertical support $QR$. The ramp makes an angle $\theta$ with the ground, and angle $PQR = 90^{\circ}$.

Given that $\tan \theta = \frac{8}{15}$, find the exact value of $\cos\left(90^{\circ} - \theta\right)$, showing your working clearly. Do not use a calculator.
Worked solution
$\tan \theta = \frac{8}{15}$, so the side opposite $\theta$ is $QR = 8$ units and the side adjacent to $\theta$ is $PQ = 15$ units.
By Pythagoras' Theorem, $PR = \sqrt{8^2 + 15^2} = \sqrt{289} = 17$ units.
Angle $PRQ = 180^{\circ} - 90^{\circ} - \theta = 90^{\circ} - \theta$ (angle sum of triangle).
For angle $PRQ$, the adjacent side is $QR = 8$ and the hypotenuse is $PR = 17$.
$\cos\left(90^{\circ} - \theta\right) = \frac{QR}{PR} = \frac{8}{17}$
(Check: this is the same as $\sin \theta = \frac{8}{17}$.)
**Answer:** $\frac{8}{17}$

Maybe Q11 · Algebra (Factorization) · 3 marks
Why it might be Advanced: Consecutive-integer parity argument after factorising (hardest part whole)
A school hall is set up with $9n^{2} + 9n + 2$ chairs, where $n$ is a positive integer.
The caretaker says that, whatever the value of $n$, the chairs can always be put into pairs with no chair left over.
Factorise $9n^{2} + 9n + 2$ and use your result to show that the caretaker is correct.
Worked solution
Factorise the expression first.
$9n^2 + 9n + 2 = (3n + 1)(3n + 2)$
Check by expanding: $9n^2 + 6n + 3n + 2 = 9n^2 + 9n + 2$.
Now look at the two factors.
$(3n + 2) - (3n + 1) = 1$, so $3n + 1$ and $3n + 2$ are consecutive integers.
Of any two consecutive integers, one is even.
Even $\times$ any integer $=$ even.
So $(3n + 1)(3n + 2)$ is even for every positive integer $n$.
An even number of chairs can be put into pairs with none left over.
**Answer:** $9n^2 + 9n + 2 = (3n+1)(3n+2)$, a product of two consecutive integers, so it is always even and the caretaker is correct.

Maybe Q15 · Algebra (Inequalities) · 4 marks
Why it might be Advanced: Bounds with negative values: squared term minimum is zero case (hardest part (c))
In a number game, Farah picks one card from a red pile and one card from a blue pile. Each card shows an integer. The number $p$ on the red card satisfies $-4 \leq p \leq 6$ and the number $q$ on the blue card satisfies $2 \leq q \leq 5$.
For each score below, find the value asked for.
(a) The lowest score Farah can get if her score is $pq$. [1]
Answer: $-20$
(b) The lowest score Farah can get if her score is $\frac{3p}{q}$. [1]
Answer: $-6$
(c) The lowest score Farah can get if her score is $(2p - q)^2$. [1]
Answer: $0$
(d) The highest score Farah can get if her score is $q - \frac{p^2}{q}$. [1]
Answer: $5$
Worked solution
$p$ can be negative, zero or positive. $q$ is always positive.

**(a)** For the lowest $pq$, we want a negative product that is as large in size as possible.
Take the most negative $p$ and the largest $q$.
$p = -4$, $q = 5$
$pq = (-4)(5) = -20$
**Answer:** $-20$

**(b)** For the lowest $\frac{3p}{q}$, we want a negative fraction that is as large in size as possible.
Take the most negative $p$ and the smallest $q$ (dividing by a smaller positive number gives a bigger size).
$p = -4$, $q = 2$
$\frac{3p}{q} = \frac{3(-4)}{2} = -6$
**Answer:** $-6$

**(c)** A square is never negative, so the lowest it can be is $0$.

$(2p - q)^2 = (2 - 2)^2 = 0$
**Answer:** $0$

**(d)** $p^2 \geq 0$ and $q > 0$, so $\frac{p^2}{q} \geq 0$.

Then the score is just $q$, so take the largest $q$.
$p = 0$, $q = 5$
$q - \frac{p^2}{q} = 5 - 0 = 5$
**Answer:** $5$

(a) A nature reserve recorded $47\,380$ visitors in 2019. In 2024, it recorded $53\,915$ visitors. Find the percentage increase in the number of visitors from 2019 to 2024. [2]
Answer: $13.8\%$
(b) A shop sells a fruit-and-nut mix in $480$ g bags. Nuts make up $15\%$ of the mass of each bag. The shop owner wants nuts to make up $32\%$ of the mass. Find the mass of nuts that he must add to one bag. [2]
Answer: $120$ g
Worked solution
(a)
Increase in visitors $= 53\,915 - 47\,380 = 6535$
Percentage increase $= \frac{\text{increase}}{\text{original}} \times 100\%$
$= \frac{6535}{47\,380} \times 100\%$
$= 13.792\ldots\%$
**Answer:** $13.8\%$ (3 s.f.)

(b)
Mass of nuts at first $= 15\% \times 480 = 72$ g
Mass of fruit $= 480 - 72 = 408$ g
Adding nuts does not change the mass of fruit.
After adding, fruit makes up $100\% - 32\% = 68\%$ of the new mass.
$68\%$ of new mass $= 408$ g
New mass $= \frac{408}{0.68} = 600$ g
Mass of nuts added $= 600 - 480 = 120$ g
Check: nuts $= 72 + 120 = 192$ g, and $\frac{192}{600} \times 100\% = 32\%$.
**Answer:** $120$ g

Maybe Q33 · Numbers (Ratio) · 3 marks
Why it might be Advanced: Inverse-square brightness ratio after finding distances from offset (hardest part whole)
A lamp stands on a straight bench between two screens, $P$ and $Q$, which face each other and are $3.3$ m apart. The lamp is $90$ cm nearer to screen $P$ than it is to screen $Q$. The brightness on a screen can be calculated using the formula brightness $= \frac{\text{power}}{(\text{distance})^2}$, where power is the power of the lamp in watts and distance is the distance of the screen from the lamp in metres. Find the ratio of the brightness on screen $P$ to the brightness on screen $Q$.
Worked solution
Change the units first: $90$ cm $= 0.9$ m.
Let the distance from the lamp to screen $P$ be $d$ m.
Then the distance from the lamp to screen $Q$ is $(d + 0.9)$ m.
The two distances add up to the gap between the screens:
$d + (d + 0.9) = 3.3$
$2d = 2.4$
$d = 1.2$
Distance to $P = 1.2$ m and distance to $Q = 1.2 + 0.9 = 2.1$ m.
It is the same lamp, so the power is the same for both screens. Let the power be $W$ watts.
Brightness on $P$ : brightness on $Q$ $= \frac{W}{1.2^2} : \frac{W}{2.1^2}$
$= \frac{1}{1.44} : \frac{1}{4.41}$
Multiply both sides by $1.44 \times 4.41$:
$= 4.41 : 1.44$
$= 441 : 144$
Divide both sides by $9$:
$= 49 : 16$
**Answer:** $49 : 16$

Maybe Q34 · Proportion · 4 marks
Why it might be Advanced: Inverse square proportion set up from a difference of tile counts (hardest part whole)
A floor is covered completely with square tiles, all of the same size. The number of tiles needed, $N$, is inversely proportional to the square of the side length of each tile, $s$ cm. Covering the floor with tiles of side 20 cm needs 360 more tiles than covering it with tiles of side 25 cm. Find the number of tiles needed if each tile has a side of 50 cm.
Worked solution
$N = \frac{k}{s^2}$, where $k$ is a constant.
When $s = 20$: $N = \frac{k}{400}$
When $s = 25$: $N = \frac{k}{625}$
Difference: $\frac{k}{400} - \frac{k}{625} = 360$
$\frac{625k - 400k}{250\,000} = 360$
$\frac{225k}{250\,000} = 360$
$k = \frac{360 \times 250\,000}{225} = 400\,000$
$N = \frac{400\,000}{s^2}$
When $s = 50$: $N = \frac{400\,000}{2500} = 160$
**Answer:** 160 tiles

$d = \dfrac{7\sqrt{176}}{15}$
$d = 6.1910\ldots$

Why it might be Advanced: Area of isosceles right triangle from hypotenuse, link to Pythagoras squares (hardest part whole)
A designer builds a logo around a right-angled triangle $KLM$, in which $\angle KLM = 90^{\circ}$.
On each side of triangle $KLM$, outside it, she draws an isosceles right-angled triangle that has that side as its hypotenuse.
These are triangle $KXL$ on $KL$, triangle $LYM$ on $LM$ and triangle $KZM$ on $KM$, with the right angles at $X$, $Y$ and $Z$.
It is given that $KL = p$ cm, $LM = q$ cm and $KM = r$ cm.
Show that area of triangle $KZM$ $=$ area of triangle $KXL$ $+$ area of triangle $LYM$.
Worked solution
Take an isosceles right-angled triangle whose hypotenuse is $d$ cm. Let each of its equal sides be $x$ cm.
By Pythagoras' Theorem, $x^{2} + x^{2} = d^{2}$
$2x^{2} = d^{2}$
$x^{2} = \frac{d^{2}}{2}$
The two equal sides meet at the right angle, so they are the base and the height.
Area $= \frac{1}{2} \times x \times x = \frac{1}{2}x^{2} = \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{d^{2}}{2} = \frac{d^{2}}{4}$
Area of triangle $KXL = \frac{p^{2}}{4}$
Area of triangle $LYM = \frac{q^{2}}{4}$
Area of triangle $KZM = \frac{r^{2}}{4}$
Since $\angle KLM = 90^{\circ}$, by Pythagoras' Theorem $p^{2} + q^{2} = r^{2}$.
Area of triangle $KXL$ $+$ area of triangle $LYM$ $= \frac{p^{2}}{4} + \frac{q^{2}}{4}$
$= \frac{p^{2} + q^{2}}{4}$
$= \frac{r^{2}}{4}$
$=$ area of triangle $KZM$ (shown)
**Answer:** Shown.

The mode is the number of goals with the highest frequency.

Total number of goals $= 0(4) + 1(7) + 2(5) + 3(k) + 4(2) + 5(1) = 30 + 3k$

Maybe Q39 · Trigonometry · 7 marks
Why it might be Advanced: Same-angle tangent without calculator, then similar triangles (hardest part (b))
At a quarry, a straight conveyor belt rises from a point $A$ on level ground. A vertical steel post $BC$ of height 2.4 m holds the belt up, with its foot $B$ on the ground 4.5 m from $A$ and its top $C$ touching the belt, as shown in the diagram.
(a) Find $\angle CAB$, the angle the conveyor belt makes with the ground. [2]
Answer: $\angle CAB = 28.1^\circ$
(b) A second, taller vertical post $DE$ is put up further along the ground, so that $A$, $B$ and $D$ lie in a straight line and the top $E$ of this post also touches the belt. Without using a calculator, write down the value of $\tan \angle EAD$ as a fraction in its simplest form, and give a reason for your answer. [2]
Answer: $\tan \angle EAD = \frac{8}{15}$, because $A$, $C$, $E$ lie on one straight line and $A$, $B$, $D$ lie on one straight line, so $\angle EAD$ is the same angle as $\angle CAB$, and equal angles have equal tangents.
(c) The feet of the two posts, $B$ and $D$, are 3 m apart. Find $DE$, the height of the second post. [3]
Answer: $DE = 4$ m
Worked solution
**(a)**
Triangle $ABC$ is right-angled at $B$.
Opposite $\angle CAB$ is $BC = 2.4$ m, adjacent is $AB = 4.5$ m.
$\tan \angle CAB = \frac{BC}{AB} = \frac{2.4}{4.5}$
$\angle CAB = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{2.4}{4.5}\right)$
$\angle CAB = 28.07^\circ$
**Answer:** $\angle CAB = 28.1^\circ$ (1 d.p.)

**(b)**
$A$, $C$, $E$ lie on the straight belt, and $A$, $B$, $D$ lie on the straight ground.
So $\angle EAD$ and $\angle CAB$ are the same angle at $A$.
Equal angles have equal tangents.
$\tan \angle EAD = \tan \angle CAB = \frac{2.4}{4.5} = \frac{24}{45} = \frac{8}{15}$
**Answer:** $\tan \angle EAD = \frac{8}{15}$, because it is the same angle as $\angle CAB$.

**(c)**
$AD = AB + BD = 4.5 + 3 = 7.5$ m
Triangle $ADE$ is right-angled at $D$.
$\tan \angle EAD = \frac{DE}{AD}$
$\frac{8}{15} = \frac{DE}{7.5}$
$DE = \frac{8}{15} \times 7.5$
$DE = 4$
**Answer:** $DE = 4$ m
