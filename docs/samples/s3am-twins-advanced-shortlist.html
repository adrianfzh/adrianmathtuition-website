Likely Q1 · Trigonometry (R-Formula) · 4 marks
Why it might be Advanced: Reciprocal of R-form denominator: greatest value comes from least denominator, always positive. (hardest part (b))
The expression $8\cos\theta + 15\sin\theta$ is to be written in the form $R\cos\left(\theta - \alpha\right)$, where $R > 0$ and $0^{\circ} < \alpha < 90^{\circ}$.
(a) State the value of $R$ and find the value of $\alpha$. [2]
Answer: $R = 17$, $\alpha = 61.9^{\circ}$
(b) Hence find the greatest value of $\dfrac{51}{8\cos\theta + 15\sin\theta + 20}$ and the value of $\theta$, for $0^{\circ} \leq \theta \leq 360^{\circ}$, at which it occurs. [2]
Answer: Greatest value $= 17$, at $\theta = 241.9^{\circ}$
Worked solution
(a)
$R\cos(\theta - \alpha) = R\cos\theta\cos\alpha + R\sin\theta\sin\alpha$
Compare: $R\cos\alpha = 8$, $R\sin\alpha = 15$
$R = \sqrt{8^2 + 15^2} = \sqrt{289} = 17$
$\tan\alpha = \dfrac{15}{8}$
$\alpha = 61.927...^{\circ} \approx 61.9^{\circ}$
**Answer:** $R = 17$, $\alpha = 61.9^{\circ}$

(b)
$8\cos\theta + 15\sin\theta + 20 = 17\cos(\theta - 61.93^{\circ}) + 20$
Since $-1 \leq \cos(\theta - 61.93^{\circ}) \leq 1$, the denominator lies between $-17 + 20 = 3$ and $17 + 20 = 37$, so it is always positive.
The fraction is greatest when the denominator is smallest.
Smallest denominator $= 3$, when $\cos(\theta - 61.93^{\circ}) = -1$
Greatest value $= \dfrac{51}{3} = 17$
$\theta - 61.93^{\circ} = 180^{\circ}$
$\theta = 241.93^{\circ} \approx 241.9^{\circ}$
**Answer:** greatest value $= 17$, at $\theta = 241.9^{\circ}$

Maybe Q3 · Binomial Theorem · 4 marks
Why it might be Advanced: Claim about coefficient being k times a term independent of x that does not exist. (hardest part whole)
Mei expands $\left(2x^3 - \frac{1}{x}\right)^9$ in descending powers of $x$. She claims that the coefficient of $x^3$ is equal to $k$ times the term independent of $x$, for some constant $k$.

Write down the general term of the expansion and use it to decide whether such a value of $k$ can be found. Justify your answer.
Worked solution
General term of $\left(2x^3 - \frac{1}{x}\right)^9$:
$T_{r+1} = \binom{9}{r}\left(2x^3\right)^{9-r}\left(-\frac{1}{x}\right)^r$
$= \binom{9}{r}\, 2^{9-r}\, (-1)^r\, x^{27-3r}\, x^{-r}$
$= \binom{9}{r}\, 2^{9-r}\, (-1)^r\, x^{27-4r}$

For the term in $x^3$: $27 - 4r = 3$
$4r = 24$
$r = 6$
Coefficient of $x^3$ $= \binom{9}{6}\, 2^{3}\, (-1)^6$
$= 84 \times 8$
$= 672$

For the term independent of $x$: $27 - 4r = 0$
$r = \frac{27}{4} = 6\frac{3}{4}$
$r$ must be a whole number from $0$ to $9$, so no term of the expansion is independent of $x$.
The term independent of $x$ is $0$.

Mei's claim needs $672 = k \times 0$.
$k \times 0 = 0$ for every value of $k$, so this can never equal $672$.

**Answer:** No such $k$ can be found, because the coefficient of $x^3$ is $672$ while the expansion has no term independent of $x$.

Maybe Q4 · Linear Law · 10 marks
Why it might be Advanced: Unusual linear form: x/y against sqrt(x) for y=hx/(sqrt(x)+k). (hardest part whole)
A laboratory technician lowers a dry sponge into a tank of water and records the mass of water, $y$ grams, that it has soaked up after $x$ seconds. It is believed that $x$ and $y$ are connected by the formula $y = \dfrac{hx}{\sqrt{x} + k}$, where $h$ and $k$ are constants. The readings are shown in the table.
$$\begin{array}{|c|c|c|c|c|c|} \hline x & 2 & 5 & 9 & 14 & 20 \\ \hline y & 1.13 & 2.39 & 3.75 & 5.19 & 6.69 \\ \hline \end{array}$$
(a) On graph paper, plot $\dfrac{x}{y}$ against $\sqrt{x}$ and draw a straight line graph. Use a scale of 2 cm to 1 unit on the $\sqrt{x}$-axis and a scale of 4 cm to 1 unit on the $\dfrac{x}{y}$-axis, starting both axes from 0. [3]
Answer: Straight line through $(1.41, 1.77)$, $(2.24, 2.09)$, $(3, 2.40)$, $(3.74, 2.70)$ and $(4.47, 2.99)$
(b) Use your graph to estimate the value of $h$ and of $k$. [4]
Answer: $h = 2.5$ (accept $2.4$ to $2.6$), $k = 3$ (accept $2.8$ to $3.2$)
(c) A denser sponge is tested in the same tank. For $0 < x < 25$, the mass of water it has soaked up after $x$ seconds is $\dfrac{x}{6 - \sqrt{x}}$ grams. By adding a suitable straight line to your graph, find the time at which the two sponges have soaked up the same mass of water. [3]
Answer: $x \approx 11.8$ (accept $11.2$ to $12.3$)
Worked solution
**(a)**
Work out the two quantities to be plotted.
$$\begin{array}{|c|c|c|c|c|c|} \hline \sqrt{x} & 1.41 & 2.24 & 3 & 3.74 & 4.47 \\ \hline \dfrac{x}{y} & 1.77 & 2.09 & 2.40 & 2.70 & 2.99 \\ \hline \end{array}$$
Plot the five points with $\sqrt{x}$ across and $\dfrac{x}{y}$ up.
Rule the best-fit straight line and extend it back to the vertical axis.
**Answer:** a straight line through $(1.41, 1.77)$, $(2.24, 2.09)$, $(3, 2.40)$, $(3.74, 2.70)$ and $(4.47, 2.99)$

**(b)**
Rearrange the formula so that $\dfrac{x}{y}$ is the subject.
$y\left(\sqrt{x} + k\right) = hx$
$\sqrt{x} + k = \dfrac{hx}{y}$
$\dfrac{x}{y} = \dfrac{1}{h}\sqrt{x} + \dfrac{k}{h}$
Compare with $Y = mX + c$: gradient $= \dfrac{1}{h}$, vertical intercept $= \dfrac{k}{h}$.
Take two points on the ruled line, far apart: $(0, 1.2)$ and $(5, 3.2)$.
Gradient $= \dfrac{3.2 - 1.2}{5 - 0} = 0.4$
$\dfrac{1}{h} = 0.4$
$h = 2.5$
The line cuts the vertical axis at $1.2$.
$\dfrac{k}{h} = 1.2$
$k = 1.2 \times 2.5 = 3$
**Answer:** $h = 2.5$, $k = 3$

**(c)**
The masses are equal when $y = \dfrac{x}{6 - \sqrt{x}}$.
Make $\dfrac{x}{y}$ the subject, to match what is plotted.
$\dfrac{x}{y} = 6 - \sqrt{x}$
So draw the line $Y = 6 - X$ on the same axes, for example through $(3, 3)$ and $(5, 1)$.
The two lines cross where $\sqrt{x} \approx 3.43$.
$x \approx 3.43^2 \approx 11.8$
**Answer:** $x \approx 11.8$, so about $11.8$ seconds

Maybe Q5 · Polynomials · 2 marks
Why it might be Advanced: Using remainder theorem on integers to find remainder of 12^75 divided by 13. (hardest part (ii))
It is given that $f(x) = x^{75} + 20$.
(i) Use the remainder theorem to find the remainder when $f(x)$ is divided by $x + 1$. [1]
Answer: $19$
(ii) By choosing a suitable value of $x$, deduce the remainder when $12^{75}$ is divided by $13$. [1]
Answer: $12$
Worked solution
(i) The divisor is $x + 1$, so substitute $x = -1$.
$f(-1) = (-1)^{75} + 20$
$75$ is odd, so $(-1)^{75} = -1$.
$f(-1) = -1 + 20 = 19$
**Answer:** remainder $= 19$

(ii) From (i), $x^{75} + 20 = (x + 1)Q(x) + 19$.
Let $x = 12$, so that $x + 1 = 13$.
$12^{75} + 20 = 13Q(12) + 19$, where $Q(12)$ is an integer.
$12^{75} = 13Q(12) - 1$
A remainder cannot be negative, so take one $13$ out of the multiple:
$12^{75} = 13\left[Q(12) - 1\right] + 12$
**Answer:** remainder $= 12$

Maybe Q6 · Polynomials, Surds · 4 marks
Why it might be Advanced: Spotting surds as cubes so sum-of-cubes factorisation simplifies the fraction. (hardest part whole)
Factorise $8m^3 + n^3$ completely.
Hence, without using a calculator, show that $\frac{16\sqrt{2} + 5\sqrt{5}}{2\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{5}}$ can be written in the form $a + b\sqrt{10}$, where $a$ and $b$ are integers, and state the values of $a$ and $b$.
Worked solution
$8m^3 + n^3 = (2m)^3 + n^3$
$= (2m + n)\left((2m)^2 - (2m)(n) + n^2\right)$
$= (2m + n)(4m^2 - 2mn + n^2)$
Now spot the cubes in the numerator.
$(2\sqrt{2})^3 = 8 \times 2\sqrt{2} = 16\sqrt{2}$, so take $m = \sqrt{2}$: $8m^3 = 16\sqrt{2}$.
$(\sqrt{5})^3 = 5\sqrt{5}$, so take $n = \sqrt{5}$: $n^3 = 5\sqrt{5}$.
The denominator is $2m + n = 2\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{5}$.
$\frac{16\sqrt{2} + 5\sqrt{5}}{2\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{5}} = \frac{8m^3 + n^3}{2m + n}$
$= 4m^2 - 2mn + n^2$
$= 4(\sqrt{2})^2 - 2(\sqrt{2})(\sqrt{5}) + (\sqrt{5})^2$
$= 8 - 2\sqrt{10} + 5$
$= 13 - 2\sqrt{10}$
**Answer:** $a = 13$, $b = -2$
