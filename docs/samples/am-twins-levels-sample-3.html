Advanced Q1 · Nature of Roots · 10 marks
Why Advanced: Denominator is always negative, so the numerator must be negative: sign trap. (hardest part a(ii))
(a) [6]
(i) Find the range of values of $c$ for which the curve $y = c - 4x - 3x^{2}$ lies entirely below the $x$-axis. [3]
Answer: $c < -\dfrac{4}{3}$
(ii) Hence, solve the inequality $\dfrac{(5 - 2x)(x + 1)}{-2 - 4x - 3x^{2}} > 0$. [3]
Answer: $x < -1$ or $x > \dfrac{5}{2}$
(b) [4]
(i) Find the range of values of $q$ for which the equation $x(2x + q) = q - 6$ has two real and distinct roots. [3]
Answer: $q < -12$ or $q > 4$
(ii) Write down the values of $q$ for which the $x$-axis is a tangent to the curve $y = x(2x + q) - q + 6$. [1]
Answer: $q = -12$ or $q = 4$
Worked solution
(a)(i) The curve lies entirely below the $x$-axis, so $c - 4x - 3x^2 < 0$ for all real $x$.
Coefficient of $x^2$ is $-3 < 0$, so the curve opens downwards.
We need it never to meet the $x$-axis: $b^2 - 4ac < 0$.
$(-4)^2 - 4(-3)(c) < 0$
$16 + 12c < 0$
$12c < -16$
**Answer:** $c < -\dfrac{4}{3}$

(a)(ii) The denominator is $-2 - 4x - 3x^2$, which is the curve in (i) with $c = -2$.
Since $-2 < -\dfrac{4}{3}$, from (i) $-2 - 4x - 3x^2 < 0$ for all real $x$.
The fraction is positive and the denominator is always negative, so the numerator must be negative:
$(5 - 2x)(x + 1) < 0$
Multiply by $-1$ and reverse the sign: $(2x - 5)(x + 1) > 0$
Critical values: $x = -1$, $x = \dfrac{5}{2}$
The graph of $(2x - 5)(x + 1)$ opens upwards, so it is positive outside the roots.
**Answer:** $x < -1$ or $x > \dfrac{5}{2}$

(b)(i) $x(2x + q) = q - 6$
$2x^2 + qx - q + 6 = 0$
$a = 2$, $b = q$, $c = 6 - q$
Real and distinct roots: $b^2 - 4ac > 0$
$q^2 - 4(2)(6 - q) > 0$
$q^2 + 8q - 48 > 0$
$(q + 12)(q - 4) > 0$
**Answer:** $q < -12$ or $q > 4$

(b)(ii) The $x$-axis is a tangent when $2x^2 + qx - q + 6 = 0$ has equal roots: $b^2 - 4ac = 0$.
$(q + 12)(q - 4) = 0$
**Answer:** $q = -12$ or $q = 4$

Advanced Q2 · Quadratic Functions · 7 marks
Why Advanced: Same extreme value 5 but at different x, so paths never meet: trap. (hardest part b)
In a computer game, the screen is a coordinate grid. A spaceship travels along the curve $y = 2x^{2} - 12x + 23$ and a missile travels along the curve $y = -3x^{2} - 6x + 2$.
(a) Write $2x^{2} - 12x + 23$ and $-3x^{2} - 6x + 2$ each in the form $a(x - h)^{2} + k$, where $a$, $h$ and $k$ are constants. [4]
Answer: $2(x - 3)^{2} + 5$; $-3(x + 1)^{2} + 5$
(b) Without solving any equation, and using your results from part (a), explain whether the path of the missile ever meets the path of the spaceship. [3]
Answer: The spaceship's path has minimum point $(3, 5)$ and the missile's path has maximum point $(-1, 5)$. Since $2x^{2} - 12x + 23 \geq 5$ and $-3x^{2} - 6x + 2 \leq 5$, the paths could only meet where $y = 5$, but this happens at $x = 3$ on one path and at $x = -1$ on the other. The paths do not meet.
Worked solution
(a)
Take out the coefficient of $x^{2}$ from the $x^{2}$ and $x$ terms only.
$2x^{2} - 12x + 23 = 2(x^{2} - 6x) + 23$
$= 2[(x - 3)^{2} - 9] + 23$
$= 2(x - 3)^{2} - 18 + 23$
$= 2(x - 3)^{2} + 5$

$-3x^{2} - 6x + 2 = -3(x^{2} + 2x) + 2$
$= -3[(x + 1)^{2} - 1] + 2$
$= -3(x + 1)^{2} + 3 + 2$
$= -3(x + 1)^{2} + 5$

**Answer:** $2(x - 3)^{2} + 5$ and $-3(x + 1)^{2} + 5$

(b)
Spaceship: $y = 2(x - 3)^{2} + 5$.
$(x - 3)^{2} \geq 0$, so $y \geq 5$.
The minimum point is $(3, 5)$.

Missile: $y = -3(x + 1)^{2} + 5$.
$-3(x + 1)^{2} \leq 0$, so $y \leq 5$.
The maximum point is $(-1, 5)$.

The only height the two paths could share is $y = 5$.
The spaceship is at $y = 5$ only when $x = 3$.
The missile is at $y = 5$ only when $x = -1$.
These are different points.

**Answer:** The paths do not meet.

Core Q3 · Binomial Theorem · 4 marks
Why Core: Binomial expansion first three terms and substitute p and q. (hardest part whole)
The constants $p$ and $q$ are non-zero. Expand $\left(px^2 + \frac{q}{x}\right)^6$ as far as its third term, starting with the highest power of $x$ and simplifying each term. Use your expansion to find the coefficient of $x^6$ in $\left(2x^2 - \frac{3}{2x}\right)^6$.
Worked solution
$\left(px^2 + \frac{q}{x}\right)^6 = (px^2)^6 + \binom{6}{1}(px^2)^5\left(\frac{q}{x}\right) + \binom{6}{2}(px^2)^4\left(\frac{q}{x}\right)^2 + \ldots$
$= p^6x^{12} + 6\left(p^5x^{10}\right)\left(\frac{q}{x}\right) + 15\left(p^4x^8\right)\left(\frac{q^2}{x^2}\right) + \ldots$
$= p^6x^{12} + 6p^5qx^9 + 15p^4q^2x^6 + \ldots$
The term in $x^6$ is the third term, $15p^4q^2x^6$.
Compare $\left(2x^2 - \frac{3}{2x}\right)^6$ with $\left(px^2 + \frac{q}{x}\right)^6$: $p = 2$, $q = -\frac{3}{2}$.
Coefficient of $x^6$ $= 15(2)^4\left(-\frac{3}{2}\right)^2$
$= 15 \times 16 \times \frac{9}{4}$
$= 540$
**Answer:** $p^6x^{12} + 6p^5qx^9 + 15p^4q^2x^6 + \ldots$; coefficient of $x^6$ is $540$

Core Q4 · Differentiation (Techniques) · 3 marks
Why Core: Product and chain rule, then factorise the derivative. (hardest part whole)
A curve has equation $y = \sqrt{e^{x}}\,\cos^3(2x - 1)$.
Find an expression for $\frac{dy}{dx}$, giving your answer in a fully factorised form.
Worked solution
Rewrite the root first: $\sqrt{e^{x}} = e^{\frac{x}{2}}$, so $y = e^{\frac{x}{2}}\cos^3(2x - 1)$.
Product rule: $\frac{dy}{dx} = \cos^3(2x - 1)\cdot\frac{d}{dx}\left(e^{\frac{x}{2}}\right) + e^{\frac{x}{2}}\cdot\frac{d}{dx}\left[\cos^3(2x - 1)\right]$
$\frac{d}{dx}\left(e^{\frac{x}{2}}\right) = \frac{1}{2}e^{\frac{x}{2}}$
Chain rule: $\frac{d}{dx}\left[\cos^3(2x - 1)\right] = 3\cos^2(2x - 1)\cdot\left(-\sin(2x - 1)\right)\cdot 2 = -6\cos^2(2x - 1)\sin(2x - 1)$
So $\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{1}{2}e^{\frac{x}{2}}\cos^3(2x - 1) - 6e^{\frac{x}{2}}\cos^2(2x - 1)\sin(2x - 1)$
Take out the common factor $\frac{1}{2}e^{\frac{x}{2}}\cos^2(2x - 1)$:
$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{1}{2}e^{\frac{x}{2}}\cos^2(2x - 1)\left[\cos(2x - 1) - 12\sin(2x - 1)\right]$
**Answer:** $\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{1}{2}e^{\frac{x}{2}}\cos^2(2x - 1)\left[\cos(2x - 1) - 12\sin(2x - 1)\right]$

Core Q5 · Indices · 4 marks
Why Core: Write every term as a power of 3 and equate indices. (hardest part whole)
It is given that $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt[3]{27^{y}}} = 81 \times 9^{y}$.
() Find, without using a calculator, the exact value of $27^{y}$. [4]
Answer: $27^{y} = \dfrac{1}{81}$
Worked solution
Write every term as a power of 3.
$\sqrt[3]{27^{y}} = \left(3^{3y}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}} = 3^{y}$
So the left side is $\dfrac{1}{3^{y}} = 3^{-y}$
Right side: $81 \times 9^{y} = 3^{4} \times 3^{2y} = 3^{2y+4}$
So $3^{-y} = 3^{2y+4}$
Same base, so equate the powers:
$-y = 2y + 4$
$-3y = 4$
$y = -\dfrac{4}{3}$
Now bring back what the question asks for:
$27^{y} = 3^{3y} = 3^{3 \times \left(-\frac{4}{3}\right)} = 3^{-4}$
$= \dfrac{1}{81}$
Check: left side $= 3^{\frac{4}{3}}$, right side $= 3^{4} \times 3^{-\frac{8}{3}} = 3^{\frac{4}{3}}$ ✓

Core Q6 · Integration (Definite Integrals) · 4 marks
Why Core: Integrate exponential and cosine of linear terms, exact values. (hardest part whole)
It is given that $\displaystyle\int_0^{\frac{\pi}{6}} \left[6e^{-3x} + 8\cos\left(2x + \frac{\pi}{6}\right)\right]dx = p + qe^{-\frac{\pi}{2}}$, where $p$ and $q$ are integers.
Without using a calculator, find the value of $p$ and of $q$.
Worked solution
Integrate each term. Divide by the coefficient of $x$ each time.
$\displaystyle\int_0^{\frac{\pi}{6}} \left[6e^{-3x} + 8\cos\left(2x + \frac{\pi}{6}\right)\right]dx = \left[\frac{6e^{-3x}}{-3} + \frac{8\sin\left(2x + \frac{\pi}{6}\right)}{2}\right]_0^{\frac{\pi}{6}}$
$= \left[-2e^{-3x} + 4\sin\left(2x + \frac{\pi}{6}\right)\right]_0^{\frac{\pi}{6}}$
Substitute the upper limit, then subtract the lower limit.
$= \left(-2e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}} + 4\sin\frac{\pi}{2}\right) - \left(-2e^{0} + 4\sin\frac{\pi}{6}\right)$
$= \left(-2e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}} + 4\right) - \left(-2 + 2\right)$
$= 4 - 2e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}}$
Compare with $p + qe^{-\frac{\pi}{2}}$.
**Answer:** $p = 4$, $q = -2$

Core Q7 · Logarithms · 4 marks
Why Core: Log laws to turn a magnitude difference into an amplitude ratio. (hardest part a)
Seismologists rate the strength of an earthquake by its magnitude $M$ on the Richter scale, where $M = \lg\frac{A}{A_0}$. Here $A$ is the largest amplitude of the ground wave recorded by a seismograph, and $A_0$ is the amplitude of a standard reference tremor measured at the same distance.
(a) Two earthquakes, P and Q, were recorded at the same station. Earthquake P had a magnitude of 7.2 and earthquake Q had a magnitude of 5.7. Find the ratio of the largest amplitude of earthquake P to that of earthquake Q, leaving your answer in the form $10^k$. [2]
Answer: $10^{1.5}$ (about 31.6)
(b) Engineers consider that an earthquake of magnitude 6.0 or above is likely to cause serious damage to buildings. A seismograph records a tremor with largest amplitude $10^{2.4}$ mm, and the reference amplitude is $10^{-3}$ mm. Explain whether this tremor is likely to cause serious damage to buildings. [2]
Answer: $M = 5.4$. Since $5.4 < 6.0$, the tremor is not likely to cause serious damage to buildings.
Worked solution
(a)
$M_P - M_Q = \lg\frac{A_P}{A_0} - \lg\frac{A_Q}{A_0}$
$= \lg\left(\frac{A_P}{A_0} \times \frac{A_0}{A_Q}\right)$
$= \lg\frac{A_P}{A_Q}$
So $\lg\frac{A_P}{A_Q} = 7.2 - 5.7 = 1.5$
$\frac{A_P}{A_Q} = 10^{1.5}$
**Answer:** $10^{1.5}$ (about 31.6 times)

(b)
$M = \lg\frac{10^{2.4}}{10^{-3}}$
$= \lg 10^{2.4 - (-3)}$
$= \lg 10^{5.4}$
$= 5.4$
Since $5.4 < 6.0$, the tremor is below the damage level.
**Answer:** $M = 5.4$, so the tremor is not likely to cause serious damage to buildings.

Core Q10 · Binomial Theorem · 8 marks
Why Core: General term powers; coefficient from product with a short bracket. (hardest part b)
The expression $\left(x - \frac{2}{x^3}\right)^{10}$ is expanded in descending powers of $x$.
(a) Write down the general term of this expansion and use it to explain why every power of $x$ that appears is even, and why the expansion has no term independent of $x$. [3]
Answer: General term $= \binom{10}{r}(-2)^r x^{10-4r}$. Power of $x = 10 - 4r = 2(5 - 2r)$, a multiple of 2, so every power is even. $10 - 4r = 0$ gives $r = 2.5$, not an integer, so there is no term independent of $x$.
(b) Find the coefficient of $x^2$ in the expansion of $\left(x^2 + \frac{3}{x^2}\right)^2\left(x - \frac{2}{x^3}\right)^{10}$. [5]
Answer: $-60$
Worked solution
**(a)**
General term $= \binom{10}{r} x^{10-r}\left(-\frac{2}{x^3}\right)^r$
$= \binom{10}{r}(-2)^r x^{10-r} x^{-3r}$
$= \binom{10}{r}(-2)^r x^{10-4r}$
Power of $x = 10 - 4r = 2(5 - 2r)$
$r$ is an integer, so $5 - 2r$ is an integer.
So the power of $x$ is a multiple of 2, which means every power is even.
For a term independent of $x$: $10 - 4r = 0$
$r = 2.5$
$r$ must be a whole number, so no such term exists.
**Answer:** Power of $x = 2(5 - 2r)$, always even; $10 - 4r = 0$ gives $r = 2.5$, not an integer, so there is no term independent of $x$.

**(b)**
Expand the first bracket only:
$\left(x^2 + \frac{3}{x^2}\right)^2 = x^4 + 6 + \frac{9}{x^4}$
To make $x^2$, each of these needs a partner from the expansion:
$x^4$ needs $x^{-2}$, $6$ needs $x^2$, $\frac{9}{x^4}$ needs $x^6$.
Term in $x^{-2}$: $10 - 4r = -2$, so $r = 3$
Coefficient $= \binom{10}{3}(-2)^3 = 120 \times (-8) = -960$
Term in $x^2$: $10 - 4r = 2$, so $r = 2$
Coefficient $= \binom{10}{2}(-2)^2 = 45 \times 4 = 180$
Term in $x^6$: $10 - 4r = 6$, so $r = 1$
Coefficient $= \binom{10}{1}(-2) = -20$
Coefficient of $x^2 = (1)(-960) + (6)(180) + (9)(-20)$
$= -960 + 1080 - 180$
$= -60$
**Answer:** $-60$

Core Q12 · Coordinate Geometry · 8 marks
Why Core: Midpoints, gradient and line equation; midpoint theorem ratio and area. (hardest part a)
Solutions to this question by accurate drawing will not be accepted.

A surveyor plots a triangular garden on a coordinate grid, where 1 unit on each axis represents 1 metre. The corners of the garden are $P(-4, 9)$, $Q(-8, -3)$ and $R(10, 3)$, as shown in the diagram. A straight footpath runs from $S$, the point halfway along the fence $PQ$, to $T$, the point halfway along the fence $PR$. The footpath crosses the $y$-axis at $K$.
(a) Find the coordinates of $K$. [4]
Answer: $K(0, 5)$
(b) Find the ratio of the length of the footpath $ST$ to the length of the fence $QR$. [1]
Answer: $1 : 2$
(c) The part of the garden between the footpath and the fence $QR$ is the quadrilateral $QRTS$. Find its area. [1]
Answer: $72$ m$^2$
(d) Explain why the quadrilateral $QRTS$ is a trapezium. [2]
Answer: Gradient of $ST$ = gradient of $QR$ = $\frac{1}{3}$, so $ST \parallel QR$; $QS$ and $RT$ meet at $P$ so they are not parallel. $QRTS$ has exactly one pair of parallel sides.
Worked solution
**(a)**
$S$ is the midpoint of $PQ$.
$S = \left(\frac{-4 + (-8)}{2}, \frac{9 + (-3)}{2}\right) = (-6, 3)$
$T$ is the midpoint of $PR$.
$T = \left(\frac{-4 + 10}{2}, \frac{9 + 3}{2}\right) = (3, 6)$
Gradient of $ST = \frac{6 - 3}{3 - (-6)} = \frac{3}{9} = \frac{1}{3}$
Equation of $ST$: $y - 3 = \frac{1}{3}(x + 6)$
$y = \frac{1}{3}x + 5$
On the $y$-axis, $x = 0$.
$y = 5$
**Answer:** $K(0, 5)$

**(b)**
$ST = \sqrt{(3 + 6)^2 + (6 - 3)^2} = \sqrt{90} = 3\sqrt{10}$
$QR = \sqrt{(10 + 8)^2 + (3 + 3)^2} = \sqrt{360} = 6\sqrt{10}$
$ST : QR = 3\sqrt{10} : 6\sqrt{10}$
**Answer:** $1 : 2$

**(c)**
List the vertices anticlockwise: $Q(-8, -3)$, $R(10, 3)$, $T(3, 6)$, $S(-6, 3)$, then $Q$ again.
Area $= \frac{1}{2}\begin{vmatrix} -8 & 10 & 3 & -6 & -8 \\ -3 & 3 & 6 & 3 & -3 \end{vmatrix}$
$= \frac{1}{2}\left[(-24 + 60 + 9 + 18) - (-30 + 9 - 36 - 24)\right]$
$= \frac{1}{2}\left[63 - (-81)\right]$
$= \frac{1}{2}(144)$
**Answer:** $72$ m$^2$

**(d)**
Gradient of $ST = \frac{1}{3}$ (from part (a)).
Gradient of $QR = \frac{3 - (-3)}{10 - (-8)} = \frac{6}{18} = \frac{1}{3}$
