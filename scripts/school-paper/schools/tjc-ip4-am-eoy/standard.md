# The standard — Temasek Junior College IP4 Additional Mathematics End-of-Year paper

Written from the school's own 2023, 2024 and 2025 papers (`profile.mjs`), 20 Sep 2026.
Adrian's brief for school papers, binding: "must be similar in topics tested, duration,
number of marks. do not just change numbers, must be genuinely different, though similar
in idea. same difficulty or slightly harder". This is the first upper-secondary paper
built by the method; St Nicholas Sec 1 was the first.

## The paper
One paper, 2 hours 30 minutes, 100 marks, 14 questions, about 37 answer parts, 3 to 5
figures, 4 to 7 "show that" parts, 1 or 2 explain parts. Students answer on the question
paper under each question ("[Answers for Question n]"). A graphing calculator is allowed
EXCEPT where a question opens with "The use of a graphing calculator is not allowed for
this question. Show all working clearly." — TJC printed that on three questions in 2025
(the integration technique, the area, the circle). The examiner's grid carries a
"Presentation Deduction −1 / −2" line: clear working is marked. Questions run 5 to 12
marks; the largest is the circle or the linear-law closer. The paper is IP4, so the
formulae page prints the four sum-to-product identities as well as the O-Level list.

## The order — fixed for three years, keep it
Algebra first: polynomials, partial fractions, binomial theorem, logarithms (questions
1–4 in some order). Then trigonometry: one identities-and-equations question, one
R-formula application with a figure. Then permutations and combinations. Then calculus:
rates of change (figure), maximum and minimum (figure), a "differentiate, hence
integrate" pair, an area between curves. Then coordinate geometry of the circle (figure,
the biggest question), vectors, and linear law closes the paper. 2023 also had
kinematics inside the integration question; 2024 and 2025 dropped it — leave it out.

## Scope — what these three papers tested, and nothing outside it
Remainder and factor theorems with a quadratic factor found by comparing coefficients;
partial fractions with a linear, a repeated linear or an irreducible quadratic factor,
improper fractions divided first; the binomial expansion of (ax^p + b/x^q)^n, the general
term, a term that is independent of x or does not exist, a product with a second
bracket; logarithms in bases 2, 3, 4, 9, 10 and e, change of base, equations reducing to
a quadratic in the log, "express y in terms of x"; trigonometric identities including
sec, cosec, cot and the double-angle formulae, equations in degrees or radians over a
stated interval, the R-formula with a geometric figure, maximum and the θ that gives it;
permutations of the letters of a word with vowels together or apart, arrangements in a
row with people at the ends or beside each other, selections; differentiation of
products, quotients, powers, e^x, ln, trig; connected rates of change with a
"show that" for the relation between the variables; stationary values with a
constraint, the nature test; "show d/dx[...] = ..., hence integrate"; definite
integrals; area between a curve and a line or the axes, sometimes with a sideways
parabola x = f(y); the circle: centre and radius from the expanded form, tangent at a
point, tangents from an external point, a second circle, lines meeting a circle;
vectors in two dimensions as columns or as a and b, ratios along a line, collinearity,
parallel vectors, unit vectors; linear law: choose the plot, the meaning of gradient
and intercept, plot on a grid, read the constants, use the graph.
Out of scope: integration by parts or substitution beyond "hence", small-angle
approximations, proof by induction, complex numbers, three-dimensional vectors,
functions and inverse functions, sets, statistics, anything from the H2 syllabus.
A graphing calculator may be assumed for a numerical intersection (2024 Q13(i)) — but
say so in the part, and never let it replace the working the question is testing.

## Where the difficulty sits
- **In the "show that" that sets up the question.** Every calculus figure question and
  the R-formula question begins with a 1- or 2-mark "show that" whose result the rest
  uses; a student who cannot show it may still use it. Keep that structure.
- **In one long chain inside a short question**: 2025 Q1(b) (find h from a quadratic
  factor, 4 marks), Q4(a) (five marks of logs to a quadratic), Q8(iii) (five marks of
  connected rates). The chain is the difficulty, not exotic content.
- **In exact answers**: "leaving your answer in exact form", "in terms of π", surds and
  logs left as they are. The graphing calculator is barred exactly where an exact answer
  is wanted.
- **In a decision at the end**: "determine whether", "explain whether the term exists",
  "do you agree with her?", "suggest one reason why this model may not give accurate
  predictions". One or two per paper, each needing a fact or a number in the reason.
- **In the closer**: the linear-law question asks the student to explain the
  linearisation before plotting, then read the constants off their own line.
"Same or slightly harder" means the same skills asked in an order or from a direction
the class has not rehearsed: the dual of a rehearsed optimisation, a tangent as a region
boundary, a power law where the class saw an exponential. Never harder by going outside
the scope, by uglier arithmetic, by a longer chain than five marks, or by more parts.

## How the school words things — keep these
"Show that …" · "Hence, or otherwise, …" · "Hence find/solve/evaluate …" · "leaving
your answer in exact form" · "correct to 3 significant figures" · "The diagram below
(not drawn to scale) shows …" · "The use of a graphing calculator is not allowed for
this question. Show all working clearly." · "Determine whether … Explain your reasoning
clearly." · "Do you agree with her? State your reasons." · "[Formula for volume of a
cone: ⅓πr²h]" in square brackets in the stem when a mensuration formula is needed ·
"On the grid provided, plot …" · "Using a scale of 1 cm to … on the horizontal axis …".
Parts are (i), (ii), (iii) — or (a), (b) when the parts are unrelated, with (i), (ii)
inside — and every part carries its marks in square brackets. Functions are written
f(x), P(x); constants k, h, a; vectors as columns or bold a, b; the origin is O. Angles
in equations are x or θ; intervals are written −180° ≤ x ≤ 360° or 0 ≤ x ≤ 2π. Settings
for applied questions are plain and physical (a rod, a trough, a ladder, a wire, a
forest, a machine), not brand names.

## What "genuinely different" means here
The real questions on a topic are shown to the author as the SKILL and the LEVEL. A new
question keeps the skill and changes at least two of: the situation, the direction asked
(forwards becomes backwards — given the coefficient find a; given the area find θ), the
given-versus-found quantities, the representation (sine form becomes cosine form; a
table becomes a described experiment), the shape or solid. Changing only the numbers,
names or units is a re-skin and is rejected. Three years of the same R-formula shape
(figure → show a sinθ + b cosθ → R sin(θ + α) → maximum) means the new one must break
that shape somewhere. A student who memorised the 2023–2025 answers must gain nothing.
