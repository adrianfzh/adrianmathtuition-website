# The standard — St Nicholas Girls' Sec 1 End-of-Year paper

Written from the school's own 2024 and 2025 papers (`profile.mjs`), 18 Sep 2026.
Adrian's brief, binding: "must be similar in topics tested, duration, number of marks.
do not just change numbers, must be genuinely different, though similar in idea. same
difficulty or slightly harder".

## The paper
One paper, 2 hours 15 minutes, 90 marks, 20 questions, 7 figures, about 47 answer parts.
Students answer on the question paper. A calculator is allowed except where a part says
"without using a calculator". Most parts carry 2 marks; about a dozen carry 1; eight to
ten carry 3; one or two carry 4. The last question is the longest (10 marks).

## Scope — what these two papers tested, and nothing outside it
This school runs ahead of the national Sec 1 syllabus. In scope: primes, HCF and LCM,
index-form prime factorisation, perfect squares and cubes; real numbers, ordering,
rational and irrational; rounding, significant figures and estimation; ratio, rate,
percentage, reverse percentage, GST at 9%, discount, simple AND compound interest, hire
purchase, income tax and tiered charges read from a table; algebraic expansion (including a squared
bracket), factorisation by common factor and by grouping, algebraic fractions with
numeric denominators, linear equations, simultaneous linear equations, change of subject
(including a square root); angles on parallel lines, triangles, polygons (angle sum,
regular polygons); ruler-and-compass constructions with bisectors and a scale; perimeter
and area of plane figures, Pythagoras' theorem, volume and surface area of prisms,
cylinders, pyramids, cones and spheres (cone and sphere formulas are PRINTED in square
brackets inside the question); statistics: tables, pictograms, dot diagrams, line graphs, histograms,
mean, median, mode, estimated mean of grouped data, misleading graphs.
Out of scope: quadratic equations and factorisation of quadratics, trigonometric ratios,
indices laws beyond positive integer powers, congruence and similarity, coordinate
geometry, graphs of functions, probability, standard form, inequalities, sets.

## Where the difficulty sits
Not in long questions. It sits in short parts that carry a twist:
- a second idea inside a 2-mark part (work backwards, a unit change, "minimum number" so
  round UP, "in terms of $\pi$", "to the nearest dollar");
- parts that forbid the calculator, so the structure must be seen;
- explain, justify, "do you agree" parts — five or six in the paper, each needing a
  number or a named fact in the reason, not an opinion;
- geometry asked as "Stating your reasons clearly, find …";
- algebra joined to mensuration: "Form an algebraic equation in $x$ and solve it";
- one "show that" with the target printed, used by the next part;
- a long closing question on a real object made of two solids, ending in a decision.
"Same or slightly harder" means: the same skills, asked from a direction the girls have
not rehearsed. Never harder by going outside the scope, by ugly arithmetic, or by length.

## How the school words things — keep these
"Stating your reasons clearly, find …" · "Form an algebraic equation in $x$ and solve it
to find …" · "Show your working clearly." · "Without using a calculator, …" · "Explain
why …" · "Do you agree? Justify your answer." · "Leave your answer in terms of $\pi$." ·
"[Volume of a cone $= \frac13\pi r^2h$]" printed in square brackets at the end of the stem.
Parts are labelled (a), (b), (c) with sub-parts (i), (ii); every answer part carries its
marks in square brackets. Settings are Singaporean and concrete (GST 9%, PUB water, MRT,
hawker stall, HDB flat, school CCA), with ordinary Singapore names (Mei Ling, Arjun,
Siti, Mr Tan). Money to the nearest cent unless told otherwise. Units always stated.

## What "genuinely different" means here
The real questions on a topic are shown to the author as the SKILL and the LEVEL. A new
question keeps the skill and changes at least two of: the situation, the direction asked
(forwards becomes backwards), the given-versus-found quantities, the representation
(table becomes graph), the shape or solid. Changing only the numbers, names or units is a
re-skin and is rejected. A student who memorised the 2024 and 2025 answers must gain
nothing from it.

## Set 2 (Adrian, 21 Sep 2026)
"Vary the questions, and concepts tested. Similar difficulty." Set 1 is shown in every
brief. A Set 2 question that tests the same concept from the same direction as its Set 1
slot, or as any Set 1 question, is rejected as too close, even with a new context. Each
slot's content names the Set 1 direction it must NOT repeat. The difficulty is the same
as Set 1: never easier, and no harder than "slightly harder".
