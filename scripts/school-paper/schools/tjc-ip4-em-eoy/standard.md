# The standard — Temasek Junior College IP4 Intermediate Mathematics End-of-Year paper

Written from the school's own 2020, 2023, 2024 and 2025 papers (`profile.mjs`), 20 Sep
2026. TJC calls its IP4 E Math "Intermediate Mathematics"; the bank files it as EM.
Adrian's brief for school papers, binding: "must be similar in topics tested, duration,
number of marks. do not just change numbers, must be genuinely different, though similar
in idea. same difficulty or slightly harder".

## The paper
One paper, 2 hours 30 minutes, 100 marks. The shape moved: 12 questions in 2020 and
2024, 13 in 2023, 16 in 2025. **Follow 2025** — the paper the class sits next — 16
questions of 4 to 8 marks, about 49 answer parts, 9 figures, many 1-mark parts (about
20), 4 or 5 "show that" parts and 4 explain parts. Students answer on the question paper.
A graphing calculator is allowed except where a question says "The use of graphing
calculators is not allowed in this question" (2025 Q3; 2024 Q5 said "solved using a
matrix method and without the use of a graphing calculator"). The examiner's grid carries
a "Presentation Deduction −1 / −2" line.

## The order — follow 2025
Algebra first (indices; simultaneous equations in context; quadratics; inequalities),
graphs of functions, congruence and similarity, matrices, two trigonometry questions
(right-angled in a built setting; bearings with the sine/cosine rule), mensuration,
coordinate geometry, vectors, probability, circular measure, circle properties, and
statistics closes the paper.

## Scope — what these papers tested, and nothing outside it
Indices with negative and fractional powers, surds rationalised, equations in a^x
solved by matching bases, by collecting terms or by substitution; simultaneous linear
equations formed from a context and solved; quadratics by completing the square, the
turning point, sketching with intercepts, exact roots, a quadratic inequality read from
the sketch, a line meeting the curve; linear and quadratic inequalities including a
double inequality; graphs of y = ax^n, y = a/x^n + c and exponentials, identifying from
a sketch and sketching with asymptotes and intercepts; congruence and similarity proofs
"a reason for each statement", area ratios (similar figures, and triangles sharing a
height); matrices: 2×2 and 3×3 systems in context, inverse, a row matrix for a total,
interpreting elements; right-angled trigonometry with angles of elevation and depression
in a built setting; the sine and cosine rules with bearings, the area of a triangle;
mensuration of composite solids (cone, hemisphere, cylinder, pyramid, frustum), volume
and surface area, melt-and-recast, "minimum number so round up"; coordinate geometry:
midpoint, gradient, perpendicular, equation of a line, area by shoelace, rhombus,
rectangle, trapezium, parallelogram, "solutions by accurate drawing will not be
accepted"; vectors as columns and as a, b: ratios, parallel, collinear, "do you agree";
probability with and without replacement, tree diagrams, two-way tables, set notation
A ∩ B, A ∪ B′, (A′ ∩ B′)′, mutually exclusive and independent; circular measure in
radians with sectors, segments, exact perimeters and areas in π and √3, "given the area
find r"; circle properties "stating all reasons clearly" with tangents, diameters, cyclic
quadrilaterals; statistics: misleading charts, histograms, grouped mean and standard
deviation, cumulative frequency, box-and-whisker, stem-and-leaf, comparing two groups.
Occasional in one year only — allowed but not required: speed–time graphs (2024),
polygons (2024), plotting a cubic on a grid with a tangent for the gradient (2023).
Out of scope: calculus, A Math identities and the R-formula, logarithms, functions
notation beyond y = f(x), sets beyond two-event probability, standard form as a topic.

## Where the difficulty sits
- **In the context, not the maths.** A 6-mark question is often two or three routine
  steps inside a story (puff stalls, a bottle of three parts, a rescue drone). The
  difficulty is reading which quantity is which and keeping units straight (cm and m,
  $ and cents).
- **In the reasoning parts**: "Explain your reasoning clearly", "stating all reasons
  clearly", "which model should the company recommend", "explain whether the curve would
  be to the left or right". Four or five per paper; each wants a named fact or a number,
  not an opinion.
- **In the 5- and 6-mark parts**: a composite volume of three solids (2025 Q10(i)), a
  perimeter-then-area in circular measure (2025 Q14(ii)), a nine-mark area of a compound
  tile (2024 Q8(ii)). One long part per paper is enough.
- **In "minimum number" and "least/greatest"**: round UP after a division; the least
  angle of depression is at the greatest altitude.
- **In the proofs**: congruence and similarity need a reason per statement; a missing
  reason loses the mark.
"Same or slightly harder" means the same skill in a setting or direction the class has
not rehearsed: given the area find the angle; the shortest distance to a path; surface
area to paint where the rehearsed question found the volume to fill. Never harder by
going outside the scope, by a fourth solid in the composite, or by uglier numbers.

## How the school words things — keep these
"The diagram below (not drawn to scale) shows …" · "Show that …" · "stating all reasons
clearly" · "Give a reason for each statement you make." · "Solutions to this question
using accurate drawing will not be accepted." · "The use of graphing calculators is not
allowed in this question." · "Find, as a fraction in its simplest form, the probability
that …" · "leaving your answer correct to 3 significant figures" · "Explain your
reasoning clearly." · "Do you agree with her? Justify with clear reasoning." · "Write
down an equation in x and y to represent this information and show that it simplifies
to …" · "Find the minimum number of …" · "Sketch the graph of … on the axes below.
Label clearly the axial intercepts and the equations of the asymptotes."
Parts (i), (ii), (iii), or (a), (b) with (i), (ii) inside; every part carries its marks
in square brackets. Set notation with a prime for the complement. Settings are Singapore
and concrete: hawker stalls, MRT stations, BTO flats, playgrounds, a carpark, a
fund-raiser; ordinary names (Ali, Beng, Jaime, Rebecca, Gregg). Money to the nearest
cent; angles to 1 d.p.; lengths to 3 s.f. unless exact is asked.

## What "genuinely different" means here
The real questions on a topic are shown to the author as the SKILL and the LEVEL. A new
question keeps the skill and changes at least two of: the situation, the direction asked
(given the area find the angle; given the totals find the prices), the given-versus-found
quantities, the representation (a two-way table becomes a Venn diagram; a histogram
becomes a grouped table beside a box plot), the shape or solid. Changing only the
numbers, names or units is a re-skin and is rejected. A student who memorised the
2023–2025 answers must gain nothing from it.
