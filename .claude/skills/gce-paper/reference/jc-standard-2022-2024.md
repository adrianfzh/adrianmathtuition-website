# The 2022–2024 H2 Mathematics standard — what setters must hit

Evidence: every question of GCE 2022, 2023 and 2024 (P1 + P2: 35, 32 and 34 answer units in Paper 1; 38, 39 and 48 in Paper 2) read against 2017–2021 (108 questions) and 2025 (22 questions + the 2025 specimen). Syllabus 9758 was first examined in 2017 (two 3-hour papers of 100 marks; Paper 2 = Section A pure 40 marks + Section B statistics 60 marks); the 9740 papers of 2008–2016 carried Poisson and are never cited. The syllabus was revised for the 2024 JC1 intake (first examined 2025): recurrence sequences in, the method of differences out. Counts come from the bank's question texts (`ref`, `[n]` marks, `topics`).

## 1. Which years are representative, and why 2025 is discounted

Topic-mark profile per year against the 2017–2024 mean (lower = more typical): Paper 1 — 2022 11.7, 2023 12.0, 2021 13.2, 2024 13.5, 2020 14.0, 2019 14.4, 2025 14.6; Paper 2 — 2022 15.2, 2024 15.7, 2020 16.7, 2023 16.9, 2021 18.9, 2025 16.4. Of the last five sittings, **2022 sits closest to the long-run shape in both papers; 2024 is the next most typical; 2023 carries the heaviest reasoning load** (10 show/prove units in Paper 1, the 15-mark algebraic binomial question P2 Q10). 2021 is the odd one out (Paper 2 gave probability 4 marks and integration 34) and 2020, the COVID sitting, leaned on differentiation (32 marks across the two papers). So: 2022 is the anchor when the three disagree; 2023 sets the ceiling for reasoning; 2024 sets the shape of Section B's explain-in-context parts.

2025 reads easier than all three, and the counts agree with Adrian: Paper 1 has 3 show/prove units (5, 10 and 8 in 2022–2024) and no unparted question; Paper 2 has NO part above 4 marks (largest 5, 6 and 6), 2 show/prove units and 4 explain units against 8, 4 and 11 — its 17-mark closer (Q10) is eight short parts, each a calculator read or a one-line reason. Its questions are still the only real examples of the revised syllabus (GCE 2025 P1 Q1, a recurrence $u_{r+1} = 3u_r + 5$ summed to find $a$; GCE 2025 P1 Q3, modulus and argument in cartesian form with no polar form to fall back on), so they are read for SCOPE and cited for that alone.

## 2. What a 2022–2024 paper does

1. **Parts build, and the printed line is a gate.** GCE 2022 P1 Q4: "(a) Show that $\frac{d}{dx}(\cot x) = -\operatorname{cosec}^2 x$ [2] (b) Show that $\sin 2x \tan x = 2\sin^2 x$ [1] (c) Hence, find the exact value of $\int_{\pi/18}^{\pi/9} \operatorname{cosec} 6x \cot 3x\,dx$ [4]" — two printed facts, then a 4-mark part in which the candidate must SEE that $\operatorname{cosec} 6x \cot 3x$ is $\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{cosec}^2 3x$ before the printed derivative applies. GCE 2024 P1 Q8: a differential relation shown in (a) [3], differentiated again for the Maclaurin series in (b) [4], matched to a binomial expansion "using standard series from the List of Formulae (MF26)" in (c) [2]. GCE 2023 P1 Q4: a general result in $n$ shown in (b), used twice in (c) and (d) with a modulus in (d).
2. **The model is built from words, and the constant is found from the data.** GCE 2022 P1 Q12(a) [5]: "the rate of change of the population is proportional to the population. Write down a differential equation for this model and solve it" — with "Initially … 50 and after 10 days … 100" the candidate finds both constants; (b) [6] is the logistic refinement $\frac{dP}{dt} = \lambda P(500 - P)$ solved by partial fractions; (c) [2] the long-term population and WHY that makes the model better. GCE 2023 P1 Q10(a) [1]: "Show that $\frac{dM}{dt} = k(C - 30M)$" from three sentences; (c) [4] solve to the printed $M = 88 + 22e^{-30kt}$; (d) [4] the extra days; (e) [3] sketch and "Explain why Andrew cannot achieve a mass of 80 kg". GCE 2024 P1 Q11: a second-order equation reduced to first order in $v$, solved to $v = A(1 - e^{Bt})$ [5], then the time to fall 2000 m [5] — an integral of the solution, not a read-off.
3. **Vectors are a 3-D situation with a decision at the end.** GCE 2022 P1 Q11 (13): $p$ from a length with "the level of $P$ is below that of $Q$" choosing the root [3]; a plane through three points [4]; where the line meets it [4]; the angle with the horizontal [2]. GCE 2024 P1 Q12 (15): the closest point of a path to the tower [4], "Hence determine whether the air traffic controllers will see this aircraft" [2], the values of $k$ for which two paths do NOT intersect [3], and (d)(ii) [2]: "By considering the vector between the aircraft and the drone at this instant, determine whether the distance found … is the shortest distance between the two paths" — a perpendicularity test the candidate must think of. GCE 2023 P1 Q9: $a$ and $B$ from two lines that cross [5], then a plane perpendicular to one line and containing a point.
4. **Section A of Paper 2 mixes one short technique with one long structured question.** 2022: a given substitution [4]; a container filled at rate $kt$ then $kt + 25$ [7]; complex numbers [9]; series [9]; the spherical cap [11] — "Show by integration that the volume … is $\frac{1}{3}\pi h^2(3r - h)$" [5], then a cubic in $p$ from an ornament with two caps removed [4], then a second ornament by reasoning alone [2]. 2023: inequalities [6] (modulus, then "Express … as a single simplified fraction. Hence, without using a calculator, solve exactly"); $\ln(\sec x)$ Maclaurin via $y''' = 2y''y'$ [9]; vectors ending in the centre of a circle through three points [12]; parametric curves meeting, "Show that there are no other points of intersection" [13]. 2024: complex numbers with no calculator [6]; a flower-bed area maximised, "showing that it is a maximum value" [6]; functions [8]; $S_n$ to $t_n$ and "Explain why all the terms in series W are odd numbers" [9]; graph transformations on printed figures [11].
5. **Section B asks for reasons in context, in small doses, all through the section.** 2024 P2 has 11 explain units: "Explain whether the 65 members comprise a sample or a population" [1], "Explain an advantage of choosing a random sample" [1], "Explain whether Lee should carry out a one-tailed test or a two-tailed test" [1], "State hypotheses … defining any parameters" [2], "determine the minimum possible value of the integer $\alpha$" [3] (a p-value read as a significance level), "Explain whether using the number of birds for each of 10 mornings would have been sufficient" [1], "State, in context, two assumptions needed for $Y$ to be well modelled by a binomial distribution" [2], "Explain why Kai should use the sum of the squares of the residuals rather than the sum of the residuals" (2024 P2 Q11(b)(ii)(B)). 2022 P2 Q10(e) [3]: "Explain why these distances are squared, and why this is referred to as the 'method of least squares'." 2023 P2 Q8(a)(ii) [2]: "Give two reasons why the production manager's sample is not suitable for a $z$-test."
6. **The statistics closer is a modelled situation with an algebraic or reasoning part.** GCE 2023 P2 Q10 [15]: binomial assumptions in context, "Show that the numerical values of the mean and variance … differ by 0.08", a binomial of a binomial (days in a week), the total over a week, then (b) [6] "Show algebraically that Mrs Ming accepts a greater proportion of boxes than Mr Lu does" — two acceptance schemes compared as polynomials in $p$. GCE 2022 P2 Q11 [13]: two swimmers' normal times, "Find the probability that Zhou wins" (a difference of normals), unbiased estimates from $\sum x, \sum x^2$, a one-tail test with hypotheses defined and a conclusion in context, "Explain whether Tan should use a 1-tail or a 2-tail test", "State these two assumptions". GCE 2024 P2 Q10 [12]: plates drilled (a 5% reduction scales the normal), "at least 5 of these 8 plates have masses between 190 and 192 grams" (a binomial on a normal probability), a pack of 20, then a Value pack of plates + screws + bolts and "the mass exceeded by just 5%". GCE 2022 P2 Q9 [10]: a counter on a board, "Show that the probability the counter arrives at B is $2p^3q + \frac{1}{2}p^4$", the probability two counters followed the same route in terms of $p$ [4], $p$ from P(B) = P(C) [4].
7. **Regression is a modelling choice, not a formula.** 2022 P2 Q10 [14]: an outlier removed for a stated reason, a reciprocal transformation $p$ against $\frac{100\,000}{d}$, the line by eye, then by calculator, an estimate and its reliability. 2023 P2 Q7 [7]: $r$ for $y$ on $x$ and for $e^y$ on $x$, "Explain which of Ling's models … gives a better fit", the 2024 estimate "Explain whether your estimate is reliable". 2024 P2 Q11 [15]: $p = a + b\ln m$ against $p = d + e\sqrt{m}$, then residuals marked, squared, summed, and "the range of values of $c$ for which the line $y = \frac{5}{2}n + c$ is a better fit" [3] — a quadratic in $c$ from the residual sum.
8. **Calculator discipline is explicit and tested both ways.** "Do not use a calculator in answering this question" opens GCE 2022 P1 Q1, GCE 2023 P1 Q8, GCE 2024 P2 Q1 (complex numbers) and GCE 2024 P1 Q4(a) (an inequality solved by a sign test, then $x \to |x|$ for [2]). "Use calculus", "Using calculus", "Show by integration" and "find the exact" mark the parts where a calculator read earns nothing (GCE 2024 P1 Q6(a), Q9(c); GCE 2023 P2 Q1(b); GCE 2022 P1 Q7(b) "Find the exact area"). Elsewhere the value is read: "Find the volume … correct to 2 decimal places" (GCE 2024 P1 Q10(b)(iii)), "Give your answer correct to 1 decimal place" (GCE 2024 P1 Q6(b)(ii)), every probability in Section B.
9. **Familiar topic, unfamiliar last part.** GCE 2022 P1 Q6(d) [2]: $\mathrm{f}^{2023}(1)$ in terms of $a$ and $k$ after $\mathrm{f}^2$ turns out to be the identity. GCE 2022 P2 Q3(c) [4]: the smallest $n$ for which $z_3^n$ is purely imaginary (polar form — now excluded — but the DECISION, a condition on an argument, survives in cartesian questions such as GCE 2023 P1 Q8(a)(ii)). GCE 2024 P1 Q5(c) [3]: $k$ from $80\sum_{r=k+1}^{\infty} u_r = \sum_{r=1}^{k} u_r$ with $r < 0$. GCE 2024 P1 Q7(c) [3]: three possible values of a sum because $\sin\theta$ takes three values. GCE 2023 P2 Q4(b) [5]: "Find a point of intersection … Show that there are no other points of intersection." GCE 2024 P2 Q7(a)(iii) [3]: the range of $n$ for which C's probability is more than 8 times A-or-B's.

**Did not change across 2017–2024 (and holds in 2025):** the roster — every Paper 1 carries complex numbers, vectors, a differential equation, an AP/GP question, a functions-or-graphs question, an integration-technique question and a parametric-or-tangent question; every Section B carries a probability or counting starter, a binomial question, a normal question (usually with sums and differences of independent normals), a hypothesis test with unbiased estimates from summarised data, and a regression question with a transformation and a reliability judgement. Paper 1 has 11–12 questions climbing from 4–5 to 12–15 marks; Paper 2 has 10–11, Section A of four (occasionally five) questions of 4–13 marks, Section B of six of 5–17 marks. "Hence" links 4–6 units a paper. Unparted questions are rare (0–2 a paper; GCE 2023 P1 Q1, GCE 2024 P2 Q2, GCE 2022 P2 Q1).

## 3. Paper 1 at the 2022–2024 standard

These bands describe the parts that CARRY the standard; ordinary parts beside them (GCE 2024 P1 Q3(a) "State a condition on $a$, $b$ and $c$" [1]; GCE 2023 P1 Q7(b) "Hence state the range" [1]) are acceptable inside a question whose idea sits elsewhere.

- **1–2 marks** — a consequence read exactly, or a fact deduced. GCE 2022 P1 Q6(c) [1]: $\mathrm{f}^2(x)$ from $\mathrm{f}^{-1}$ ("Hence, or otherwise"). GCE 2024 P1 Q6(b)(i) [1]: the exact $a$ from "the tangent passes through the origin". GCE 2022 P1 Q12(c) [2]: the limit and the sentence that explains why it improves the model.
- **3–4 marks** — one routine with one decision. GCE 2024 P1 Q4(b) [2] + (a) [4]: the sign test, then the $|x|$ form read off it. GCE 2022 P1 Q3(a) [3]: the gradient of the NORMAL at $t = \frac{1}{3}\ln 2$ (negative reciprocal after parametric differentiation). GCE 2023 P1 Q3(a) [3]: $|\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}| = 1$ from a perpendicularity condition on two composite vectors. GCE 2024 P1 Q9(b) [3]: the point where the tangent makes $60^\circ$ with the $x$-axis — $kt = \sqrt3$, then exact coordinates.
- **5–6 marks** — a technique with a condition to manage, or a relation set up first. GCE 2022 P1 Q7(b) [5]: the exact area under $x^{-3}\ln x$ from 1 to 3 by parts with the limits' logarithms combined. GCE 2024 P1 Q10(b)(ii) [5]: the area in the form $a + b\ln c$ after the printed substitution, with $\frac{2u^2}{u^2 - 9} = 2 + \frac{18}{u^2 - 9}$ seen and the standard form applied. GCE 2022 P1 Q5(a) [4]: $m$ satisfies $3m^2 + 56m + 36 = 0$ from a tangent condition (discriminant zero), then (b) [4] the points of contact from the two $m$. GCE 2024 P1 Q6(a) [5]: a tangent in the form $y = e(px + q) + ra$ with the constant $a$ carried.
- **7 or more in one part** — rare, and always a chain with a recognisable first step. GCE 2022 P1 Q12(b) [6]: the logistic equation by partial fractions to $P$ in terms of $t$. GCE 2024 P1 Q11(b) [5] and (c) [5]: solve for $v$, then integrate $v$ to a distance and solve for $t$ numerically.
- **Closers (12–15 marks)** — a situation in 4–6 parts whose last part is a judgement: GCE 2024 P1 Q12(d)(ii), GCE 2022 P1 Q12(c), GCE 2023 P1 Q10(e)(i), GCE 2023 P1 Q11(d)(ii) ("Hence find the total saving").

## 4. Paper 2 at the 2022–2024 standard

**Section A (40 marks, questions 1–4, occasionally 5).** One short technique question (a given substitution [4], GCE 2022 P2 Q1; a modulus inequality then an exact rational inequality [6], GCE 2023 P2 Q1; complex numbers without a calculator [6], GCE 2024 P2 Q1), one or two mid-length questions (a rate model in a container [7]; a Maclaurin series from a differential relation with a numerical use [9]; functions [8]; sequences [9]; a maximised area [6]) and one long question of 11–13 marks (the spherical cap; vectors to a circumcentre; two parametric curves; five printed graphs to transform). Every Section A closer has a part the candidate must think of: GCE 2022 P2 Q5(c) [2] (a second ornament by comparing radii, no integration), GCE 2023 P2 Q3(d) [5] (the centre equidistant from three points as two scalar-product conditions), GCE 2023 P2 Q4(c) [5] (a tangent to one curve meeting the other curve again).

**Section B (60 marks).**
- **The starter (5–6 marks)** is probability or counting with one idea: GCE 2022 P2 Q6 (a game of turns → a geometric series, then a conditional probability "Given that Babs wins"); GCE 2023 P2 Q5 (independence tested on 36 slots, then re-tested on 35 — "Determine whether $A$ and $C$ are now independent"); GCE 2024 P2 Q6 (sample or population [1], an advantage of random sampling [1], a committee count with three conditions turned into a probability [4]).
- **Counting (5–8 marks)** has a restriction that needs cases: GCE 2022 P2 Q7(c) [5] ("at least one from each department AND more from Administration than from any other single department"); GCE 2023 P2 Q6 (two probability ratios → two equations in $r$ and $b$ → the probability of exactly one red).
- **Binomial (9–15 marks)**: assumptions in context [2]; a binomial of a binomial (days in a week); a total over several days; an expectation of a count; one algebraic or comparative part (GCE 2023 P2 Q10(b) [6]; GCE 2024 P2 Q9(b) [2], $p$ estimated from a frequency table).
- **Normal (8–14 marks)**: sums and differences of independent normals with the parameters stated; a scaling by a percentage; "the mass exceeded by just 5%"; a sketch with features (GCE 2022 P2 Q8(b)); a "Without doing any detailed calculation, explain how your answer would change" [1] (GCE 2023 P2 Q9(f)).
- **Hypothesis test (8–13 marks)**: one-tail or two-tail with the reason; hypotheses with the parameter defined; unbiased estimates from $\sum x, \sum x^2$ or $\sum (x - a), \sum (x - a)^2$; the test at a stated level with the conclusion in context; a reversed part (the smallest significance level at which $\mathrm{H}_0$ is rejected, GCE 2024 P2 Q8(c); the critical region, GCE 2023 P2 Q8(b)(i) [5]); assumptions and the Central Limit Theorem in words.
- **Regression (7–15 marks)**: a scatter diagram read; two candidate models compared by $r$; the regression line for the better model; an estimate with its reliability (inside or outside the data, $|r|$); a residuals part in 2024.

## 5. The setter's checklist

**List A — every question; the slot author and the slot moderator check these.** An item that starts "If …" is skipped when the condition does not hold.

1. **The move.** The question's MAIN step is one of the moves in §8, and it is not the textbook route of its topic (§9). A question whose every part is the standard route of the topic — however clean the numbers, however good the register — is BELOW standard. The move is named in `skills`, and the candidate has to FIND it: the stem never names it.
2. **Chaining is the standard.** A part may chain two or three ideas when they form one line of thought and the first step can be found from the syllabus and the given information (GCE 2022 P1 Q4(c), GCE 2023 P2 Q4(c), GCE 2024 P1 Q12(d)(ii)). A part is "above" only when its first step is a trick that cannot be found, or a result outside 9758 must be discovered, or the algebra outlasts the time (§6).
3. **The stem is short.** At most three sentences before the first part, unless the situation carries the maths — and then the situation states only what the maths needs (GCE 2023 P1 Q3 is two sentences; GCE 2024 P1 Q12's story is four sentences and every one is used). The thinking lives in the mathematics, not in the story; a paragraph of context that a candidate can skip is a fault.
4. If the situation gives the relation, the candidate BUILDS it; a formula is printed only where the syllabus could not expect it to be produced.
5. Every "Show that" is a gate for a later part, a proof whose last mark is a reason, or a general result later re-used with a parameter changed (GCE 2023 P1 Q4(b)→(c),(d)); never the whole of the next part's thinking.
6. At least one part asks for words the mark scheme can pay — explain why, determine whether, show that there is no / only one, state in context — and in Section B at least two such parts per question of 8 marks or more.
7. Numbers engineered to close exactly; a general constant carried where the move calls for one (in terms of $n$, $p$, $a$, $\theta$).
8. Finishable at about 1.8 minutes a mark, a 13-mark question in twenty-five minutes.

**List B — the whole paper; only the session checks these, when it chooses the ideas (§10) and again at the read-through; a miss is repaired at assembly or by re-briefing ONE slot:**

9. Every §8 move used at most twice in a paper, and the two papers of a Set between them use at least seven different moves.
10. The must-appear topics of the blueprint land somewhere in the paper (a topic may land as the second topic of a fused question); the roster of §2's "Did not change" holds across the Set.
11. The closers — Paper 1's last two questions, and the last question of each section of Paper 2 — carry the paper's hardest moves; the opener of a paper may be its one routine-shaped question, at most 5 marks, and still carries a decision.
12. Shape counts (from `paper-shape-report.json`): answer units 32–38 in Paper 1 and 38–48 in Paper 2; parts of 2 marks or fewer 10–19 and 20–35; show/prove units 5–10 and 1–5; explain units in Section B 4–11; unparted questions 0–2; largest single part 5–6; at most one calculator-ban question a paper; Section A exactly 40 marks.
13. Nothing outside the revised 9758: no method of differences, polar or exponential form, Poisson, induction, reduction formulae, skew-line distance, t-test, normal approximation to the binomial, Type I/II error.

## 6. The moderator's test

The verdict carries two extra keys beside the usual ones: `"template"` — the textbook question the slot follows, named in a few words, or null — and `"move"` — the §8 move its main step uses, by number and name, or null.

**Below standard** — score at most 3, with fixes that name the move to use: (i) the whole question is a template from §9, the standard route of its topic with new numbers, whatever its polish; (ii) the marks can be earned by executing named procedures in the order the stem lists them; (iii) the "twist" is only a root rejected at the end of an otherwise routine solution, or a boundary read exactly; (iv) the story adds words but no decision — strip it and the question is unchanged; (v) no §8 move can be named. A question that IMPLEMENTS its `Q<n>.idea.md` badly (the move is there but the candidate is walked to it, or the interesting step is printed as a "Show that") is below.

**At standard** — the main step is a §8 move the candidate must find, inside 9758, at the marks 2022–2024 paid for the work; the other parts may be routine leads or consequences; two or three chained ideas in one part are AT standard when the chain is one line of thought. A question of 12 marks or more holds two moves or one move used twice from different directions.

**Above standard** — only: a result outside 9758 must be discovered rather than applied; the first step cannot be found from the syllabus and the given information (a trick); or the algebra outlasts the time the marks buy. Never "above" merely for chaining ideas or for being unfamiliar — unfamiliar is the point.

**Routine** — a whole routine question is allowed ONCE a paper, as the opener, at most 5 marks, and it still carries a decision (GCE 2022 P2 Q1's substitution, GCE 2024 P1 Q2's vector fact). The moderator writes `"standard": "routine"` for it; a second routine slot is below.

**Marks match the work** — a standard routine is paid, within one mark, what 2022–2024 paid it (a given-substitution integral [4]; a foot of perpendicular [4]; unbiased estimates [2]; a full $z$-test [5]; hypotheses defined [2]; a regression line with $r$ [3]; two assumptions in context [2]; a Maclaurin series to $x^3$ from a relation [4–5]). The repair is to MOVE MARKS; the moderator writes "re-mark" in its fixes.

**Not a re-skin** — the novelty gate compares words; the moderator compares STRUCTURE: the same object, the same sequence of parts and the same move as a real 2017–2025 question or an earlier-Set question is `too_close_to` / `repeats_set` even with every number changed.

## 8. The moves — what makes a 2022–2024 question smart

Every question's MAIN step is one of these. The examples are real (the corpus has their full text); read three of them before writing.

1. **Define, then reason.** A rule, object or game is defined in the question and the candidate reasons with it. GCE 2024 P2 Q11(b)(ii)–(iv): the residual is defined, the sum of squares computed, then "the range of values of $c$ for which the line $y = \frac52 n + c$ is a better fit". GCE 2024 P2 Q4: four series $T, U, V, W$ defined by their terms, "the smallest number greater than 100 that is in both", "explain why all the terms in $W$ are odd". GCE 2025 P2 Q1: token points from three players' scores, then "Sam came second" fixes his tokens. GCE 2022 P2 Q9: a counter moving on a board with probabilities $p$ and $q$.
2. **Reverse the direction.** The usual output is given and the usual input asked for. GCE 2025 P1 Q3(b): the rotation is given, the complex number that represents $B$ is asked for "in terms of $z$, explaining your answer". GCE 2023 P2 Q6: two ratios of probabilities are given, the numbers of counters are found ($9r + 5 = 4b$). GCE 2024 P1 Q1: an asymptote and a turning point are given, the three constants of the curve are found. GCE 2024 P2 Q8(c): the conclusion is given, the minimum significance level is found. GCE 2025 specimen P2 Q10(d): "given that the Finance Manager concludes there is no reason to reject … find the range of possible values of the population variance".
3. **A general parameter carried to the end.** GCE 2023 P1 Q4: $\int x\cos nx\,dx$ for all positive integers $n$, then $\int_0^{\pi}$ as $\frac{k}{n^2}$ "where the possible value(s) of $k$ are to be determined". GCE 2024 P1 Q7: from $\mathrm{f}(2r-1) - \mathrm{f}(2r+1)$ with $\mathrm{f}(r) = \cos r\theta$ to a sum in $\theta$ and $n$, then "the three possible values" of a numerical sum. GCE 2022 P1 Q6: $\mathrm{f}^{2023}(1)$ in terms of $a$ and $k$. GCE 2022 P1 Q9(b): a GP with first term $\sin\theta$ and ratio $-\cos\theta$ summing to $\tan k\theta$. GCE 2025 P1 Q9: the Maclaurin series of $\sin^{-1}px$ in terms of $p$, then matched to $\frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - 9x^2}}$.
4. **Structural or discrete reasoning.** Parity, divisibility, periodicity, counting solutions, what a domain excludes. GCE 2024 P2 Q4(d): $3n^2 - 5n + 7$ is always odd, hence no common term with $50n - 204$. GCE 2023 P2 Q5: independence tested on 36 slots, then re-tested when a ball is stuck and only 35 remain. GCE 2024 P1 Q7(c): a sum takes three values because $\sin\theta$ does. Set 1 P1 Q1: a period-2 recurrence whose fixed points also satisfy $u_3 = u_1$ and must be rejected; Set 1 P1 Q3: a function constant on each of two intervals, so its range has two elements.
5. **Show that there is none, or only one, or which.** GCE 2023 P2 Q4(b): "Find a point of intersection … Show that there are no other points of intersection." GCE 2024 P1 Q12(d)(ii): "determine whether the distance found is the shortest distance between the two paths". GCE 2024 P2 Q4(d)(ii). Set 1 P2 Q3(c): "Explain why $\mathrm{f}(x) = \mathrm{f}^{-1}(x)$ has no solution." Set 1 P1 Q11(e): whether a shadow lands inside a rectangle.
6. **The situation changes the maths.** A condition in the words removes a root, fixes a case or forces an integer. GCE 2022 P1 Q11(a): "the level of $P$ is below that of $Q$" picks the root. GCE 2024 P1 Q2: $|\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}| = 3$, $\mathbf{a}$ a unit vector, $\mathbf{b}\cdot\mathbf{b} = 9$, show perpendicular. 12. **Geometry seen, not computed.** GCE 2025 P1 Q11(b): the points where the tangent is parallel to the $y$-axis give the greatest width of a loop. The main step of a question is ONE of these, found by the candidate. A question may use two. The moves are not decorations to add to a template: the question is designed FROM the move, and the topic follows.

## 9. The templates — below standard as whole questions

Each of these is the textbook route of its topic. A paper may use one as a LEAD-IN part; a question that is one of these from start to finish, with new numbers, is below standard (§6(i)) however clean. The first draft of Set 1 (26 Sep 2026) was made almost entirely of them, which is what Adrian rejected.

- Sequences: an AP or GP with two given sums → $a$ and $d$ or $r$ → "the smallest $n$ such that"; a proof that $S_{m+n} = 0$ when $S_m = S_n$.
- Functions: range → domain restriction for $\mathrm{f}^{-1}$ → $\mathrm{f}^{-1}(x)$ → does $\mathrm{fg}$ exist → solve $\mathrm{f}(x) = \mathrm{f}^{-1}(x)$ on $y = x$.
- Graphs: a rational curve → asymptotes → sketch → an inequality read from it; a list of transformations described in order.
- Inequalities: a modulus inequality, then the same inequality with $x \to \mathrm{e}^x$, $\ln x$ or $|x|$.
- Complex numbers: one complex root → conjugate → the real coefficients → the other roots; simultaneous equations in $z$ and $w$ by elimination; "Do not use a calculator" on a modulus and argument.
- Differentiation: implicit differentiation → stationary points → nature by the second derivative; a parametric curve → tangent at $t$ → where it meets the curve again; a named solid optimised (a cylinder in a sphere, a sphere in a cone, an open box); connected rates in a cone, cylinder or bowl filling at a constant rate.
- Maclaurin: a differential relation shown → differentiate again → the series → an approximation or a match to a standard series.
- Integration: a GIVEN substitution shown → an exact area → a volume of revolution; integration by parts twice; partial fractions then integrate.
- Differential equations: exponential, logistic or Newton-cooling model → separate → constants from data → long-term value → sketch.
- Vectors: two lines and a plane → intersection, foot of perpendicular, angle, distance, hung on a story (an aircraft, a pipeline, a roof).
- Counting and probability: people at round tables or in a line with restrictions; committees with conditions; a tree with an unknown $p$ → conditional probability → an independence check.
- Binomial: overbooking or quality control → two assumptions → probabilities from the calculator → the largest $n$ by tabulation.
- Normal: two machines or two products → sums and differences of independent normals → a percentage scaling → the value exceeded by 5%.
- Hypothesis testing: unbiased estimates from $\sum(x - a)$ → hypotheses with the parameter defined → one-tail or two-tail with the reason → the test → the conclusion in context.
- Regression: a table → a scatter diagram → two models compared by $r$ → the line for the better one → an estimate and its reliability.

## 10. The ideas step (27 Sep 2026)

Since the first draft, an H2 slot is not written from its topic. Before authoring, two ideas agents (one Fable, one Opus) each propose THREE candidate ideas per open slot, each built from a §8 move, with the topics that follow, the parts and marks, the step the candidate must find and a sketch of the answers; the session chooses one idea per slot — the most non-standard that stays inside 9758, keeps every move to at most twice a paper and lets the must-appear topics land — and writes it to `Q<n>.idea.md`. The author implements THAT idea; the moderator checks the move is present and names the template if the question fell back into one. The `gce-paper` skill's §H2 has the commands.
