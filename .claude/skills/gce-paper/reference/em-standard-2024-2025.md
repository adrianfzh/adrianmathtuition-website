# The 2024/2025 E Math standard — what setters must hit

Evidence: every question of GCE 2024 P1/P2 and GCE 2025 P1/P2 (72 questions) read against the whole of GCE 2019–2022 (P1 + P2) and GCE 2023. Counts come from the corpus (`ref`, `[n]` marks, `topics`).

## 1. What changed

1. **Paper 1 withholds the ladder.** P1 questions with no (a)/(b) parts: 8 of 25 (2019), 10 of 24 (2021), 12 of 25 (2022), 13 of 27 (2024), 14 of 27 (2025); parts per question fell from 1.92 to 1.2. GCE 2025 P1 Q22: "After 100 seconds $B$ has travelled 75 m further than $A$ … Find the value of $v$." — no parts. The same two-cyclist scenario in GCE 2021 P1 Q24 was cut into [1], [1], [4] with the expression for B's distance asked for first.

2. **Paper-2-grade items now sit in Paper 1.** GCE 2025 P1 Q23, "Solve the equation $\frac{2x}{x + 4} + \frac{5}{3x - 5} = 3$ … correct to 2 decimal places" was a 5-mark P2 part in GCE 2021 P2 Q1(c). GCE 2025 P1 Q27(b) (two spinners, two unknown probabilities, [3]) is GCE 2021 P2 Q9(b) [4]. GCE 2025 P1 Q21(b) (cone 65% full, depth 78 cm, find $h$ [3]) is GCE 2020 P2 Q5(b) without its "Explain why he is wrong [1]" lead-in.

3. **The equation is hidden; nobody says "form an equation".** GCE 2025 P1 Q20(b): "The perimeter of the larger square is 124 cm. The perimeter of the smaller square is 100 cm. Find the length of $AP$" [3] — a quadratic, $AP^2 + (31-AP)^2 = 625$, inside a congruence question. GCE 2025 P1 Q13 (isosceles pair, angle $BAD = 42^\circ$) needs $x - 42 = 180 - 2x$. GCE 2022 P1 Q13 and Q6 said "Form an equation" / "Form and solve two simultaneous equations".

4. **Routine items carry one twist.** GCE 2024 P1 Q20, "$\sin(2x^\circ) = 0.561$ Find two possible values of $x$ in the range $0 \le x \le 90$" is the only multiple-angle equation in the corpus (GCE 2022 P1 Q19: $5\sin x = 2$). GCE 2025 P1 Q14 reverses a three-stage percentage chain; GCE 2021 P1 Q8 was one increase. GCE 2025 P1 Q26, "solve the inequality $4x^3 - 12x^2 + 3x + 5 < 0$" from a drawn graph; GCE 2021 P1 Q23(b) asked "Find the equation of the straight line [2]" first, then an equation.

5. **Reasoning moved from charts to structure.** GCE 2024 P1 Q23(a), "Show that $y$ is an odd number. [4]"; GCE 2025 P1 Q12, "Explain why $a$, $b$ and $c$ cannot all be integers."; GCE 2024 P1 Q13(b), "Explain why $DC$ is not a tangent"; GCE 2024 P1 Q4(b), "Is he correct? Give a reason" (box plot). The *count* of explain-type P1 questions did not change (2–4 every year); 2019–2022 asked about misleading charts (GCE 2019 P1 Q2, GCE 2020 P1 Q4). Structural proofs return from GCE 2018 P1 Q5; they are not an invention.

6. **Radians inside triangle trigonometry.** GCE 2025 P2 Q6(a)(ii), "Calculate the length $CM$. [5]" with angles 1.5, 1.1 and 0.8 radians — sine rule, cosine rule, then $3:5$. Before 2025 radians appeared only in arc/sector work (GCE 2020 P2 Q6(b), GCE 2024 P2 Q8(a)(ii)); GCE 2019 P2 Q2 gave $CX$ [3], an angle [2], an area [3] in degrees, one rule each.

7. **Exponential $y = ka^x$ from two points, exact.** GCE 2025 P2 Q5(c), "passes through the points $(0, \frac14)$ and $(-2, 9)$ … Find the exact $y$-coordinate of $P$. [3]" ($a = \frac16$, $y = \frac1{864}$); GCE 2024 P2 Q4(b) the same with $(0,5)$, $(3,135)$. No 2019–2022 paper has it; GCE 2022 P1 Q25 gave $N = m \times 2^{3t}$ and asked for $m$.

8. **Graph reading at a point you must first locate.** GCE 2025 P2 Q5(b): "At time $t = T$, the value … is 1.5 times its initial value. By drawing a tangent, work out an estimate for the gradient" [4]. GCE 2024 P2 Q4(a)(iii): "explain why the equation … has only one solution. [1]". GCE 2021 P1 Q23(a) named the point, "at $(2, 1)$".

9. **A discrete decision mid-chain.** GCE 2025 P2 Q6(b), "made into as many solid cubes as possible … The remaining metal is used to make a sphere" [6] — $\tan 70^\circ$, volume, integer division, remainder, sphere. GCE 2021 P2 Q4(b) [6] was as long but purely algebraic (surface area → $x$ → $H$).

**Did not change:** the P2 Q1 algebra medley (GCE 2025 P2 Q1 ≈ GCE 2022 P2 Q1); the matrices question (GCE 2025 P2 Q3(d) ≈ GCE 2021 P1 Q22); the number of 5+-mark single parts in P2 (three per paper in 2021, 2022, 2024, 2025); the last question's shape (warm-ups then one 6–8-mark "justify": GCE 2019 P2 Q10 [7], GCE 2022 P2 Q10 [8], GCE 2024 P2 Q9 [7], GCE 2025 P2 Q9 [6]); topic mixing per P2 question (2.4–2.7 tags throughout). P2 went from 10 to 9 questions in 2023.

## 2. Paper 1 at the 2024/25 standard

- **2 marks** — one routine with a re-ordering or hidden operation. GCE 2024 P1 Q7(b): factorise $3 + 2m^2xy - 2my - 3mx$ — the terms as printed do not group; re-order first. GCE 2024 P1 Q3(b): HP total → minus cash price → ÷ cash price; trap: the wrong base.
- **3 marks** — one decision, then a two-step routine. GCE 2025 P1 Q21(b): cone 65% full, depth 78 cm — decide which cone 0.65 belongs to, cube-root, scale. Trap: 0.65 as a length ratio. GCE 2025 P1 Q27(b): two unknowns from two products, then a third product.
- **4–5 marks** — form the model yourself, no parts. GCE 2025 P1 Q22: two speed-time areas in $v$, one equation. GCE 2024 P1 Q23(a) [4] is the proof version: $(3x+4)^2 - (3x-1)^2 = 15(2x+1)$, so $y^2$ is odd. GCE 2024 P1 Q26(b) [4]: total volume → half → which cone → cube root → depth.
- **6–7 marks** — a proof yielding a fact, then an unscaffolded equation using it, then a 1-mark consequence. GCE 2025 P1 Q20: congruence [3], then $AP = 7$, $PB = 24$ from a quadratic [3]. GCE 2024 P1 Q27: show $\overrightarrow{OC}$ [2], two-parameter collinearity for $OQ : QB$ [4], area ratio [1].

## 3. Paper 2 at the 2024/25 standard

- **Q1 algebra medley** — GCE 2025 P2 Q1 [2, 2, 3, 3], unchanged in kind. The A1 in (d) is the cancellation to $\frac{1}{x+4}$; B3 stops at the unsimplified numerator. GCE 2024 P2 Q1(d) [4] separates on the $(4-x)$ sign.
- **Q2/Q3 number + matrices** — GCE 2025 P2 Q3. Separator (c) [3]: a two-ratio chain then "$\frac38$ of Team C move into Team A. There are now 87 students".

- **Trigonometry** — GCE 2024 P2 Q5: show $124.1^\circ$ [3]; $AD$ [3] where the A1 is angle $DAC$ from "bearing of $048^\circ$" and "$D$ is due west"; elevation from a second point [3]. GCE 2025 P2 Q6(a) does it in radians.

- **Circle / mensuration** — GCE 2024 P2 Q8: RHS congruence via "centre to midpoint of chord is perpendicular" [3]; (b) [5], two intersecting circles, radii 7 and 4.5 — the candidate chooses the decomposition (sector − triangle, twice) and finds the angle at $Q$ by sine rule first.
- **Quadratic / speed** — GCE 2024 P2 Q6 [5, 3, 1, 2]: form $\frac{40}{x} + \frac{20}{x-30} = \frac56$ (50 min), clear denominators, solve to 2 dp, reject, then "minutes and seconds, correct to the nearest second". Same shape as GCE 2020 P2 Q9.
- **Final real-world problem** — GCE 2025 P2 Q9(c) [6]: two claims to test ("less than 12 hours", "less than \$165"), battery state over four legs from a distance table, charging time from a rate table, cost per km, then (d) "Explain what effect this will have" when the rate is not constant. GCE 2024 P2 Q9(c) [7]: similar cylinders (×3.375) → mass → wax/oil split (÷1.08) → pack choice → a price, "Justify the decisions you make". The A1s are the explicit decisions and stated assumptions, not the arithmetic.

## 4. The setter's checklist

A new question is AT the 2024/25 standard only if every answer is yes:

1. Does at least one step have to be **chosen** by the candidate (which rule, which cone, which base) rather than named in the stem?
2. For a P1 question worth 3+ marks: is it one question, with parts only where a part is independently worth its marks?
3. Is every equation the question needs **formed by the candidate**, with no "form an equation" / "write an expression" cue?
4. Does each routine item carry one twist ($2x$ inside sin, a $(4-x)$ denominator, a reversed chain, an inequality in place of an equation)?
5. Is every "show that" placed where it gates a later part, and worth its marks (a 5-mark show-that has four steps, as GCE 2024 P2 Q6(a))?
6. Does every 5+-mark part chain at least three techniques with no intermediate answer supplied?
7. Does the context **change the maths** (a bearing fixes an angle; "as many cubes as possible" forces an integer step) rather than decorate it?
8. Is each "explain / is he correct / agree or disagree" answerable only from one specific fact (quartiles, parity, one intersection, grouped data), never by a generic sentence?
9. Are the numbers chosen so the wrong decision still gives a tidy-looking answer (0.65 as a length ratio; dividing by the HP total)?
10. Does the last P2 question require two explicit decisions with assumptions stated, for 6–8 marks, after 1–3 short warm-ups?
11. Can a strong candidate finish at about 1.5 minutes per mark with no part needing more than one non-obvious idea? Two ideas in one part is above standard.
12. Anti-patterns — reject if any hold: every part is a single routine; the context is decorative; the hard step is scaffolded by a show-that; a later part merely re-uses the previous answer; a "reason" mark is satisfiable by "because it is bigger"; the P1 paper has fewer than 12 single-answer questions.

## 5. The moderator's test

Below standard: the marks can be earned by executing named procedures in the order the stem lists them, with no step where the candidate decides which method, case or quantity applies. At standard: for the marks given, exactly one idea must be supplied by the candidate (the model, the case, the rearrangement, the reason) and the remaining marks are the routine that follows, finishable at about 1.5 minutes per mark. Above standard: two independent ideas are needed inside one part, an unfamiliar result must be discovered rather than applied, or the arithmetic outlasts the time the marks buy.
