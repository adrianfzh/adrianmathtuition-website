# The 2024/2025 E Math standard — what setters must hit

Evidence: every question of GCE 2024 P1/P2 and GCE 2025 P1/P2 (72 questions) read against the whole of GCE 2019–2022 (P1 + P2) and GCE 2023. Counts come from the corpus (`ref`, `[n]` marks, `topics`).

## 1. What changed

1. **Paper 1 withholds the ladder.** P1 questions with no (a)/(b) parts: 8 of 25 (2019), 10 of 24 (2021), 12 of 25 (2022), 13 of 27 (2024), 14 of 27 (2025); answer spaces per question (one per printed mark bracket, so a question with no parts counts as one) fell from 1.92 to 1.2. GCE 2025 P1 Q22: "After 100 seconds $B$ has travelled 75 m further than $A$ … Find the value of $v$." — no parts. The same two-cyclist scenario in GCE 2021 P1 Q24 was cut into [1], [1], [4] with the expression for B's distance asked for first.

2. **Paper-2-grade items now sit in Paper 1.** GCE 2025 P1 Q23, "Solve the equation $\frac{2x}{x + 4} + \frac{5}{3x - 5} = 3$ … correct to 2 decimal places" was a 5-mark P2 part in GCE 2021 P2 Q1(c). GCE 2025 P1 Q27(b) (two spinners, two unknown probabilities, [3]) is GCE 2021 P2 Q9(b) [4]. GCE 2025 P1 Q21(b) (cone 65% full, depth 78 cm, find $h$ [3]) is GCE 2020 P2 Q5(b) without its "Explain why he is wrong [1]" lead-in.

3. **The equation is hidden; nobody says "form an equation".** GCE 2025 P1 Q20(b): "The perimeter of the larger square is 124 cm. The perimeter of the smaller square is 100 cm. Find the length of $AP$" [3] — a quadratic, $AP^2 + (31-AP)^2 = 625$, inside a congruence question. GCE 2025 P1 Q13 (isosceles pair, angle $BAD = 42^\circ$) needs $x - 42 = 180 - 2x$. GCE 2022 P1 Q13 and Q6 said "Form an equation" / "Form and solve two simultaneous equations".

4. **Routine items carry one twist.** GCE 2024 P1 Q20, "$\sin(2x^\circ) = 0.561$ Find two possible values of $x$ in the range $0 \le x \le 90$" is the only multiple-angle equation in the corpus (GCE 2022 P1 Q19: $5\sin x = 2$). GCE 2025 P1 Q14 reverses a three-stage percentage chain; GCE 2021 P1 Q8 was one increase. GCE 2025 P1 Q26, "solve the inequality $4x^3 - 12x^2 + 3x + 5 < 0$" from a drawn graph; GCE 2021 P1 Q23(b) asked "Find the equation of the straight line [2]" first, then an equation.

5. **Reasoning moved from charts to structure.** GCE 2024 P1 Q23(a), "Show that $y$ is an odd number. [4]"; GCE 2025 P1 Q12, "Explain why $a$, $b$ and $c$ cannot all be integers."; GCE 2024 P1 Q13(b), "Explain why $DC$ is not a tangent"; GCE 2024 P1 Q4(b), "Is he correct? Give a reason" (box plot). The *count* of explain-type P1 questions did not change (2–4 every year); 2019–2022 asked about misleading charts (GCE 2019 P1 Q2, GCE 2020 P1 Q4). Structural proofs return from GCE 2018 P1 Q5; they are not an invention.

6. **Radians inside triangle trigonometry.** GCE 2025 P2 Q6(a)(ii), "Calculate the length $CM$. [5]" with angles 1.5, 1.1 and 0.8 radians — sine rule, cosine rule, then $3:5$. Before 2025 radians appeared only in arc/sector work (GCE 2020 P2 Q6(b), GCE 2024 P2 Q8(a)(ii)); GCE 2019 P2 Q2 gave $CX$ [3], an angle [2], an area [3] in degrees, one rule each.

7. **Exponential $y = ka^x$ from two points, exact.** GCE 2025 P2 Q5(c), "passes through the points $(0, \frac14)$ and $(-2, 9)$ … Find the exact $y$-coordinate of $P$. [3]" ($a = \frac16$, $y = \frac1{864}$); GCE 2024 P2 Q4(b) the same with $(0,5)$, $(3,135)$. No 2019–2022 paper has it; GCE 2022 P1 Q25 gave $N = m \times 2^{3t}$ and asked for $m$.

8. **Graph reading at a point you must first locate.** GCE 2025 P2 Q5(b): "At time $t = T$, the value … is 1.5 times its initial value. By drawing a tangent, work out an estimate for the gradient" [4]. GCE 2024 P2 Q4(a)(iii): "explain why the equation … has only one solution. [1]". GCE 2021 P1 Q23(a) named the point, "at $(2, 1)$".

9. **A discrete decision mid-chain.** GCE 2025 P2 Q6(b), "made into as many solid cubes as possible … The remaining metal is used to make a sphere" [6] — $\tan 70^\circ$, volume, integer division, remainder, sphere. GCE 2021 P2 Q4(b) [6] was as long but purely algebraic (surface area → $x$ → $H$).

**Did not change:** the P2 Q1 algebra medley (GCE 2025 P2 Q1 ≈ GCE 2022 P2 Q1); the matrices question (GCE 2025 P2 Q3(d) ≈ GCE 2021 P1 Q22); the number of 5+-mark single parts in P2 (three per paper in 2021, 2022, 2024, 2025); the last question's shape (warm-ups then one 6–8-mark "justify": GCE 2019 P2 Q10 [7], GCE 2022 P2 Q10 [8], GCE 2024 P2 Q9 [7], GCE 2025 P2 Q9 [6]); topic mixing per P2 question (2.4–2.7 tags throughout). P2 went from 10 to 9 questions in 2023.

## 2. Paper 1 at the 2024/25 standard

- **2 marks** — one routine with a re-ordering or hidden operation. GCE 2024 P1 Q7(b): factorise $3 + 2m^2xy - 2my - 3mx$ — the terms as printed do not group; re-order first. GCE 2024 P1 Q3(b): HP total → minus cash price → ÷ cash price; trap: the wrong base.
- **3 marks** — one decision, then a two-step routine. GCE 2025 P1 Q21(b): cone 65% full, depth 78 cm — decide which cone 0.65 belongs to, cube-root, scale. Trap: 0.65 as a length ratio. GCE 2025 P1 Q27(b): two unknowns from two products, then a third product.
- **4–5 marks** — form the model yourself, no parts. GCE 2025 P1 Q22: two speed-time areas in $v$, one equation. GCE 2024 P1 Q23(a) [4] is the proof version: $(3x+4)^2 - (3x-1)^2 = 15(2x+1)$, so $y^2$ is odd. GCE 2024 P1 Q26(b) [4]: total volume → half → which cone → cube root → depth.
- **6–7 marks** — two shapes, either at standard. (i) A proof yielding a fact, then an unscaffolded equation using it, then a 1-mark consequence. GCE 2025 P1 Q20: congruence [3], then $AP = 7$, $PB = 24$ from a quadratic [3]. GCE 2024 P1 Q27: show $\overrightarrow{OC}$ [2], two-parameter collinearity for $OQ : QB$ [4], area ratio [1]. (ii) An unscaffolded model worth 3–4 marks, then one or two short parts that use its result and each need a small idea of their own. Set 1 P1 Q23: a hidden quadratic from an area [3], a side [2], the shortest distance from the area [1]. Set 1 P1 Q27 [4, 2].

## 4. The setter's checklist

A new question is AT the 2024/25 standard only if the answer is yes to every item that applies to it. Items 1, 3, 7, 8, 11 and 12 apply to every question; items 2, 5, 6 and 10 apply to the questions they name; item 4 applies as worded; item 9 must hold for at least a third of the questions in each paper (Set 1: about 14 of 36); item 13 is about the whole paper and is checked once, by the session, at the read-through — never by a slot's moderator.

1. Does the candidate have to **choose** a step (which rule, which cone, which base) rather than find it named in the stem — and do the parts where this happens carry at least half of the question's marks?
2. For a P1 question worth 3+ marks: is it one question, with parts only where each part asks for a different final quantity worth its own marks? A part may use an earlier answer. What is not allowed is a rung: a 1-mark part that names the expression, equation or intermediate quantity the next part needs (GCE 2021 P1 Q24's expression for B's distance).
3. Is every equation the question needs **formed by the candidate**, with no "form an equation" / "write an expression" cue?
4. Does each routine question with no parts, and each routine part worth 2+ marks, carry one twist ($2x$ inside sin, a $(4-x)$ denominator, a reversed chain, an inequality in place of an equation, a squared bracket in an expansion)? Exempt: a 1-mark opener, the table, the plot, the standard deviation, and solving an equation the paper has just shown.
5. Is every "show that" placed where it gates a later part, and worth its marks (a 5-mark show-that has four steps, as GCE 2024 P2 Q6(a))?
6. Does every 5+-mark part chain at least three techniques with no intermediate answer supplied?
7. Does the context **change the maths** (a bearing fixes an angle; "as many cubes as possible" forces an integer step) rather than decorate it?
8. Is each "explain / is he correct / agree or disagree" answerable only from one specific fact (quartiles, parity, one intersection, grouped data), never by a generic sentence?
9. Are the numbers chosen so the wrong decision still gives a tidy-looking answer (0.65 as a length ratio; dividing by the HP total)?
10. Does the last P2 question require two explicit decisions, each justified by a calculation (and any assumption the context leaves open stated), for 6–8 marks, after 1–3 short warm-ups?
11. Can a strong candidate finish at about 1.5 minutes per mark, with no part of 4 marks or fewer needing more than one non-obvious idea (one standard case split beside it is allowed)? A part of 5+ marks may need two.
12. Anti-patterns — reject if any hold: every part is a single routine; the context is decorative; a show-that prints a result the candidate can reach without forming anything (a substitution, one expansion), or prints the intermediate value of a chain that a real paper would leave unbroken — a show-that where the candidate must still build the model to reach the printed result is the Paper 2 norm (GCE 2024 P2 Q6(a); Set 1 P2 Q2(a), Q8(a)); a later part worth 3+ marks is nothing but the previous answer put into a rule the stem has named — a 1–2-mark follow-on that uses the previous answer is normal (GCE 2024 P2 Q6(b)–(d)); a "reason" mark is satisfiable by "because it is bigger".
13. **The whole paper — checked once per paper by the session at the read-through.** Two fences: the real 2024/25 paper is the floor, Set 1 is the ceiling. **P1:** 13–18 questions with no parts (real papers 13 and 14; Set 1 has 18); plain routine marks between Set 1's about 6 and the real papers' about 30 of 90 (GCE 2024 P1 Q1, Q2, Q14, Q22, Q25 are whole questions with no idea in them; a Set does not copy that — item 1 applies to every question, so a Set carries its routine marks as the early marks or parts of questions that pass item 1, never as a whole question); about a quarter of the marks top-of-band, never over a third, spread through the paper and not saved for the end; at most one part goes as far as Set 1 P1 Q27(a), which is one rung past GCE 2024 P1 Q27(b); 2–4 explain or show-that questions, as every real P1 has (finding 5) — Set 1 P1 has one explain mark and no show-that, so a later Set carries more reasoning in P1, not less. **P2:** exactly three single parts of 5+ marks; plain routine marks between Set 1's 11–13 and the real papers' about 35 of 90; the hardest part of each question is its last or second-last. **Across the Set:** one congruence or similarity proof, as each real year has (GCE 2025 P1 Q20, GCE 2024 P2 Q8); Set 1 has none. A paper made wholly of lower-edge questions passes items 1–12 and is easier than Set 1; a paper made wholly of top-of-band questions also passes and cannot be finished. This item is what stops both.

## 5. The moderator's test

Apply the test to each part (a question with no parts is one part), then to the question.

**Below standard:** the marks can be earned by executing named procedures in the order the stem lists them; or the parts in which the candidate decides the method, case or quantity carry fewer than half of the question's marks.

**At standard:** a part is at standard when the candidate must supply one non-obvious idea (the model, the case, the rearrangement, the reason) and the rest of its marks are the routine that follows, finishable at about 1.5 minutes per mark. Choosing the standard rule for a configuration already in front of the candidate (cosine rule for three sides, tan in a right-angled triangle, mid-points for grouped data) is a routine decision, not an idea; a part may need one or two of these as well as its idea. A plain routine part with no idea is acceptable at 1–3 marks when it opens a question or is the table, the plot, the standard deviation or solving an equation the paper has just shown (GCE 2024 P2 Q4(a)(i), GCE 2024 P2 Q6(b)). A question is at standard when every part is at standard or is such a routine part, and the parts that need an idea carry at least half its marks.

**Above standard:** a part of 4 marks or fewer needs two non-obvious ideas that do not follow from one another; a part of 5 or more marks needs more than two; an unfamiliar result must be discovered rather than applied; a technique the real papers only set with its scaffold printed (GCE 2024 P1 Q27(b) prints "$AQ = mAP$ and $OQ = nOB$") is set without it more than once in a paper; or the working outlasts the time the marks buy. Two things are not "above": a hidden model paired with one standard case split in a 3–4-mark part (both orders, reject a root — GCE 2025 P1 Q27(b), GCE 2024 P2 Q7(b)(iii)), and the two decisions item 10 requires of the last Paper 2 question.

## 6. What "as good as Set 1" means

Adrian, 17 Sep 2026: "just make sure the standard is as good as set 1 for am and em". E Math Set 1 is the paper he read and approved. These are its qualities, to be matched with different content — never its contexts, numbers or structures.

- **Every question has a decision, and the deciding parts carry at least half of its marks.** Lowest in Set 1: P1 Q12, 2 of 4. Typical: P2 Q7, 7 of 8. No whole question can be done on autopilot.
- **The decision is handed over by leaving a name out, not by adding length.** No stem names a rule, a locus, an equation or a variable; a stem with no parts runs 2–5 lines and every sentence carries a fact. Check: search the stem for "using the … rule", "form an equation", "let … be", "write an expression" — Set 1 has none.
- **The given information is one step away from what the routine wants.** A change instead of a value, a total stated in terms of the unknown, a cumulative table instead of frequencies, a median instead of $x$, two points with neither on the $y$-axis.
- **The context carries a constraint that changes the answer.** Set 1 P2 Q9(c): the 1.3 m side of the base rejects the tank the capacity test prefers. Check: strip the context — if the maths is unchanged, it fails.
- **In about two questions in five, the wrong decision lands on a believable number.** Check: name the tempting wrong answer; at least a third of the questions should have one.
- **Parts work on one object, each asks for a different quantity, and each needs its own small idea; there are no rung parts.** The last part is the hardest, or a 1–2-mark sting that uses an earlier result in a new way.
- **Numbers reward the right route.** Where the idea is the point, the honest route comes out clean. A decimal answer appears only where the rounding is itself asked for.
- **Every explain or justify mark hangs on a fact computed from the question's own data**, never on a generic sentence; every show-that gates the parts after it.
- **Short questions fuse two topics.** 11 of the 27 Set 1 P1 questions carry two topic tags inside 3–4 marks.
