# The 2024/2025 A Math standard — what setters must hit

Evidence: every question of GCE 2024 P1/P2 and GCE 2025 P1/P2 (48 questions) read against every question of GCE 2019–2023 and the 2021 specimen (137 questions). Syllabus 4049 was first examined in 2021 (two papers of 90 marks, 2 h 15 min each); GCE 2019 and 2020 are syllabus 4047 (P1 80 marks, P2 100 marks), so every structural count below compares 2021–2025 only, in the order 2021, specimen, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, and any 2019/2020 citation is flagged (4047). Counts come from the corpus (`ref`, `[n]` marks, `topics`). The files print no mark for an unparted question; those marks are derived as 90 minus the printed part marks and are never quoted per question.

## 1. What changed

1. **2025 asks for fewer, longer answers; 2024 does not.** Answer units (leaf parts plus unparted questions) over both papers: 51, 51, 56, 49, 51, 46 — that is 3.53, 3.53, 3.21, 3.67, 3.53, 3.91 marks per answer. Parts worth 6+ marks: 1, 0, 2, 4, 2, 5; parts worth 2 or fewer: 13, 11, 21, 12, 16, 10. The only 7-mark parts in 4049 are GCE 2023 P1 Q13(b), GCE 2023 P2 Q5(a), GCE 2025 P1 Q10(a), GCE 2025 P1 Q13(a) and GCE 2025 P2 Q9(a). GCE 2025 P1 Q13, "(a) Find the equation of the circle. [7]", is GCE 2021 specimen P2 Q8 with the rungs removed: that paper asked "(a) Find the equation of the perpendicular bisector of $PQ$. [5]" and then "(b) Hence find the equation of the circle. [5]". By these counts 2024 is built like 2021 and 2023 already leans towards 2025; the extra difficulty of 2024 sits inside its questions (items 2–9), not in its structure.

2. **Unparted questions now hide a model.** Their number did not rise — 8, 3, 1, 7, 8, 7, carrying 42, 17, 7, 35, 41, 44 marks — but their kind did. In the 2021–2023 papers 14 of 16 are an instruction on a printed expression, equation or curve, with no diagram and no situation (GCE 2021 P1 Q5: "Express $\dfrac{13x-6}{x^2(2x-3)}$ in partial fractions."); the exceptions are GCE 2021 P1 Q1 and GCE 2021 P2 Q10. In 2024/25, 7 of 15 sit in a diagram or a worded situation that the candidate must turn into an equation: GCE 2024 P1 Q1, Q3, Q6, Q7, GCE 2024 P2 Q1, GCE 2025 P1 Q1, GCE 2025 P2 Q1. GCE 2025 P1 Q1 prints no formula at all: "the price is automatically reduced by 5% … How many complete months have elapsed before the item is sold?" GCE 2024 P2 Q1 ends "Find the value of $t$ when a new machine is purchased." with no parts; the same $\mathrm{e}^{-kt}$ model in GCE 2022 P2 Q1 was cut into "(a) Estimate the number of birds in the year 2017. [4]" and a [2], as was GCE 2021 specimen P1 Q5. Surds: GCE 2021 P1 Q1, "Find, without using a calculator, the width of the rectangle", is one division; GCE 2024 P1 Q7, "express the total surface area of the cuboid in the form $(a + b\sqrt{2})$ cm$^2$", needs the height by division and then $2s^2 + 4sh$.

3. **In 2025 *prove* leaves its two homes and loses its hints.** Answer units worded *prove*: 3, 1, 3, 3, 1, 7 — and before 2025 every one is a trigonometric identity or value, or a circle-geometry part. 2025 adds GCE 2025 P1 Q3(b), "Use part (a)(i) to prove that $\frac{(x+n)^3}{2} - \frac{(x-n)^3}{2}$ is divisible by $n$. [3]" (the only divisibility argument in the corpus), GCE 2025 P2 Q5, "prove that $y$ increases for all real values of $x$.", and GCE 2025 P1 Q12(a)(ii). Plane geometry carries 10 marks in GCE 2025 P2 Q10 [3, 3, 4] against 7, 8, 6, 9 in GCE 2021 P1 Q11, GCE 2021 specimen P1 Q11, GCE 2022 P1 Q7, GCE 2023 P2 Q2, and "(ii) If $DC$ is parallel to $AB$ prove that $AD = BC$. [4]" names no triangles. GCE 2024 P1 Q6, "Show, giving all reasons, that angle $ATB = 2 \times$ angle $AXB$.", is the configuration of GCE 2022 P1 Q7 (a diameter, two tangents that meet, the line through the far end of the diameter), which supplied "angle $RPS = x$" and split the work into "(i) angle $RST = 2x$, [3]" and "(ii) triangle $RST$ is isosceles. [3]"; 2024 offers no angle letter and no parts. The total reasoning load did **not** rise: units with a printed target (show that / prove / verify) number 12, 9, 9, 12, 9, 11 and explain-type units 5, 3, 6, 2, 6, 1. 2024 reasons by *explain* (six units, 15 marks); 2025 reasons by *prove* (seven units, 20 printed marks plus one unparted question).

4. **A general constant is carried to the final answer (2025, continuing 2023).** Questions asking for an answer *in terms of* a constant: GCE 2021 P2 Q9 alone in 2021; none in the specimen, 2022 or 2024; three in 2023 (GCE 2023 P1 Q13, GCE 2023 P2 Q5, GCE 2023 P2 Q8); three in 2025 (GCE 2025 P1 Q10, GCE 2025 P1 Q12, GCE 2025 P2 Q9) plus the general $n$ of GCE 2025 P1 Q4. GCE 2025 P1 Q4(a), "Differentiate $x^{n+1}\ln x$ with respect to $x$, where $n \geq 0$. [2]", is GCE 2019 P2 Q1 (4047), "(i) Differentiate $x^2 \ln x$ with respect to $x$. [2]", for the same marks. GCE 2025 P1 Q10(a): "Find, in terms of the positive constant $a$, the equation of the normal to the curve $y = 2\sin ax - 3\cos 2x$ at the point $P$ where $x = 0$. [7]". GCE 2025 P2 Q9(a): "By finding $k$ in terms of $\alpha$, show that $R_1 = \frac{\alpha^3}{6}$. [7]" — the area closers GCE 2021 P1 Q14 [5, 5] and GCE 2022 P2 Q10 [6, 6] were numerical throughout.

Trigonometric graphs: GCE 2025 P1 Q12(a)(ii), "Triangle $ABC$ is equilateral. Prove that $a = \frac{\pi\sqrt{3}}{2k}$. [3]", against GCE 2021 P1 Q7, "(a) Explain why $c=-3$. [2]", and GCE 2022 P1 Q10 (amplitude, period, sketch: [1, 2, 3]). GCE 2021 P1 Q13 printed "[The volume of a sphere of radius $r$ is $\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$.]" and "$PV=k$"; GCE 2023 P1 Q11 printed $\frac{dr}{dt}$ and began "(a) Show that $k = 0.5$. [1]". 7. **Routine items carry one twist.** GCE 2024 P1 Q9(a-ii): the range of $k$ for which the equation "does not have two distinct roots" — $k \le \frac52$, equality included. GCE 2025 P1 Q7(b), "find the non-zero value of $a$ for which the coefficient of $x$ is twice the coefficient of $x^2$. [3]": the factor $\frac{5}{ax}$ shifts every power, so the $x^2$ coefficient needs the fourth term of (a); GCE 2022 P1 Q5(b) was "Given that there is no term in $x^2$" with the factor $(2 + 3x)$, for [2]. GCE 2025 P1 Q2, "is completely below the line $y = x + 1$", joins GCE 2023 P1 Q7 ("lies completely below the $x$-axis", which already needed $k < 0$) to a line — a modest step, since GCE 2019 P1 Q2 (4047) had "lies entirely above the line $y = x$". 8. **Linear law without the plot, or with a faulty datum.** GCE 2025 P1 Q5 has no table and no grid: "determine, with working, the function of $x$ which must be plotted on the $X$-axis. [2]", then $A$ and $b$ from two points on the line [4]. GCE 2024 P2 Q10(a): "It is believed that an error was made in recording one of the values of $R$." and "(ii) From your straight line graph, identify the incorrect reading and suggest a corrected value for $R$. [2]" — unique in the corpus. Not new: choosing the transformation unaided (GCE 2021 P2 Q7(a), "Explain how a straight line graph can be drawn to represent the formula", [4]). GCE 2022 P1 Q2(a) named the axes: "Plot $\ln m$ against $t$ and draw a straight line graph to illustrate the information. [2]".

9. **Techniques are chained, and the target form is no longer printed.** GCE 2024 P1 Q12(b), "Use your answer to part (a) to show that $\int_2^3 \frac{3x - 5}{x^2(x - 1)}\,dx = \frac{5}{6} - \ln\frac{16}{9}$. GCE 2025 P2 Q8(a), "Solve the equation $2^{p} = 5\left(3^{2 - p}\right)$. [4]", is GCE 2021 P1 Q12(a), "Show that the solution of the equation $6^x=5\times 3^{x+1}$ is $x=\dfrac{\lg 15}{\lg 2}$. ## 4. The setter's checklist

The checklist has two lists.

**List A — every question; the slot author and the slot moderator check these:** items 1, 2, 3a, 4, 5, 8, 10, 11 (per part) and the question-level anti-patterns of 12. An item that starts "If …" is skipped when the condition does not hold. A question offered as AT standard needs yes to every List A item that applies to it; a question offered as a ROUTINE slot (§5) needs only 3a, 5, 10, 11 and the anti-patterns.

**List B — the whole paper; only the session checks these, when it plans the paper and again at the read-through, and a miss is repaired at assembly (unpart a slot, merge two parts, move marks) or by re-briefing one slot, never by rejecting a slot that passed List A:** items 3b, 6, 7, 9, 11 (the paper's total time), 13, the routine allowance of §5 and the paper-level anti-patterns of 12.

1. Does at least one step have to be **chosen** by the candidate (which identity, which two binomial terms, which line to draw, which point the diameter passes through) rather than named in the stem? "Which identity" counts as a choice only when the first identity a candidate would try does not finish the job, or a second topic has to be brought in.
2. If the question sits in a figure or a context, does the candidate **build the relation** ($300(0.95)^n$, $AB = \sqrt{9 + x^2}$, $\tan\alpha = \frac2x$), with a formula printed only where the syllabus could not expect it to be produced? A relation printed as a *show that* target which the candidate must derive from the figure counts as built, provided the fact that produces it (the half-diagonal, the radius to the point of contact, the perpendicular that halves the isosceles triangle) is not stated in the stem (GCE 2024 P1 Q8(a), GCE 2024 P1 Q11(a)).
3. **3a (question).** Is the question split into parts only where each part is independently worth its marks — and if it is a model, a single equation or a single proof worth 4–6 marks, is it left whole? **3b (paper).** Does the paper leave two to five questions unparted, averaging 5–6 marks each, at least one of them a model in a figure or a worded situation? (GCE 2024 P1 five, GCE 2025 P1 two, GCE 2024 P2 three, GCE 2025 P2 five. Set 1 has none in P1 and one bare equation in P2, so a later Set has more, never fewer than two a paper.)
4. In a question offered as AT standard, does at least one routine item carry a twist (a non-strict boundary, a $\frac{5}{ax}$ multiplier that shifts which terms matter, a general $n$, a sign condition on $k$, halved values, a repeated factor, an end point that counts)? Routine parts beside it need none (GCE 2024 P1 Q10(a), GCE 2025 P1 Q3(a)(i)). A whole question with none is a ROUTINE slot — counted against the allowance, not rejected.
5. Is every *show that* or *prove* one of: (i) a gate for a later part; (ii) a proof whose last mark is a stated reason or conclusion; (iii) a trigonometric identity, where the marks are for the chain of equal expressions? In the plane-geometry question, is at least one part unguided — no angle letter supplied, the angles or triangles to use left to the candidate? Naming the triangles in one other part is normal (GCE 2025 P2 Q10(a)).
6. **(paper)** Does the paper hold one to three answer units of 6–7 marks with no intermediate answer printed, at least one of them a chain of three techniques? (GCE 2024: one [6] in each paper. GCE 2025: two [7]s in P1; a [7] and two [6]s in P2. Set 1 has no part above 5 marks, so a later Set has at least one such unit in each paper.)
7. **(paper; wanted, not required)** Does at least one question carry a general constant ($a$, $k$, $n$, $\alpha$) through the calculus or trigonometry to an UNPRINTED answer in terms of it? GCE 2025 has two per paper and GCE 2024 none, so a paper without one is not rejected for that alone; a Set of two papers has at least one between them.
8. If the question has a context, does the context **change the maths** (whole months force an integer, positive speeds force an acute angle, a rate that cannot exceed a value makes an inequality) rather than decorate it? A question with no context skips this item.
9. **(paper)** Across the paper, do at least three solving steps force a rejection, a kept second case or a range check that costs the A1 if missed ($x = -2$ under a logarithm, $\theta = 94.5^\circ$, the branch $k > \frac12$, an end point that counts)? A question with no solving step is not failed for lacking one.
10. If exact working is the PURPOSE of the question (the calculator-ban question; a surd form asked for in the stem), is the form demanded in a setting (a cuboid, a trapezium, a line meeting a curve, a definite integral) that needs two or three exact steps rather than one division? An exact form asked for at the end of another question's part ($\sqrt5$, $32\sqrt3$, $3\ln3-2$, a multiple of $\pi$) needs no such setting (GCE 2024 P2 Q6(b), GCE 2024 P2 Q5(b)).
11. Can a strong candidate finish at about 1.5 minutes per mark (90 marks in 135 minutes), with a 7-mark part costing about ten minutes and no part needing more than one non-obvious idea? Two ideas in one part is above standard.
12. Anti-patterns. **Question level — reject the slot if any hold:** a question OFFERED AS AT STANDARD in which every part is a single named routine (that question is a ROUTINE slot, acceptable only inside the paper's allowance, §5); a model question is cut into [4] + [2] where 2024/25 asks it whole; a circle question that starts with centre and radius from a printed equation AND whose later parts are all the standard follow-ons (the tangent at a given point, a point inside or outside, a second circle) — starting that way is acceptable when a later part makes the candidate find something not given: the point of contact, the gradient, the second point (for example, both tangents from an external point); a context that prints the formula and asks only for substitution; a *show that* whose printed line is the WHOLE of the next part's thinking, leaving that part three or more marks of pure routine — a printed line that lets a candidate restart after a slip is a gate (item 5) and is wanted, even when it shows a factorised or rearranged form, provided the next part still has a decision of its own (in Set 1 P1 Q5, part (c) still needs the quadratic in $\tan^2\theta$, the rejected $-41$ and $\theta = 0$ kept); a proof markable without reasons; an R-formula, linear-law or trigonometric-graph question that follows the 2021 template step for step with no twist. **Paper level — the session repairs these at assembly:** a paper with fewer than two unparted questions, or with more than three 7-mark parts; more than about one answer unit in five with its answer printed as a target (the real papers print 9–12 across BOTH papers of a year; Set 1 prints 22); a paper whose last question is a routine slot.
13. **(paper)** The shape of the whole paper. Two fences: for question quality Set 1 is the floor (§6); for paper shape the real 2024/25 paper is the target and Set 1 is the loosest a later Set may be. Answer units per paper: 20–27 in the real papers, 36 and 32 in Set 1 — a later Set has at most 30. Parts of 2 marks or fewer: no more than Set 1 P1's 17. The marks climb and the last question is the hardest (Set 1 P1 ends on a routine question; do not copy that). Across the two papers of a Set: one linear-law question and one exponential or logarithmic model in context (the real roster has both every year; Set 1 has neither), one proof outside trigonometry and plane geometry where the topic draw allows, and the constant of item 7. A paper made wholly of idea-in-a-2-mark-part questions passes List A and is easier to finish than the real paper; a paper made wholly of 7-mark chains also passes and cannot be finished — List B is what stops both.

## 5. The moderator's test

Below standard: the marks can be earned by executing named procedures in the order the stem lists them — the formula is printed, the form is printed, the ladder is printed — and no step asks the candidate to decide which relation, case, term or line applies — or the question is a bare instruction (Solve, Differentiate, Express in partial fractions, Prove the identity) whose whole solution is the first method taught for that instruction, with nothing to notice on the way, and it is offered as AT standard. Also below: a routine question beyond the paper's allowance, a routine question of a type 2024/25 no longer asks that way (a model cut into [4] + [2], a circle opener with nothing withheld), or one given more marks than 2024/25 gave the same work.

Routine (acceptable, counted): a question of a type §1 lists under "Did not change" or §2/§3 call unchanged or 2021-standard, correct and clean, asked at the marks 2024/25 gave it, with no idea to supply. A routine slot is not rejected. The session counts them: at most three questions and at most 26 of the 90 marks of a paper. That is Set 1's own level (Set 1 P1: Q1, Q13 = 14 marks; Set 1 P2: Q3, Q4, Q6 = 26 marks), inside the real range (GCE 2024 P1: Q2, Q5, Q8, Q11 = 27 marks; GCE 2025 P1: Q6, Q9 = 14 marks) — a later Set is never more routine than Set 1. The moderator scores a clean routine slot 4 and writes "routine" as its standard.

At standard — rate the QUESTION, not each part: at least one part needs an idea supplied by the candidate (the model; the unfamiliar piece of given information; the rearrangement; a link between two topics where the output of one technique is the input of the next and the intermediate form is not printed; a boundary, a count or a case kept or rejected for a reason that comes from the question; the reason that closes a proof); no single part needs more than one; the other parts are the syllabus routine that leads to the idea or follows from it, and may have nothing to decide; a general constant or an exact form may ride along; and every part is finishable at about 1.5 minutes per mark. A question of 9 marks or more may hold two such parts. A negative length or time, or a logarithm of a negative number, discarded at the end of an otherwise routine solution is NOT the idea; it counts only towards item 9.

Above standard: two independent non-obvious ideas are needed inside one part, a result outside the 4049 syllabus must be discovered rather than applied, a 7-mark chain has no recognisable first step, or the algebra outlasts the time the marks buy — 2025 itself never stacks more than three 7-mark parts across two papers, and 2024 has none.

Marks match the work: a standard routine is paid, within one mark, what 2024/25 paid it — repeated-factor partial fractions [5] (GCE 2024 P1 Q12(a)); a definite integral of three simple terms to a stated exact form [4–5]; differentiate, then "Hence" integrate [4–5 for the pair] (GCE 2024 P1 Q2, GCE 2025 P1 Q4); two conditions for two polynomial constants [4–6] (GCE 2024 P2 Q6(a), GCE 2025 P2 Q4(a)); similar triangles with reasons [3] (GCE 2025 P2 Q10(a)). More marks than that for the same work is an easier paper that no other clause detects. Fewer fails the time clause — and in both cases the repair is to MOVE MARKS, not to rewrite the question; the moderator reports "re-mark" in its fixes, not "above" or "below".

## 6. What "as good as Set 1" means

> **Adrian, 17 Sep 2026 (binding):** "just make sure the standard is as good as set 1 for am and em". He had already said of it: "a math set 1 paper was good".

A Math Set 1 is the paper Adrian read and approved. A later Set matches its QUALITIES with different content (the variety rule still holds: never its situations, structures or skills). Each quality can be checked on a draft by counting.

1. **The thinking sits in a small part, first or last, and the middle is honest routine.** Each long question has one or two 2–4 mark parts that need an idea and the rest follow from it. Set 1 P2 Q10: (a)(i) [3] is the whole idea (radius to the point of contact, similar triangles); (b)–(d) are clean routine. Check: in every question of 7+ marks, point to the part that needs the idea; if you cannot, it is a routine slot.
2. **The relation is derived from a fact the stem does not state.** Where a context question prints its relation, it prints it as a target, and the fact that produces it is left to the candidate. Set 1 P1 Q9(a): nothing says $OA$ is half the diagonal of the square. Check: underline the geometric fact the derivation turns on; it must not be in the stem.
3. **The last part turns the question round and finds a fact about the configuration** — perpendicular, mid-point, a square, exactly twice, the least value, back at the start (Set 1 P1 Q8(c), P1 Q13(b), P2 Q8(c), P2 Q9(c)(ii), P2 Q10(d)). Check: at least half the questions of 7+ marks end on a structural fact, not a number for its own sake.
4. **Every part feeds the next; nothing can be deleted.** Set 1 P1 Q12: $k$, then the factors, then partial fractions, then the integral, with only $k = 8$ printed. Check: delete any part — a later part must break. (Set 1 P1 Q3 is the one place Set 1 fails this; do not copy it.)
5. **Two topics meet inside one question through a natural bridge, the second working on the OUTPUT of the first.** Set 1 P1 Q5 (a binomial difference becomes an equation in $\tan\theta$), P1 Q4 (distance, quadratic inequality, least value), P2 Q2 (remainder theorem, then discriminant), P2 Q5 (product rule, discriminant, reverse integration). Check: at least four questions a paper carry two syllabus topics joined this way, not placed side by side as (a) and (b).
6. **The A1 hangs on a boundary, a count or a case — and sometimes the right move is to KEEP the case.** Set 1 P1 Q2(b): $1 \le k < 3$, one end in and one out. P1 Q5(c): $\theta = 0$ kept, $\tan^2\theta = -41$ rejected. P2 Q1: both roots stand. Check: at least four such A1s a paper, and not all of them rejections.
7. **The context carries information the solution needs.** Set 1 P2 Q7: "40 revolutions per minute" is $k$, "first revolution" is the interval, "diametrically opposite" is the second model. P2 Q10: $h > 6$ because the ball must fit. Check: strip the story — if the question is still fully answerable, the context is decoration.
8. **Numbers are engineered to close exactly without being small or obvious.** Set 1 P2 Q9: gradients $\frac34$ and $-\frac43$, contacts $(10, 3)$ and $(3, 4)$, every side 5. P1 Q9: $32\sqrt3$. P2 Q10: $h = 12$, $72\pi = 2 \times 36\pi$. Check: every final answer is an integer, a simple fraction, a simple surd, a logarithm of a simple fraction or a multiple of $\pi$, unless the context needs a decimal.
9. **Reasons are asked for in words, in small doses, all through the paper** — "determine its nature", "state whether the area is increasing or decreasing", "show that … has only one real root", and a three-part geometry proof with no angle letters. Check: at least three units a paper whose last mark is a sentence, besides the geometry proof.
10. **Stems are short, the figure can be drawn from the words alone, and the marks climb.** Both Set 1 papers rise steadily (4 to 10 marks; 5 to 12) and open with three questions of 5 marks or fewer that any prepared candidate can start. Check: a reader can sketch every diagram from the text; question marks never fall by more than one from one question to the next.

**Where a later Set does BETTER than Set 1, not the same** (List B holds the numbers): end each paper on its hardest question; leave two or more questions unparted, at least one of them a model; one answer unit of 6–7 marks in each paper; one constant carried to an unprinted answer; linear law and an exponential or logarithmic model in the Set; the work paid what 2024/25 paid it; printed targets at about one answer unit in five, not one in three.
