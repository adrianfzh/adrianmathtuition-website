
THREE LESSONS FROM THE TUTOR'S READ OF A MATH SET 1 PAPER 2 (Adrian, 9 Oct 2026) — binding for every O-Level slot, author and moderator alike:
1. A "Hence" part must follow from the part before it with NO unstated leap. If the candidate has to choose a value of a constant, substitute a particular value, or pick which earlier result to use, the question SAYS SO ("By choosing a suitable value of k, …", "Using your answer to part (a) with x = 2, …"). A "Hence" that only works through a trick the question did not signal is a defect, not a difficulty.
2. No two questions in a paper — least of all neighbours — may have the same feel or end with the same step. Two questions that both end in a quadratic inequality in a constant, both end with "find the range of values of k", both close on a discriminant condition, both finish by solving a quadratic by the formula, both ask "find the exact value of …" from the same kind of identity: one of them is wrong. The author reads the closing step of every accepted question in paper-so-far.md and closes differently; the moderator names any match as a defect.
3. After every slot is accepted, one reader goes through the WHOLE paper as a candidate would and lists repeated skills, missing hints, uneven marks (a part whose marks are out of line with its demand), and anything a real SEAB paper would not do; those slots are rewritten and re-solved blind. Write so that reader finds nothing: marks in line with demand, every hint a real paper would give, no skill used twice.
